# SERIES 10 — The Sweeper

# EP01 — Is the Libero Really Dead?

## From Beckenbauer to Stones — Has the Libero Really Disappeared?

10 June 2023, Istanbul. Champions League final, Manchester City vs Inter.

When City had the ball, centre-back **John Stones** walked forward.

He took up a position in central midfield, next to Rodri.

When City lost the ball, he dropped back into the defensive line.

When they won it back, he stepped up into midfield again.

Defender or midfielder?

City won 1-0 and completed the treble.

Many people called Stones' role a brand-new innovation.

But fifty years earlier, there was already a defender who played like this.

A defender who moved freely behind his defensive line,

and who, once he had the ball, advanced into midfield — and sometimes all the way to the opponent's box.

**Franz Beckenbauer.**

People had a name for his position:

# LIBERO

Italian for **“the free man.”**

In English: **the Sweeper.**

Today, the position has all but disappeared.

But has it really?

---

# 1. What Is a Libero?

The Libero is **a free defender who stands behind the defensive line.**

```text
THE LIBERO SYSTEM

     FW          FW        ← OPPOSING FORWARDS
     ↕           ↕
  STOPPER     STOPPER      ← MAN-MARKING

         LIBERO            ← NO MARKING, COVERS
           GK
```

In the past, most defending was **man-to-man.**

One defender followed one opposing forward all the way.

But there was a problem:

**What if the marker gets beaten?**

So one defender was left **without a man to mark.**

A player who closed the gap when a teammate was beaten,

and cleared the balls played into the space behind.

**That was the Libero.**

# THE SPARE MAN

---

# 2. The Beginning — The Bolt

The roots of the Libero go back to Switzerland in the 1930s.

Coach Karl Rappan created a system called the **“verrou”** — French for **“the bolt.”**

One defender dropped behind the rest of the defence to cover freely.

Switzerland took this system to the 1938 World Cup.

Then, in the 1960s, the idea was perfected in Italy:

**Helenio Herrera's Inter.**

In Italian, **“catenaccio”** — which also means **“the bolt.”**

Inter's Libero and captain was **Armando Picchi.**

With Picchi guarding the space behind, full-back Facchetti was free to join the attack boldly.

Inter won **back-to-back European Cups in 1964 and 1965.**

In the 1964 final in Vienna, they beat Real Madrid 3-1.

```text
1930s  RAPPAN — THE VERROU (SWITZERLAND)
          ↓
1960s  HERRERA — CATENACCIO (INTER)
          ↓
       LIBERO = THE DEFENCE'S LAST SAFETY NET
```

In this era, the Libero was a strictly **defensive role.**

# THE LAST LINE

# 3. Beckenbauer — Redefining the Libero

In the 1970s, one player completely changed what a Libero meant.

**Franz Beckenbauer.**

For Beckenbauer, the Libero's “freedom” was not only about defending.

**Having nobody to mark also meant being free to step forward with the ball.**

```text
CATENACCIO LIBERO          BECKENBAUER'S LIBERO

  STOPPER  STOPPER            STOPPER  STOPPER
                                   ↑
      LIBERO                    LIBERO ───→ MIDFIELD / ATTACK
   (STAYS BEHIND)          (ADVANCES WITH THE BALL)
```

He received the ball behind his defensive line, dribbled forward, and directed the game from midfield.

And the results are part of football history:

**Bayern Munich — three consecutive European Cups, 1974, 1975 and 1976.**

**West Germany — 1974 World Cup winners, with Beckenbauer as captain.**

**Ballon d'Or — 1972 and 1976.**

Beckenbauer remains **the only defender to have won the Ballon d'Or more than once.**

In 1990, he led West Germany to the World Cup again — this time as coach.

He is one of only three people to have won the World Cup as both a player and a manager.

In January 2024, Beckenbauer died at the age of 78.

# DEFENDER + PLAYMAKER

*(Beckenbauer's story is covered in detail in EP03.)*

# 4. Italy's Liberos — Scirea and Baresi

Italy was the land of the Libero.

### Gaetano Scirea

As Juventus' Libero, he won Serie A seven times.

He was part of the Italy side that won the 1982 World Cup.

And he is remembered as a defender who was **never sent off** in his entire career.

Scirea's defending was never rough. **He read the play, waited, and intervened precisely.**

### Franco Baresi

AC Milan's Libero.

In 1978-79, under coach Nils Liedholm, he won Serie A playing as the Libero.

Then came the 1994 World Cup.

In the group game against Norway, he tore the meniscus in his right knee.

After surgery, Baresi returned for the final about 25 days later.

Against Brazil, he directed Italy's defence for 120 minutes, and Italy held on for a 0-0 draw.

But in the shootout, his penalty flew over the bar.

# READ, WAIT, INTERVENE

---

# 5. Sammer — The Last Great Libero

In the 1990s, one Libero rose to the top once again.

**Matthias Sammer.**

Originally a midfielder, Sammer was moved into the Libero role by Dortmund coach Ottmar Hitzfeld.

And then:

**Euro 1996 — champions with Germany, and player of the tournament.**

**1996 Ballon d'Or — the first defender to win it since Beckenbauer in 1976.**

**1997 Champions League — as Dortmund captain, he beat Juventus 3-1 in the final.**

```text
1972, 1976  BECKENBAUER — BALLON D'OR
      ↓
   20 YEARS
      ↓
1996        SAMMER — BALLON D'OR
```

Like Beckenbauer, Sammer was a Libero who stepped forward with the ball.

And many people call him **the last great Libero.**

# THE LAST OF HIS KIND

---

# 6. Why Did the Libero Disappear? — ① Sacchi's Revolution

Ironically, the Libero's decline began with **a Libero.**

In 1987, Arrigo Sacchi arrived at AC Milan.

He rejected the common sense of Italian football.

**No man-marking. No Libero.**

Instead:

**Pure zonal defending.**

**A flat back four in a single line.**

**A high defensive line and the offside trap.**

**An aggressive press.**

```text
LIBERO SYSTEM                SACCHI'S FLAT BACK FOUR

 ST   ST  (MAN-MARKING)       D ── D ── D ── D  ← ONE LINE
   LIBERO (BEHIND)               (ZONAL)
```

And the man who led that flat back four was **Baresi.**

Liedholm's Libero had become the leader of Sacchi's defensive line.

When Baresi gave the signal, the whole back line stepped up together and caught the opponent offside.

Milan won back-to-back European Cups in 1989 and 1990.

**One of the last great Liberos had perfected a defence without a Libero.**

# NO LIBERO, ONE LINE

*(Sacchi's revolution is covered in detail in EP05.)*

# 7. Why Did It Disappear? — ② The End of Man-Marking

The Libero existed for **man-to-man defending.**

He was the insurance for when a marker was beaten.

But in zonal defending, every defender shares responsibility for the space.

```text
MAN-MARKING:  EACH MARKS ONE MAN → IF BEATEN, THE LIBERO COVERS
ZONAL:        SPACE IS SHARED    → EVERYONE COVERS EVERYONE
```

**If every defender covers every other defender, there is no need to keep one man spare.**

When man-marking disappeared, so did the Libero's reason to exist.

# NO MAN-MARKING, NO SPARE MAN

---

# 8. Why Did It Disappear? — ③ The High Line

The third reason is **the high defensive line.**

The Libero stood **behind** his defensive line.

But that position does not work with an offside trap.

```text
WITH A LIBERO BEHIND THE LINE

D ── D ── D ── D
                    ← OPPOSING FORWARD IS ONSIDE
      LIBERO
```

If one player stays behind, the opposing forwards are kept onside by him.

**To push the whole line up, everyone has to stand on the same line.**

And as we saw in SERIES 07 and 08, the job of protecting the space behind a high line passed to

**quick centre-backs** and **sweeper keepers.**

# THE HIGH LINE HAS NO ROOM FOR A MAN BEHIND IT

---

# 9. Where Did They Go?

The Libero disappeared.

But the Libero's **functions** did not.

Just like the Second Striker in SERIES 09, they were split across several roles.

```text
                            LIBERO
                              │
     ┌──────────────┬─────────┼────────────┬────────────────┐
     ↓              ↓         ↓            ↓                ↓
SWEEPER KEEPER  BALL-PLAYING  DROPPING   CENTRAL CB      OVERLAPPING CB
   (Neuer)          CB         No.6     OF A BACK THREE    (Bastoni)
                 (Stones)   (Busquets)   (Bonucci)
```

**Sweeper Keeper** — protects the space behind the defensive line. Neuer (SERIES 08)

**Ball-Playing CB** — steps forward from the defensive line with the ball. Stones, Van Dijk (SERIES 07)

**Dropping No.6** — drops between the centre-backs to start the build-up. Busquets, Rodri (SERIES 03)

**Central CB of a back three** — covers and distributes from between two stoppers. Bonucci

**Overlapping CB** — the wide centre-back of a back three who pushes into midfield and attack. Bastoni

# THE POSITION DIED. THE FREEDOM SURVIVED.

---

# 10. Stones — The Libero of 2023

Let us return to Istanbul.

In the second half of the 2022-23 season, Guardiola gave Stones a new role.

**Without the ball: a centre-back.**

**With the ball: a midfielder next to Rodri.**

```text
OUT OF POSSESSION          IN POSSESSION

D   STONES   D   D            STONES  RODRI
                            D        D        D
```

Stones said the hardest part of the role at first was **the 360-degree picture** — seeing everything around him.

In the Istanbul final, he was named man of the match in polls including a BBC users' vote.

Beckenbauer started **behind** his defensive line and stepped forward.

Stones started **inside** his defensive line and stepped forward.

The starting point was different. The principle was the same:

> **A defender with nobody to mark is free to step forward with the ball.**

# THE FREE MAN STEPS FORWARD

---

# 11. Bastoni — The Centre-Back Who Attacks

In Inter's 2025-26 title-winning season, there was another heir to the Libero:

**Alessandro Bastoni.**

In Inter's 3-5-2, the left centre-back steps into midfield during the build-up,

and sometimes runs down the flank all the way to the edge of the opponent's box.

He is regarded as **the first creative outlet** of Inter's build-up, averaging around 11 progressive passes per 90 minutes.

A defender as the starting point of the attack.

**That was the essence of the Libero that Beckenbauer showed fifty years ago.**

---

# 12. How to Watch a Modern Libero

When you watch a modern defender with the DNA of a Libero, look at four things.

### 1. Free Moments

Does he recognise the moments when nobody is his to mark?

### 2. Stepping Forward

In those moments, does he step forward — with the ball or without it?

### 3. Cover

When a teammate is beaten, is he the first to close the space?

### 4. Return

When the ball is lost, does he get back into position?

# FREEDOM WITH RESPONSIBILITY

---

# Conclusion — The Position Disappeared, but the Freedom Lives On

Picchi guarded the space behind.

Beckenbauer stepped forward.

Baresi began as a Libero and perfected a defence without one.

Sammer became the last great Libero.

Then zonal defending and the high line took the Libero's place away.

But the Libero's freedom did not disappear.

It lives on in Neuer's sweeping,

in the dropping movements of Busquets and Rodri,

in Stones' steps forward,

and in Bastoni's runs.

> **The Libero did not die. He simply stepped out from behind the defensive line — and spread across the whole pitch.**
