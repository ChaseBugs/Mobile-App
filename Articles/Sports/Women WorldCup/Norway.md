# Norway — 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup Opponent Analysis

## Gresshoppene

> Data as of 17 October 2026. The 2027 Women's World Cup will be held in Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027. The final tournament draw has not yet taken place.
>
> ⚠️ **Qualification status:** not yet qualified. Norway finished **second in League A Group 4** (behind Germany, 12 points) and entered the UEFA play-offs. They beat **Romania 2-0 and 6-0** in round 1 (October 2026). Round 2 is against **Wales (1 and 5 December 2026)**; the winner qualifies.
>
> **Purpose:** a scouting report for coaching staff — how Norway play, who decides their games, where they are vulnerable, and a concrete game plan.

**One-line verdict:** Norway have **two of the best attackers in the world** (Graham Hansen 89, Reiten 86) plus Hegerberg (85) and a new goal source in **Signe Gaupset** (8 goals in 22 caps). They **beat Japan and Brazil** in late 2025. But **Germany beat them 4-0 and 2-0**, scoring at **18' in both games** and twice in first-half stoppage time. Norway's **slow centre-backs** and **soft first-half endings** are the clear targets.

## 5. Head Coach — Gemma Grainger

- English coach; previously **Wales** head coach (2021–2024, took them to the 2023 World Cup play-offs), and England youth teams.
- **Results:** Euro 2025 group winners (three wins), QF exit to Italy; 8 wins in 11 games in 2025-26.
- **Approach:** 4-3-3 that **frees Graham Hansen and Reiten to attack 1v1**, with Hegerberg as the reference point. Has introduced young players (Gaupset, Terland, Østenstad).
- **Problem:** two heavy defeats to Germany — Norway were outplayed both home and away.

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

### Strategy 1: Press high and run at the centre-backs — early
- **Evidence:** Germany scored at 18' in both games.
- **How:** a high press on Engen and the centre-backs; quick forwards running behind them.

### Strategy 2: Attack hard at the end of the first half
- **Evidence:** 43', 44', 45+1', 45+4'.
- **How:** keep the tempo high in the last ten minutes of the half; send numbers into the box.

### Strategy 3: Double-mark Graham Hansen
- **Evidence:** OVR 89, DRI 90 — Norway's main creator.
- **How:** left-back plus left-sided midfielder; show her down the line onto her weaker foot.

### Strategy 4: Track Gaupset's runs
- **Evidence:** five goals from midfield runs.
- **How:** the holding midfielder follows her into the box.

### Strategy 5: Be ready at the start of the second half
- **Evidence:** Norway scored at 46', 48', 52', 53', 54'.
- **How:** full concentration from the restart; no early-second-half substitutions that disrupt shape.

### Strategy 6: Compact block for teams that can't press
- **Evidence:** Italy beat Norway 2-1 with a compact block and a late goal.
- **How:** two lines of four, narrow; counter into the space behind Norway's attacking full-backs.

```text
HIGH PRESS + PACE AT THE CBs (first 20')
          +
ATTACK 40'–45+'
          +
DOUBLE-MARK GRAHAM HANSEN
          +
TRACK GAUPSET
          +
STAY FOCUSED AT 45'–55'
          ↓
GERMANY 4-0 AND 2-0 — THE BLUEPRINT
```

## 13. Conclusion

Norway have **elite attacking talent**: Graham Hansen (89), Reiten (86), Hegerberg (85) and the rising Gaupset. They beat **Japan and Brazil** in late 2025, and they are dangerous at the start of every second half.

But **Germany beat them 6-0 over two games** with the same plan both times: **press, run at the slow centre-backs, score by 18'**, and punish the lapses before half-time.

> **Run at Norway's back line early, double up on Graham Hansen, and strike before the break.**
