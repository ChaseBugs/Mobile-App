# SERIES 09 — The Second Striker

# EP01 — Where Did the Second Striker Go?

## What Happened to Players Like Del Piero, Bergkamp and Totti?

3 May 2026, San Siro. Inter vs Parma.

Marcus Thuram scored the opener.

Then Lautaro Martínez set up Mkhitaryan for the second.

2-0. With three games to spare, Inter had sealed the 2025-26 Serie A title.

**Two strikers had decided the title together.**

Twenty years ago, that would not have been special at all.

Back then, almost every team played with two forwards.

And one of those two was often a very particular kind of player.

He scored goals, but he was not a classic striker.

He passed like a playmaker, but he was not a midfielder.

**Del Piero.**

**Bergkamp.**

**Totti.**

**Raúl.**

**Baggio.**

We had a name for them:

# SECOND STRIKER

Today, you hardly ever hear the term.

So where did they go?

---

# 1. What Is a Second Striker?

The Second Striker lives **between the No.9 and the No.10.**

```text
              No.9          ← FRONT LINE, BATTLES THE DEFENDERS
               ↑
         SECOND STRIKER     ← THE SPACE BETWEEN THE LINES
               ↑
              No.10         ← CREATES FROM MIDFIELD
```

The No.9 fights the opposing centre-backs.

The No.10 builds attacks from midfield.

The Second Striker operates in between — **in the space between the opponent's defensive line and midfield line.**

And in that space, he does two things at once:

**He scores goals.**

**He creates goals.**

In Italy, the role was called the **“seconda punta,”** and it overlapped with the **“trequartista.”**

Some people simply called it the **“No.9½.”**

# BETWEEN THE LINES

---

# 2. The World of Two Strikers — Where the Second Striker Lived

The Second Striker existed because of **a system.**

In the 1990s and early 2000s, the default system in European football was the **4-4-2.**

```text
4-4-2

      ST      SS      ← FRONT TWO
  LM   CM   CM   RM
  LB   CB   CB   RB
          GK
```

With two forwards, you can split the roles:

**One pushes the defenders back. (Target / Finisher)**

**The other uses the space in front of them. (Second Striker)**

Think of the famous front twos of that era:

```text
Juventus     Trezeguet  +  DEL PIERO
Roma         Batistuta  +  TOTTI
Arsenal      Henry      +  BERGKAMP
Real Madrid  Morientes  +  RAÚL
```

In each pairing, the player in capitals was closest to the Second Striker role.

While Batistuta dragged the opposing centre-backs around, Totti received the ball in the space in front of them.

And Roma won Serie A in 2000-01.

# ONE PINS, ONE CREATES

---

# 3. Del Piero — The Artist Who Scored Goals

Alessandro Del Piero played 19 seasons for Juventus.

**705 appearances — a club record.**

**Around 290 goals — also a club record.**

(Depending on the source, the total is recorded as 289 or 290.)

His signature was cutting in from the left and curling the ball into the far corner.

In Italy, they called it the **“gol alla Del Piero.”**

```text
       GOAL
   ┌─────────┐
   │       ↖ │  ← FAR TOP CORNER
   └─────────┘
        ↑
   DEL PIERO
  (LEFT → INSIDE)
```

2006 World Cup semi-final in Dortmund. Italy vs Germany.

Moments after Grosso's late extra-time opener, Del Piero finished a counter-attack to make it 2-0.

Italy reached the final — and won it.

That summer, Juventus were relegated to Serie B because of the Calciopoli scandal.

Many stars left.

Del Piero stayed.

In 2006-07, he finished as **Serie B's top scorer with 20 goals** and took Juventus straight back to the top flight.

# SCORER + ARTIST

---

# 4. Bergkamp — The Genius Who Saw Space

Dennis Bergkamp's spell at Inter (1993-95) was close to a failure.

Inside Italy's defensive football, he was isolated.

In the summer of 1995, Arsenal signed him for **around £7.5 million, a club-record fee at the time.**

And Bergkamp changed English football.

In 1997-98, Arsenal won the league and FA Cup double, and Bergkamp was named **PFA Players' Player of the Year.**

March 2002, away at Newcastle.

With his back to goal, Bergkamp flicked the pass around one side of his marker with his first touch, spun the other way past him, and scored.

Arsenal supporters later voted it **the greatest goal in the club's history.**

```text
PASS ──→ BERGKAMP (BACK TO GOAL)
               ↓
   FIRST TOUCH FLICKS THE BALL AROUND
               ↓
      SPINS PAST THE DEFENDER
               ↓
              GOAL
```

Bergkamp's real weapon was **his first touch and his vision.**

While Henry ran in behind, Bergkamp received the ball in the space in front of the defence and found him.

# THE FIRST TOUCH SEES THE GAME

---

# 5. Totti — The Moment the Second Striker Became a False 9

Francesco Totti's story is special in this series.

It shows **the moment the Second Striker turned into the False 9.**

In 2005-06, Roma were short of a genuine striker because of injuries and departures.

Coach Luciano Spalletti made a decision:

**Play Totti at the top of the attack.**

But Totti did not fight the centre-backs like a classic No.9.

He kept dropping deep to receive the ball.

That left the opposing centre-backs with a dilemma.

```text
TOTTI DROPS DEEP
        ↓
IF THE CB FOLLOWS  → SPACE BEHIND → RUNNERS FROM MIDFIELD
IF THE CB STAYS    → TOTTI IS FREE → ATTACK BUILDS
```

With this system, Roma won 11 Serie A games in a row — a record at the time.

The run was overshadowed when Totti suffered a broken ankle in February 2006.

Then, in 2006-07, playing the same role, Totti scored **26 Serie A goals and won the European Golden Shoe.**

Many people described that Roma as effectively playing a **4-6-0.**

It was three years before Guardiola used Messi as a False 9.

# THE SECOND STRIKER BECOMES THE FALSE 9

---

# 6. Why Did the Second Striker Disappear? — ① The Lone Striker

From the mid-2000s, the systems of European football began to change.

**4-4-2 → 4-4-1-1 → 4-2-3-1 / 4-3-3**

```text
4-2-3-1

          ST          ← LONE STRIKER
   LW    No.10   RW
      No.6  No.6
  LB   CB   CB   RB
          GK
```

The number of forwards dropped to one.

So where did the Second Striker go?

**He had to become the lone striker, become a No.10, or move out wide.**

The very job of “one of the front two” disappeared.

---

# 7. Why Did It Disappear? — ② The Space Between the Lines Vanished

The Second Striker lived **between the opponent's defensive line and midfield line.**

But modern defending squeezed that space.

```text
2000s                    2020s

MF ────────              MF ────────
                            ↕ 10–15m
   (WIDE SPACE)          DF ────────
       ↕ 25m
DF ────────
```

Defensive midfielders like Makélélé emerged (SERIES 03),

and whole teams began to defend far more compactly.

The Second Striker lost the time and space to receive the ball and turn.

# NO SPACE BETWEEN THE LINES

---

# 8. Why Did It Disappear? — ③ The Duty to Press

Modern forwards must defend.

The press starts with the forwards.

Many Second Strikers of the 2000s carried little defensive burden.

When the ball was lost, the midfielders defended.

But modern football asks:

> **“What do you do when you don't have the ball?”**

It is the same reason the classic No.10 declined, as we saw in SERIES 02.

# PRESSING KILLED THE LUXURY ROLE

---

# 9. Where Did They Go?

But the Second Striker's abilities did not disappear.

**They moved into other positions.**

```text
                    SECOND STRIKER
                          │
    ┌──────────┬──────────┼──────────┬──────────────┐
    ↓          ↓          ↓          ↓              ↓
 FALSE 9     No.10      INSIDE    RAUMDEUTER    DEEP-LYING 9
 (Messi)   (4-2-3-1)   FORWARD    (Müller)        (Kane)
                       (Salah)
```

**False 9** — In May 2009, in the 6-2 win over Real Madrid at the Bernabéu, Guardiola moved Messi into the centre of the attack. Messi kept dropping deep and tore the opposing defence apart. The role Totti had shown at Roma was perfected on the biggest stage in the world.

**Raumdeuter** — Thomas Müller's own description of himself: “the interpreter of space.” His dribbling and shooting were never spectacular, but he always arrived first in the empty space between defenders. In 2019-20, he set a Bundesliga record with 21 assists. In 2025, he left Bayern for the Vancouver Whitecaps.

**Deep-Lying 9** — Harry Kane is a centre-forward, but he regularly drops into midfield to link play. And in 2025-26, he scored **36 Bundesliga goals for Bayern**, finishing as the league's top scorer for the third year running and winning the European Golden Shoe.

**Inside Forward** — A winger who cuts in from the flank to score. As we saw in SERIES 05, many goal-scoring attacking players moved out wide.

# THE ROLE DIED. THE SKILLS SURVIVED.

---

# 10. The Last Second Striker — Griezmann

If you had to name the modern player closest to a classic Second Striker,

many people would say **Antoine Griezmann.**

Simeone's Atlético Madrid were one of the rare modern teams to stay loyal to the 4-4-2.

Inside that system, Griezmann played alongside the centre-forward — dropping deep, linking play, scoring and pressing.

At the 2018 World Cup, he led France to the title.

Griezmann showed what a Second Striker needs to survive in modern football.

```text
2000s SECOND STRIKER  =  SCORING + CREATING
GRIEZMANN             =  SCORING + CREATING + PRESSING + DEFENDING
```

He left Atlético as **the club's all-time top scorer, with 211 goals.**

In March 2026, Orlando City announced his signing, and he moved to MLS that summer.

**It may be the final chapter of an era closing.**

# THE MODERN SECOND STRIKER MUST DEFEND

---

# 11. Are Two Strikers Coming Back?

But there is an interesting change.

Some teams are using **two strikers** again.

The most prominent is Inter.

In a 3-5-2, **Lautaro Martínez and Marcus Thuram** — nicknamed “ThuLa” — became the best attacking duo in Serie A.

Thuram runs into space, wins physical duels and drifts wide.

Lautaro scores in the space that opens, drops deep to link play and leads the press.

And in 2025-26, Inter won Serie A with the two of them up front.

```text
2000s FRONT TWO               2020s FRONT TWO
TARGET + SECOND STRIKER   →   BOTH PRESS + BOTH SCORE
```

The modern front two is not the same as the old one.

But **the principle of two forwards creating space for each other** is coming back to life.

*(This topic is covered in detail in EP05.)*

---

# 12. How to Watch a Second Striker

When you watch a modern player with the DNA of a Second Striker, look at four things.

### 1. Where Does He Receive?

Does he receive the ball between the opponent's defensive and midfield lines?

### 2. First Touch

Does his first touch take him towards goal?

### 3. Final Action

Can he choose between the shot and the final pass?

### 4. Partnership

Does he create space for the forward next to him — and use the space that forward creates?

# BETWEEN THE LINES, TOWARDS THE GOAL

---

# Conclusion — The Position Disappeared, but the Role Lives On

Del Piero, Bergkamp, Totti, Raúl.

They were players born into the world of the 4-4-2.

The lone striker, pressing and compact defending changed that world.

So the name “Second Striker” has almost disappeared.

But their abilities have not.

They live on in Messi's False 9,

in Müller's Raumdeuter,

in Kane's deep-lying 9,

and in the front two of Lautaro and Thuram.

> **The Second Striker never disappeared. He was simply reborn under different names.**
