**One-line verdict:** The deepest squad at the tournament, with **elite pace on both wings** (Rodman PAC 91, Thompson 93, Wilson 91) and a relentless second-half surge. But **9 of their last 11 goals conceded came in the first half**, and the two best teams they faced in 2026, **Spain and Brazil**, both scored **twice in the first 15 minutes**. The USA are beatable early, through their midfield, by a team that keeps the ball.

## 5. Head Coach — Emma Hayes

- Appointed May 2024 after 12 years at **Chelsea** (seven WSL titles, a Champions League final).
- **Record:** 42 games, **33 W, 3 D, 6 L** (.821), Olympic gold in her first tournament.
- **Philosophy:**
  - **Rotation and depth:** fully changed her XI between Japan games (April 2026) and blooded more than a dozen new players in 2025-26.
  - **Pressing intensity** and **chance quality** — "creating at least one xG a half against the top side," she said after the Japan series, contrasting it with the old reliance on "a super goal or one-off chance."
  - Seeks hostile tests: she wanted games in which "every fan in the stadium is cheering loudly against us" (Brazil, June 2026).
- **Trend to note:** her team is improving at breaking down blocks (3-0 vs Japan, 65% possession) but still concedes early against top possession sides.

## 6. Tactics and Formation

### 6.1 Shapes

```text
OUT OF POSSESSION — 4-3-3 HIGH PRESS / 4-4-2 MID BLOCK

        RODMAN            WILSON            THOMPSON / SWANSON
                 LAVELLE            HEAPS
                       HUTTON / COFFEY
   G. THOMPSON / FOX   GIRMA      DAVIDSON / SONNETT   FOX / PATTERSON
                       DICKEY / TULLIS-JOYCE

IN POSSESSION — 2-3-5 / 3-2-5

                RODMAN   LAVELLE   WILSON   HEAPS   A. THOMPSON
                              (wingers hold the touchline)
                     FOX (inverted)    HUTTON / COFFEY
                   GIRMA           DAVIDSON          (G. THOMPSON pushes high)
                              DICKEY
```

### 6.2 Build-Up
- **Centre-backs split wide**; one full-back (usually Emily Fox, PAS 71, DRI 79) **inverts into midfield** next to the No. 6.
- **Girma** (CB, OVR 84, PAS 70) and **Davidson** carry the ball into midfield.
- The goal is a quick switch to an isolated winger. **Rodman and Thompson** receive wide and attack 1v1.

### 6.3 Attacking Entry
1. **Wide isolation 1v1** — Rodman (PAC 91, PHY 82), Thompson (PAC 93), Swanson (PAC 84).
2. **Wilson in behind** — PAC 91, SHO 87; the most complete striker in the pool.
3. **Lavelle between the lines** — DRI 87, PAS 84; assisted in both Japan wins (free-kick volley, through ball).
4. **Heaps late into the box** — SHO 84; scored vs Argentina (20') and Japan (48').
5. **Right-side combination** — Rodman + Wilson combined repeatedly against Japan.

### 6.4 Pressing Triggers
- **Back pass to a centre-back** → front three jump, wingers curve the press inside.
- **Square pass from the full-back** → Heaps/Lavelle step up.
- Gisele Thompson forced the turnover that produced the winner against Japan (11 April).

### 6.5 Defensive Block and Transitions
- Mid block 4-4-2 with Lavelle stepping up next to the striker.
- **Weak spot:** when the press is beaten, the **space between the No. 6 and the centre-backs** opens. Japan's winner (14 April) came from winning the ball in midfield and springing forward; Hamano found "lots of space on the right side inside the box."
- **Rest defence is thin** because both full-backs push high.

### 6.6 Set Pieces
- **Lavelle free kicks/corners**; Girma scored from a set play vs Japan (47').
- A shot from Wilson in the first minute of the second half forced the corner that opened the scoring vs Japan (17 April).

### 6.7 Game-State Behaviour

| State | What the USA do | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0-0, first 20' | Press high, **but vulnerable to early goals** | Spain 3' and 14'; Brazil 11' and 14' |
| Trailing at half-time | **Bring on fresh 1v1 forwards** and push the full-backs higher | Spain 0-2 → 2-2 (49', 86'); Brazil 1-2 L |
| Leading | Defend the result; very solid in the second half | Spain (10 Oct): **no Spanish shot in the second half** |
| 0-0 late | Bench impact | Thompson 82' vs Colombia; Sentnor 55' vs Canada; Swanson 86' vs Spain |

## 10. Signature Tactics — Analysis of Six Games

### Game ① 10 October 2026 — USA 0-2 Spain (Washington DC)

| Minute | Event |
|---|---|
| **3'** | **Guijarro strike from well outside the box — 0-1** (Dickey beaten) |
| **14'** | **Paralluelo crosses from the left; Navarro unmarked, volleys — 0-2** |
| 15'–45' | Spain control the ball |
| 2nd half | USA press higher — **Spain do not have a shot in the second half** |
| — | **Nanclares saves a US penalty**; Wilson goal disallowed for offside (VAR) |
| Final | USA outshoot Spain 13-6 (10-3 on target), but lose 0-2 |

**How Spain won:** they **scored before the US press found its rhythm**, with a long shot and a cross to the back post exposing a full-back caught high. Then they let the USA have the ball.
**Lesson:** **The first 15 minutes are the window.** Shoot early, attack the space behind the advanced full-back, then defend the lead with possession and a compact block.

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

### Strategy 1: Attack in the first 15 minutes
- **Evidence:** Spain 3' and 14'; Brazil 11' and 14' (three minutes after conceding).
- **How:** set up to attack from the kick-off; shoot from distance early; deliver early crosses to the back post.

### Strategy 2: Play through the press and attack the space behind the full-backs
- **Evidence:** Hamano's winner for Japan came from a midfield turnover and a direct run into the space the US full-back had left.
- **How:** a press-resistant No. 6 and two centre-backs comfortable on the ball; a fast winger stays high on the side of the inverted full-back.

### Strategy 3: Run at the slower left-sided centre-back
- **Evidence:** Davidson PAC 60, Rudd PAC 65.
- **How:** the fastest forward plays on that side; diagonal runs behind.

### Strategy 4: Keep the ball after scoring — don't sit too deep
- **Evidence:** Spain (10 Oct) used possession and allowed no second-half shot. Spain (13 Oct) sat deeper and conceded twice after half-time.
- **How:** circulate the ball in midfield; target the US front three, who do not track back (DEF 45–55).

### Strategy 5: Prepare for the 45'–65' surge and the bench
- **Evidence:** three goals in 17 minutes vs Japan; Sanchez 49' and Swanson 86' vs Spain; Thompson 82' vs Colombia.
- **How:** make defensive substitutions early (60'); match the US bench with fresh full-backs; avoid conceding corners at the start of the second half.

### Strategy 6: Make them shoot from bad positions
- **Evidence:** 13 shots, 0 goals (Spain); 12 shots, 0 goals (Japan).
- **How:** a compact box defence that blocks central shots and forces the US wide.

### Strategy 7: Test the goalkeeper
- **Evidence:** Guijarro's long-range goal; neither No. 1 has an established international record.
- **How:** shots from 20–25 metres, early crosses into the six-yard box.

```text
          +

          +

          +

          +

          ↓
SPAIN 2-0 AND BRAZIL 2-1 — THE BLUEPRINT FOR 2027
```

## 13. Conclusion

The USA have the **deepest squad in the world**: 44 players rated 79+, three wide forwards with PAC 91–93, and a bench that wins games after the 80th minute. They are Olympic champions and will be among the favourites in Brazil.

But the evidence of 2026 is clear. The best possession teams, **Spain and Brazil**, beat them by **scoring early** and attacking the space behind the full-backs. Japan beat them with **one counter-attack**. The USA concede **82% of their goals before half-time** and score **80% of their goals against top teams after it**.

> **Win the first half against the USA — and keep the ball long enough to survive the second.**
