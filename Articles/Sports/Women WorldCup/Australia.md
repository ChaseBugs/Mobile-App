# Australia — 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup Opponent Analysis

## The Matildas

> Data as of 17 October 2026. The 2027 Women's World Cup will be held in Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027. The final tournament draw has not yet taken place.
>
> **Purpose:** a scouting report for coaching staff — how Australia play, who decides their games, where they are vulnerable, and a concrete game plan.

---

## 0. At a Glance

```text
FIFA RANKING        15th (official release of 16 June 2026) — highest-ever 4th (2017–18)
HEAD COACH          Joe Montemurro (since 2 June 2025; formerly Arsenal, Juventus, OL Lyonnes)
BASE FORMATION      4-3-3 / 4-2-3-1 (Kennedy as a holding midfielder or centre-back)
CAPTAIN             Sam Kerr (Gotham FC) — record scorer, 76 goals in 143 caps
BEST WORLD CUP      Fourth (2023, co-hosts)
QUALIFICATION       2026 Asian Cup QF winners (13 March 2026, 2-1 vs DPRK)
RECENT RESULTS      2026 Asian Cup runners-up (0-1 vs Japan) — third final lost 0-1 to Japan
RECORD (12 MONTHS)  W 11  D 1  L 4  (losses: England, Japan, Mexico, Germany)
```

**One-line verdict:** A physical, experienced, never-say-die team with strong set pieces and four world-class players (Kerr, Foord, Carpenter, Catley). They beat Asia's counter-attacking sides — but **lose every time an elite team keeps the ball against them**, and their centre-backs are among the slowest at the tournament.

## 5. Head Coach — Joe Montemurro

```text
Arsenal (2019 WSL title), Juventus (Serie A titles), OL Lyonnes (one season)
Jun 2025  Appointed (replacing interim Tom Sermanni)
Mar 2026  Asian Cup runners-up
```

### Profile
- Wants a team that **dominates possession, controls the tempo and presses proactively.**
- **Experiments with roles:** Alanna Kennedy as a holding midfielder — she scored five Asian Cup goals and was named the tournament's best player.
- **Honest about problems:** after the group stage, the team "tended to drop off a little bit after scoring" and "distances were too long"; after England (0-3), "we didn't start the game well."
- Five changes for the England game after beating Wales — and lost midfield control without Clare Wheeler.

## 6. Tactics and Formation

### 6.1 Shapes

```text
IN POSSESSION (4-3-3)

        FOORD                KERR                FOWLER / RASO
             COONEY-CROSS                SAYER / GORRY
                       KENNEDY / WHEELER
  CATLEY / NEVIN      HUNT        HEATLEY        CARPENTER        ← CARPENTER PUSHES HIGH
                          ARNOLD
```

```text
OUT OF POSSESSION (4-4-2 / 4-5-1 MID-BLOCK)

               KERR        FOWLER
   FOORD    COONEY-CROSS   KENNEDY    RASO
  NEVIN       HUNT        CATLEY      CARPENTER
                     ARNOLD
```

### 6.2 What Australia Do Well

| Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Set pieces** | Kennedy: 5 Asian Cup goals, including headers and the 90+8' equaliser |
| **Clinical with few chances** | 4 shots, 2 goals vs the DPRK |
| **Right-side combinations** | Foord → Carpenter → cut-back → Foord (China 17') |
| **Kerr's movement** | Near-post finish (Korea 45+6'); rounding the keeper at a tight angle (China 58') |
| **Late resilience** | Kennedy 90+8' vs Korea; never-say-die culture |
| **Second-half physical pressure** | Pinned Japan back in the final ("controlled chaos") |

### 6.3 What Goes Wrong

| Problem | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Losing midfield control | England (0-3) — "lack of control in midfield"; Japan final |
| Dropping off after scoring | Korea scored three after Australia led |
| Long distances between lines | Montemurro's own words |
| Counters against them | Korea's 14' goal came from a counter "four minutes after Australia were wasteful" |

### 6.4 Game-State Behaviour

| State | What Australia do |
|---|---|
| 0-0 | Direct, physical, crosses to Kerr and Kennedy |
| Leading | **Drop off** — distances grow (Korea 3-3) |
| Trailing | Throw bodies forward; aerial bombardment (90+8' vs Korea) |
| Against a possession team | Chase — and concede on transitions (Japan, Germany, England) |

## 10. Signature Tactics — Analysis of Seven Games Against Strong Opponents

### Game ① 12 August 2023, World Cup QF — Australia 0-0 France (won 7-6 on pens)
- Held France for 120 minutes; won the longest shootout in World Cup history.
**Lesson:** Australia can **hold a top team and win on penalties.**

### Game ② 28 October 2025 — England 3-0 Australia (Derby)
| Minute | Moment |
|---|---|
| ~19' | **Kennedy red card** (denied an obvious chance) → Beever-Jones from the rebound of the free kick |
| Before HT | Bronze 2-0 |
| 90+8' | Stanway pen (VAR) |

Reports: "a lack of control in midfield," an attack that was "passive and toothless."
**Lesson:** Against an elite possession team, **Australia's midfield disappears** — and pace in behind gets Kennedy sent off.

### Game ③ 8 March 2026, Asian Cup — Australia 3-3 Korea Republic (Sydney)
| Minute | Moment |
|---|---|
| ~14' | **Mun Eun-ju** on the counter — Jeon Yu-gyeong cross from the left |
| 32' | Kennedy |
| 45+6' | Kerr (near post) |
| 53' | **Nevin handball** → Kim Shin-ji pen |
| 57' | **Kang Chae-rim** — given space by the left-back |
| 90+8' | **Kennedy** equaliser |

**Lessons:** Australia's **left side** (Nevin) is attackable; they **concede on counters** after wasting chances; they **never give up** at set pieces.

### Game ④ 13 March 2026, Asian Cup QF — Australia 2-1 DPRK (Perth)
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Possession | DPRK 62% |
| Shots | Australia 4, DPRK 21 |
| Goals | Kennedy 9', Kerr 47' — **both from DPRK errors** |
| Saves | Arnold, incl. a 35-metre shot |

**Lesson:** Australia are **comfortable without the ball** against teams that press but cannot finish.

### Game ⑤ 17 March 2026, Asian Cup SF — China 1-2 Australia (Perth)
| Minute | Moment |
|---|---|
| 17' | **Foord** — Carpenter cut-back |
| 26' | **Arnold charged out → penalty** (Zhang Linyan) |
| 58' | **Kerr** — Torpey's lunge won the ball, Foord's pass, Kerr rounded the GK |

**Lesson:** Right-side combinations + Kerr's finishing — but a **goalkeeping risk** gave away a penalty.

### Game ⑥ 21 March 2026, Asian Cup Final — Japan 1-0 Australia (Sydney)
- Hamano 17' (turn and shot from outside the box); Australia 54% possession, dominated the second half, Kennedy's 88' header saved.
- Sam Kerr: "They beat us just off one moment."
**Lesson:** Australia's pressure is **intense but imprecise** — "controlled chaos."

### Game ⑦ 9 October 2026 — Germany 4-2 Australia (Karlsruhe)
- **Kössler 23' and 30'** — two goals in seven minutes; Küver 83'; Nüsken 90+4' (corner).
**Lesson:** Fast forwards running in behind **Kennedy and Hunt** destroy Australia.

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

### Strategy ① — Keep the Ball
- **Evidence:** England, Japan, Germany.
- Australia's midfield (71–77) cannot win it back from a technical team.

### Strategy ② — Run in Behind Kennedy and Hunt

```text
AUSTRALIA MID-BLOCK
         ↓
QUICK PASS INTO THE CHANNEL BEHIND KENNEDY (PAC 37) / HUNT (61)
         ↓
FAST FORWARD 1v1 → GOAL OR RED CARD
```

### Strategy ③ — Attack Australia's Left
- **Evidence:** Korea's 57' goal and 53' penalty.

### Strategy ④ — Defend Set Pieces With Our Best Headers
- Mark Kennedy, Catley and Kerr; win the first header; clear wide.

### Strategy ⑤ — Score First, Then Keep Possession
- Australia drop off after scoring — and struggle to unlock a team that keeps the ball after scoring.

### Strategy ⑥ — Counter When Carpenter Pushes Up
- Space behind the right-back after her overlaps.

### Strategy ⑦ — Concentrate Late
- Australia scored at 90+8' (Korea) and 45+6' (Korea), 45+2' (Germany).

### Summary

```text
KEEP THE BALL (THE JAPAN MODEL)
          +
PACE IN BEHIND KENNEDY / HUNT
          +
ATTACK THE LEFT SIDE · COUNTER BEHIND CARPENTER
          +
MARK KENNEDY AND KERR AT SET PIECES
          ↓
TURN AUSTRALIA'S PHYSICALITY INTO CHASING
```

---

## 13. Conclusion

Australia are physical, experienced and dangerous from set pieces, with Kerr still their talisman and Kennedy their set-piece weapon. But the evidence is clear:

1. **They lose to possession teams**
2. **Their centre-backs cannot handle pace in behind**
3. **They drop off after scoring**
4. **Their left side and their goalkeeper's decisions are attackable**

> **Don't fight Australia in the air. Pass around them, and run behind them.**
