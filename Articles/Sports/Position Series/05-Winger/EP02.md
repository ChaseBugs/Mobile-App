# SERIES 05 — The Evolution of the Winger

# EP02 — Beckham & Figo vs Salah & Vinícius

## How Did the Winger Become a Completely Different Position in 20 Years?

Playing on the wing does not mean playing the same role.

David Beckham received the ball on the right and thought:

**How can I deliver the perfect ball into the box?**

Luís Figo faced a defender and thought:

**How can I beat him and create the next space?**

Mohamed Salah starts on the right and often asks:

**How can I attack the goal?**

Vinícius Júnior starts on the left and looks for:

**How can I break the defensive line and enter the box?**

They are all wingers.

But their use of wide space is very different.

```text
BECKHAM  → Deliver
FIGO     → Beat

SALAH    → Attack Goal
VINÍCIUS → Break Line
```

To understand the evolution of the winger, we should not only compare positions.

We should compare **what each player is trying to achieve after receiving the ball**.

---

# 1. Beckham — A Winger Who Moved the Ball More Than the Defender

One of Beckham’s greatest strengths was that he did not always need to dribble past the full-back.

He needed only a small amount of space and a clear view of the box.

```text
        ST
         ↑
         │
         │ CROSS
         │
BECKHAM ●
```

He could look up, read the striker’s movement and deliver the ball between the defensive line and goalkeeper.

His game was therefore less about:

**Beat the Defender**

and more about:

**Beat the Defensive Line with the Ball**

He did not always have to pass the defender himself.

**The ball did it for him.**

---

# 2. Beckham’s Real Weapon Was Timing

A good cross is not only about technique.

Timing matters just as much.

```text
ST      → RUN
           ↑
           │
        CROSS
           │
      BECKHAM
```

Too early, and the striker may not be ready.

Too late, and the defence has time to recover.

So Beckham’s key question was often:

**“When should I deliver?”**

His winger play was built around:

**Timing + Delivery**

rather than constant 1v1 dribbling.

---

# 3. Figo — A Winger Who Moved the Defender

Figo played in a similar era, but his method was very different.

He enjoyed confronting defenders directly.

```text
FIGO ●      ● FB

     1v1
```

His strength was not only pace.

Body Feints.

Close Control.

Change of Direction.

Acceleration.

He tried to make the defender move first.

Then he attacked the opposite space.

```text
        INSIDE
          ↖

FIGO ● → FB

          ↘
        OUTSIDE
```

His basic mechanism was:

**Move the Defender → Attack the Space**

---

# 4. Even Beckham and Figo Were Different

This is important.

Not every old-school winger was simply a crosser.

Beckham and Figo already represented very different styles.

| Player      | Main Weapon     | Main Question                |
| ----------- | --------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Beckham** | Delivery        | When should I send the ball? |
| **Figo**    | 1v1 + Dribbling | How do I move the defender?  |

Beckham beat defensive structures with the ball.

Figo beat defenders with his own movement.

But they shared one important idea:

**The wide zone itself was a major working area.**

They created from the wing and connected the attack toward the centre.

---

# 5. Salah — The Wing Is a Starting Point

With Salah, the same right-side space has a different meaning.

He may start wide.

But the touchline is often not his destination.

```text
RIGHT SIDE

SALAH ●
      ↙
        ↙
          BOX
           ↓
          GOAL
```

The key movement is diagonal.

As Salah moves inside, he gets closer to goal.

At the same time, the defender must move sideways and backwards.

For Salah, the right wing is often not the final attacking zone.

It is a **Launching Point toward Goal**.

---

# 6. Salah’s Opponent Is Not Only the Full-Back

For a traditional 1v1 winger, the most obvious opponent is the full-back.

```text
WINGER vs FULL-BACK
```

But when Salah attacks inside, the problem changes.

```text
        CB
         ↘
SALAH → → GOAL
         ↗
        FB
```

Now the centre-back also has to react.

That transforms the winger from a wide problem into a central defensive problem.

**Wide Player → Central Defensive Threat**

His movement can change responsibilities across the entire back line.

---

# 7. Vinícius — Using Width Without Staying Wide

Vinícius is different again.

He actively uses the touchline and large spaces.

```text
Touchline

VINÍCIUS ●      ● RB
            1v1
```

Starting wide gives him two major advantages.

First, he can drag the defender away from the centre.

Second, he creates more space for acceleration.

```text
VINÍCIUS ●
          ↘
            ↘
          SPACE
```

But what happens after he beats the full-back is important.

A more traditional sequence might be:

```text
Beat FB
   ↓
CROSS
```

Vinícius can instead continue:

```text
Beat FB
   ↓
ATTACK BOX
   ↓
PASS / SHOT
```

He still values width.

But **width is not the final objective**.

It is a tool for reaching more dangerous space.

---

# 8. Same Position, Different Target Space

The easiest way to compare these four players is to look at their **Target Space**.

### Beckham

```text
RIGHT SIDE
     ↓
DELIVERY ZONE
     ↓
CROSS → BOX
```

The objective is to reach a position where he can deliver.

---

### Figo

```text
WIDE
 ↓
1v1
 ↓
BEAT DEFENDER
 ↓
CREATE SPACE
```

The objective is to eliminate or manipulate the defender.

---

### Salah

```text
WIDE
 ↓
DIAGONAL
 ↓
CENTRAL
 ↓
GOAL
```

The objective is to move from wide space toward the goal.

---

### Vinícius

```text
WIDE
 ↓
ISOLATE
 ↓
ACCELERATE
 ↓
BOX
```

The objective is to use width to break the line and attack the penalty area.

They may all be called wingers.

But their attacking maps are different.

---

# 9. Body Orientation Changed Too

Another useful way to study winger evolution is **Body Orientation**.

Beckham often received while preparing to see the box and the striker’s movement.

```text
BECKHAM ● → Look at BOX
             ↗
           CROSS
```

Figo often wanted to face the defender directly.

```text
FIGO ● ↔ ● FB
```

Salah frequently shapes his body toward the inside and the goal.

```text
SALAH ●
      ↙
    FACE GOAL
```

Vinícius often opens his body to attack forward into large space.

```text
VINÍCIUS ● → → →
               FB
```

The difference is not only technical.

It starts with **what the player sees when he receives**.

---

# 10. They Ask Defenders Different Questions

Great attackers force defenders to make decisions.

But these four players ask different questions.

### Beckham

**“How quickly will you close me down before I deliver?”**

Give him too much space and the cross arrives.

---

### Figo

**“Will you protect the inside or the outside?”**

Move first, and he can attack the opposite direction.

---

### Salah

**“Does the full-back follow him inside, or does the centre-back take over?”**

He attacks the moment when defensive responsibility becomes unclear.

---

### Vinícius

**“How much open space can you afford to give him in a 1v1?”**

The larger the space, the more dangerous his acceleration becomes.

This is one of the biggest changes in the winger role.

Older wingers often challenged the full-back directly.

Modern wingers increasingly create problems for the **entire defensive line**.

---

# 11. The Success Criteria Changed

These four players should not be judged by exactly the same standards.

For Beckham, Delivery Quality was crucial.

For Figo, 1v1 ability and Chance Creation.

For Salah, Goal Threat and Final Actions.

For Vinícius, Line Breaking and Box Penetration.

```text
BECKHAM
Delivery
   ↓
Chance

FIGO
1v1
   ↓
Creation

SALAH
Movement
   ↓
Goal

VINÍCIUS
Break Line
   ↓
Box
```

This does not simply mean modern wingers are more attacking.

It means the winger’s **method of influencing the attack has expanded**.

---

# 12. The Biggest Change Was the Destination

The most important change over the last two decades is not only where the winger starts.

It is where he wants to finish the action.

```text
BECKHAM
Wide → Deliver → Teammate

FIGO
Wide → Beat → Create

SALAH
Wide → Inside → Goal

VINÍCIUS
Wide → Break → Box
```

For Beckham and Figo, the wing itself was often a major **Work Zone**.

For modern goalscoring wingers, the wing is often a **Starting Zone**.

That difference looks small on a tactics board.

But it changes the entire meaning of the position.

---

# Conclusion — Same Touchline, Different Football

Beckham played on the right.

Salah also plays from the right.

Figo built attacks from wide areas.

Vinícius also creates 1v1 situations near the touchline.

The pitch has not changed.

The touchline is still in the same place.

The penalty box is still in the same place.

What changed is how players interpret those spaces.

**Beckham used the wing to deliver.**

**Figo used the wing to manipulate defenders.**

**Salah uses the wing to attack the goal.**

**Vinícius uses the wing to break the defensive line.**

So the evolution of the winger is not simply:

**Crossing → Scoring**

A better description is:

**The destination changed.**

They may start in similar spaces.

But they see different targets.

They attack different defenders.

And they finish attacks in different ways.

**Same Touchline.
Different Destination.
Different Winger.**

## The Rise of the Inverted Winger

### Why Could Nobody Stop Robben Even When Everyone Knew Where He Was Going?

EP02 compared how the winger’s **destination** changed.

EP03 goes deeper into one specific movement:

**Cut Inside.**

Everyone knew Arjen Robben wanted to move onto his left foot.

And yet stopping him remained extremely difficult.

Why?

Because one cut inside did not create only one problem.

It forced the full-back, midfielder and centre-back to make **different decisions at the same time**.
