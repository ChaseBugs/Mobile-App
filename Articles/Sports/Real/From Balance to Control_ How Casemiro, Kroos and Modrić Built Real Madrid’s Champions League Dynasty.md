# From Balance to Control  
## How Casemiro, Kroos and Modrić Built Real Madrid’s Champions League Dynasty

Real Madrid’s 2014 Champions League triumph is often remembered through the images of Cristiano Ronaldo, Gareth Bale, Sergio Ramos, and Ángel Di María.

But beneath those attacking stars was a midfield that represented a crucial stage in the tactical evolution of modern Real Madrid:

**Xabi Alonso – Luka Modrić – Ángel Di María.**

That midfield delivered *La Décima*.

Yet the next transformation was arguably even more important.

By 2016, Real Madrid had evolved toward:

**Casemiro – Toni Kroos – Luka Modrić.**

The 2014 midfield was designed beautifully for balance, acceleration, and attacking transitions.

The 2016–18 midfield became something different.

It became a midfield capable of **controlling Champions League knockout football**.

And that evolution was one of the fundamental reasons Real Madrid achieved something unprecedented in the modern Champions League era:

**three consecutive European Cups.**

2016 🏆  
2017 🏆  
2018 🏆

To understand why this midfield was so successful, we have to understand how Real Madrid evolved from **speed, to balance, and finally to control.**

---

# 1. The Starting Point: Xabi–Modrić–Di María

Carlo Ancelotti’s 2013–14 midfield was one of the most fascinating combinations Real Madrid have produced.

The basic structure was:

```text
                 Benzema

Ronaldo                           Bale

         Di María       Modrić

             Xabi Alonso
```

Each midfielder solved a different problem.

### Xabi Alonso — The Architect

Xabi controlled the first phase of possession.

He could receive between the centre-backs, switch play, break lines with long passes, and determine the direction of Madrid’s attacks.

### Luka Modrić — The Press Breaker

Modrić provided something different.

He could receive under pressure, turn away from opponents, carry the ball through midfield, and connect the defensive and attacking phases.

### Ángel Di María — The Accelerator

Di María was the most unconventional member of the trio.

He was effectively a hybrid:

**Winger + No.8 + Ball Carrier.**

His extraordinary running power allowed Madrid to turn midfield possession into an attack almost instantly.

The structure therefore looked like this:

```text
              Xabi
                │
             CONTROL
                ↓
             Modrić
                │
         ESCAPE PRESSURE
                ↓
            Di María
                │
          ACCELERATION
                ↓
        Ronaldo / Bale
```

This was the midfield behind *La Décima*.

But it did not last.

---

# 2. The Summer of 2014 Changed Everything

After winning the Champions League, Madrid lost two critical midfielders.

Di María left for Manchester United.

Xabi Alonso joined Bayern Munich.

Meanwhile, Toni Kroos arrived from Bayern.

This created a major tactical question.

Who would replace Xabi Alonso?

Ancelotti initially answered:

**Toni Kroos.**

Madrid often used a structure resembling:

```text
        James / Isco

   Modrić       Kroos
```

Kroos became the deepest midfielder.

With the ball, this could be beautiful.

Kroos was exceptionally secure in possession and could dictate matches through his passing.

But there was a structural problem.

Toni Kroos was not a defensive midfielder in the traditional sense.

He could control possession.

He could organize build-up.

He could position himself intelligently.

But he was not a specialist defensive destroyer.

When Madrid lost possession, there was no natural defensive midfielder protecting the centre-backs.

That weakness became increasingly important.

---

# 3. The Missing Piece Was Casemiro

The player who eventually solved this problem was Casemiro.

When Zinedine Zidane took over during the 2015–16 season, Casemiro became increasingly important.

Eventually, the famous triangle emerged:

```text
          Kroos        Modrić

               Casemiro
```

This midfield worked because the three players were not identical.

In fact, they were almost opposites.

Their differences made them complementary.

The simplest way to understand their roles is:

**Casemiro = Protect**

**Kroos = Control**

**Modrić = Progress**

Or even more simply:

```text
            REAL MADRID

        Kroos        Modrić
          │             │
       CONTROL       PROGRESS
          │             │
          └──────┬──────┘
                 │
             Casemiro
                 │
              PROTECT
```

This triangle became the tactical foundation of Real Madrid’s European dynasty.

---

# 4. Casemiro: The Player Who Made Freedom Possible

Casemiro’s importance becomes clearer when we look at the players around him.

Madrid had Cristiano Ronaldo.

They had Benzema.

They had Bale.

Marcelo was an extremely attacking left-back.

Carvajal constantly advanced from right-back.

During possession, Madrid could therefore look like this:

```text
Marcelo ↑                         ↑ Carvajal

          Kroos       Modrić

Ronaldo        Benzema          Bale


               Casemiro
```

Many players were positioned ahead of the ball.

That created enormous attacking potential.

It also created risk.

What happened when Madrid lost possession?

That was Casemiro’s job.

```text
              BALL LOST
                  ↓

              Casemiro
           ←      ↓      →

        STOP TRANSITION
```

He protected the centre-backs.

He contested second balls.

He covered spaces behind Kroos and Modrić.

He committed tactical fouls when necessary.

He challenged physically powerful attackers.

And, perhaps most importantly, he slowed opposition counterattacks.

Sometimes a defensive midfielder does not need to win the ball immediately.

He simply needs to prevent the opponent from attacking quickly.

Those extra seconds allowed Kroos, Modrić, Marcelo, and Carvajal to recover their positions.

Casemiro therefore gave freedom to almost everyone else.

---

# 5. Kroos: Control the Speed of the Match

Toni Kroos performed a completely different function.

His greatest quality was not simply passing accuracy.

It was **tempo control**.

Kroos understood when Madrid needed to attack.

But equally importantly, he understood when Madrid should not attack.

Imagine the opponent pressing one side:

```text
       Opposition Press
              ↓↓↓

Marcelo ← Kroos → Modrić
             ↓
       Switch of Play
             ↓
         Carvajal
```

Kroos could move the ball away from pressure and force the opponent to reorganize.

If Madrid were leading, he could slow the match.

If the opponent was sitting deep, he could circulate possession until space appeared.

If the opponent pressed aggressively, he could switch the point of attack.

His role was therefore not:

> “Pass forward as quickly as possible.”

It was:

> **“Make the game move at the speed Real Madrid wants.”**

That distinction is fundamental.

---

# 6. Modrić Connected the Two Worlds

Then there was Luka Modrić.

He was the bridge between Casemiro’s defensive security and Kroos’s positional control.

Modrić could receive deep.

He could turn under pressure.

He could dribble through midfield.

He could combine in tight spaces.

He could press.

He could move wide.

He could enter the final third.

Conceptually:

```text
             DEFENCE
                ↓
            Casemiro
                ↓
             Modrić
                ↓
               TURN
                ↓
        Pressure Beaten
                ↓
             ATTACK
```

This was crucial because possession alone does not necessarily move a team forward.

Sometimes somebody must eliminate an opponent.

Modrić could do that without making a risky pass.

One turn could remove an opposing midfielder from the defensive structure.

One dribble could transform:

```text
5 vs 5
```

into:

```text
5 vs 4
```

That is why his press resistance became so valuable in Champions League football.

---

# 7. Three Players Solved Three Fundamental Problems

Every elite midfield has to answer three questions.

### Question 1: What happens when we have the ball?

**Kroos.**

### Question 2: How do we progress when the opponent presses?

**Modrić.**

### Question 3: What happens when we lose the ball?

**Casemiro.**

That created an extraordinary balance:

```text
              POSSESSION
                  │
                Kroos
                  │
                  ↓
              PROGRESSION
                  │
               Modrić
                  │
                  ↓
                ATTACK


              BALL LOST
                  │
                  ↓
              Casemiro
                  │
                  ↓
              PROTECTION
```

The three midfielders were solving different phases of the same game.

That is what made the combination so difficult to break.

---

# 8. Why This Was Perfect for Champions League Football

This becomes even more important when we consider the nature of the Champions League.

League football rewards consistency across many matches.

Knockout football presents a different challenge.

The opponent changes.

The tactical problem changes.

One week Madrid might face an aggressive pressing team.

The next opponent might defend extremely deep.

Another opponent might attack through transitions.

Another might dominate possession.

A Champions League-winning midfield therefore needs multiple solutions.

Casemiro–Kroos–Modrić had them.

### Against High Pressing

```text
Opponent presses
       ↓
Modrić receives
       ↓
Turn
       ↓
Pressure broken
```

### Against a Low Block

```text
Opponent sits deep
       ↓
Kroos controls possession
       ↓
Switches play
       ↓
Marcelo / Carvajal advance
```

### Against Counterattacks

```text
Madrid lose ball
       ↓
Casemiro
       ↓
Transition stopped
```

### When Madrid Needed Speed

```text
Modrić
   ↓
Ball progression
   ↓
BBC
```

### When Madrid Were Winning

```text
Kroos
   ↓
Keep possession
   ↓
Control tempo
   ↓
Reduce chaos
```

Madrid no longer needed every match to follow the same tactical script.

They could adapt.

---

# 9. Real Madrid Learned How to Survive

This is perhaps the most important characteristic of Zidane’s Champions League teams.

They did not dominate every minute of every match.

Sometimes Bayern were better.

Sometimes Juventus created problems.

Sometimes Atlético controlled spaces extremely well.

Sometimes Madrid were forced deep.

But Madrid developed an extraordinary ability to **survive difficult periods without losing control of the tie**.

The sequence often looked like this:

```text
Opponent dominates
        ↓
Madrid defend
        ↓
Casemiro protects
        ↓
Modrić escapes pressure
        ↓
Kroos restores control
        ↓
Opponent loses structure
        ↓
Madrid attack
        ↓
Cristiano Ronaldo
```

This is different from dominating possession for 90 minutes.

It is **game-state management**.

And it is incredibly valuable in knockout football.

---

# 10. Ronaldo Was Changing Too

The evolution of the midfield coincided with another major transformation.

Cristiano Ronaldo himself was changing.

The Mourinho-era Ronaldo was an extraordinary ball carrier.

He often received from wider positions and attacked defenders directly.

```text
Ronaldo
   ↓
Receive wide
   ↓
Dribble
   ↓
Sprint
   ↓
Shoot
```

By 2016–18, Ronaldo was increasingly becoming the ultimate penalty-area attacker.

Madrid no longer needed him to create every attack.

The midfield could build the attack for him.

```text
Kroos / Modrić
       ↓
Control midfield
       ↓
Marcelo / Carvajal
       ↓
Combination / Cross
       ↓
Ronaldo
       ↓
FINISH
```

This was tactically important.

Ronaldo could conserve energy.

Instead of constantly carrying the ball 30 or 40 metres, he increasingly focused on movements that decided matches.

His responsibility shifted from:

**Create + Carry + Finish**

toward:

**Move + Attack Space + Finish.**

That made an aging Ronaldo arguably even more dangerous in Champions League knockout football.

---

# 11. Benzema Was the Tactical Connector

Another player deserves special attention here: Karim Benzema.

The midfield controlled the deeper areas.

Ronaldo attacked the penalty area.

But someone still needed to connect them.

That was often Benzema.

He could drop away from the centre-backs:

```text
               Ronaldo
                  ↑
                  │
             Benzema
                 ↓
              drops

Kroos ───── Modrić ───── Marcelo
```

When Benzema moved toward midfield, defenders faced a difficult decision.

Follow him?

Then Ronaldo could attack the space behind.

Stay deep?

Then Benzema could receive between the lines.

This relationship became one of the hidden mechanisms behind Ronaldo’s extraordinary Champions League scoring.

---

# 12. The Full-Backs Became Playmakers

Marcelo and Carvajal were also fundamental.

Because Casemiro protected the middle and Kroos–Modrić controlled possession, the full-backs could attack extremely high.

```text
Marcelo ↑                     ↑ Carvajal
        │                     │
        │                     │
    Ronaldo                 Bale

          Kroos     Modrić
               \   /
              Casemiro
```

This stretched opposition defenses horizontally.

If opponents protected the center, Madrid attacked wide.

If they moved wide, spaces opened centrally.

If they defended deep, crosses arrived.

If they pushed forward, Ronaldo and Bale attacked behind them.

Again, the key theme was **multiple solutions**.

---

# 13. 2014 vs 2016–18: The Philosophical Difference

This is where the evolution becomes clearest.

### Ancelotti’s 2014 Midfield

**Xabi Alonso – Modrić – Di María**

The structure emphasized:

- transition
- acceleration
- ball carrying
- press resistance
- attacking balance

Conceptually:

```text
Xabi
  ↓
Modrić
  ↓
Di María
  ↓
ACCELERATE
  ↓
BBC
  ↓
EXPLOSION
```

The central question was:

> **How quickly can we turn possession into a devastating attack?**

---

### Zidane’s 2016–18 Midfield

**Casemiro – Kroos – Modrić**

The priorities changed.

```text
        Kroos
          ↕
Modrić ↔ Casemiro
          ↓
    Control Match
          ↓
   Manage Danger
          ↓
 Wait for the Moment
          ↓
       Ronaldo
```

The question became:

> **How do we manage the match until the decisive moment arrives?**

That is a more mature form of tournament football.

---

# 14. 2016: Atlético Madrid

The 2016 Champions League final demonstrated the importance of control and survival.

Atlético were compact, disciplined, aggressive, and extremely difficult to break down.

This was not a match Madrid could simply win through speed.

It required patience.

It required defensive concentration.

It required midfield discipline.

The match eventually went to penalties.

Madrid survived.

And won.

🏆 **Champions League 2016**

---

# 15. 2017: The Peak

The 2016–17 team may have been the most complete version of Zidane’s Madrid.

They could counterattack.

They could dominate possession.

They could cross.

They could attack centrally.

They could play with BBC.

They could use Isco as an additional midfielder.

They could change shape during matches.

And behind all of those variations remained:

**Casemiro – Kroos – Modrić.**

The Champions League final against Juventus demonstrated their quality.

After a competitive first half, Madrid increasingly took control.

The second half became a demonstration of Madrid’s ability to increase pressure while preventing Juventus from escaping.

Real Madrid won:

**4–1.**

🏆 **Champions League 2017**

---

# 16. 2018: Winning Without Always Being Better

The 2017–18 campaign perhaps demonstrated the trio’s greatest quality.

Madrid were not always the clearly superior team.

They suffered against Juventus.

They suffered against Bayern.

Liverpool presented enormous intensity in the final.

But Madrid repeatedly survived.

That is important.

Great knockout teams do not necessarily eliminate every difficult moment.

They know how to live through those moments.

```text
PRESSURE
   ↓
SURVIVE
   ↓
RECOVER STRUCTURE
   ↓
CONTROL
   ↓
WAIT
   ↓
OPPORTUNITY
   ↓
STRIKE
```

Madrid beat Liverpool 3–1 in Kyiv.

🏆 **Champions League 2018**

Three consecutive European Cups were complete.

---

# 17. Their Greatness Was Not About Dominating Every Midfield

This distinction matters.

It would be too simplistic to say:

> “Casemiro–Kroos–Modrić always dominated their opponents.”

They did not.

There were matches in which opposing midfields caused Madrid serious problems.

There were periods when Bayern controlled possession.

There were periods when Juventus pushed Madrid backward.

There were matches filled with chaos.

But this midfield gave Madrid something arguably more useful:

> **The ability to survive almost every type of football.**

High press?

They had Modrić.

Low block?

They had Kroos.

Counterattack?

They had Casemiro.

Physical battle?

Casemiro.

Possession battle?

Kroos.

Press resistance?

Modrić.

Need to slow the match?

Kroos.

Need to accelerate?

Modrić.

Need protection while Marcelo attacks?

Casemiro.

That versatility was extraordinary.

# 19. Speed → Balance → Control

The entire evolution can therefore be summarized in three words:

## **Speed → Balance → Control**

Mourinho gave Madrid speed.

His team could destroy opponents in seconds.

Ancelotti added balance.

Madrid could still counterattack, but Modrić and Di María gave the team more ways to progress through pressure.

Zidane added control.

Casemiro protected the structure.

Kroos controlled time and possession.

Modrić connected everything.

And Cristiano Ronaldo became the final weapon waiting at the end of the system.

```text
MOURINHO
2010–2013

SPEED
   ↓

ANCELOTTI
2013–2015

   ↓

   ↓
```

# Conclusion: The Perfect Midfield for Knockout Football

The greatness of **Casemiro–Kroos–Modrić** was not that all three players could do everything.

It was almost the opposite.

Each player possessed an elite quality that solved a different tactical problem.

**Casemiro protected.**

**Kroos controlled.**

**Modrić progressed.**

Together, they gave Real Madrid answers to almost every situation Champions League football could produce.

And behind them stood Ramos and the defense.

Beside them attacked Marcelo and Carvajal.

Ahead of them operated Benzema.

And waiting for the decisive moment was Cristiano Ronaldo.

That is why the transition from:

**Xabi Alonso–Modrić–Di María**

to:

**Casemiro–Kroos–Modrić**

was more than a change of personnel.

It represented a change in Real Madrid’s football philosophy.

The 2014 midfield was built to turn balance into explosive attack.

The 2016–18 midfield was built to **manage risk, survive pressure, control different game states, and strike when the opportunity appeared.**

Mourinho had restored Real Madrid’s speed and competitive edge.

Ancelotti transformed that foundation into balance and delivered *La Décima*.

Zidane inherited those ideas and developed perhaps the defining midfield of the Champions League era.

The result was historic:

**2016 — Champions League 🏆**

**2017 — Champions League 🏆**

**2018 — Champions League 🏆**

Three consecutive European Cups.

And at the center of that dynasty stood three players whose qualities fitted together almost perfectly:

# Casemiro. Kroos. Modrić.

**Protection. Control. Progression.**

That was the midfield architecture behind Real Madrid’s European dynasty.
