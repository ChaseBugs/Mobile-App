# SERIES 08 — The Evolution of the Goalkeeper

# EP02 — Buffon & Casillas vs Neuer & Ederson

## How Have the Demands on the Goalkeeper Changed?

In the summer of 2001, Juventus signed 23-year-old goalkeeper **Gianluigi Buffon** from Parma for around €52 million.

That record stood for 17 years.

Until Alisson and Kepa arrived in 2018, Buffon was the most expensive goalkeeper in history.

In the summer of 2017, Manchester City signed **Ederson** from Benfica.

Both goalkeepers commanded huge fees by the standards of their time.

But the two clubs paid for completely different things.

Juventus bought **a goalkeeper to protect the goal.**

Manchester City bought **a goalkeeper to start the game.**

Same position. Same No.1.

But in those 16 years, what the goalkeeper was asked to do changed completely.

# SAME POSITION, DIFFERENT JOB

---

# 1. The Environment of the 2000s Goalkeeper

First, we need to remember what kind of football the 2000s goalkeeper played.

Many teams used a 4-4-2.

Defensive lines were deeper than today.

Crosses came in constantly from wide areas.

And long-range shots were far more common.

```text
THE 2000s MATCH ENVIRONMENT

  WIDE CROSSES ────→  ┌─────────┐
                      │  BOX    │ ← AERIAL DUELS
  LONG SHOTS ─────→   │   GK    │ ← REFLEXES
                      └─────────┘
  GOAL KICK → LONG BALL
```

When a goalkeeper used his feet, it was mostly for:

**Kicking goal kicks long.**

**Clearing back-passes long.**

So the core qualities of the 2000s goalkeeper can be summarised as:

# REFLEXES + POSITIONING + AERIAL COMMAND

---

# 2. Buffon — Where You Stand Is the Save

Buffon's greatest weapon was not the spectacular dive.

It was **positioning.**

He was already in the best place before the shot was struck.

That is why so many of his saves looked easy.

```text
READ THE SHOOTING ANGLE
          ↓
TAKE THE OPTIMAL POSITION FIRST
          ↓
A SAVE THAT “LOOKS EASY”
```

At the 2006 World Cup in Germany, Buffon conceded two goals in seven matches.

One own goal. One Zidane penalty in the final.

And in extra time of that final, his fingertip save from Zidane's header is still regarded as one of the best saves of the tournament.

His consistency is written into the record books too.

In 2015-16, he set a Serie A record by going **974 minutes without conceding.**

He was 38 years old.

# POSITION BEFORE THE SAVE

---

# 3. Casillas — The Genius of Reflexes

Casillas was a different type of goalkeeper from Buffon.

He was not especially tall for a goalkeeper.

But his reaction speed was the best of his era.

The world first learned his name in the 2002 Champions League final.

Glasgow. Real Madrid vs Bayer Leverkusen.

With Real Madrid leading 2-1 in the second half, first-choice goalkeeper César went down injured.

The 20-year-old Casillas came on and protected the lead with a series of late saves against Leverkusen.

Then came the 2010 World Cup final.

Spain vs the Netherlands, 0-0.

Robben broke through behind the defence and found himself 1v1 with Casillas.

Casillas had already started to dive one way, but he kept the ball out with **the foot he had left behind.**

Spain won 1-0 in extra time through Iniesta.

```text
ROBBEN 1v1
     ↓
CASILLAS REACTS
     ↓
TRAILING-FOOT SAVE
     ↓
SPAIN'S FIRST WORLD CUP
```

# REACTION SPEED

---

# 4. Čech — The Perfect Goalkeeper Inside a System

Petr Čech was another model of the 2000s goalkeeper.

In 2004-05, Mourinho's Chelsea conceded only **15 goals** in 38 league games.

It is still the Premier League record for fewest goals conceded.

Čech kept **24 clean sheets** that season — another Premier League record — and at one point went **more than 1,000 minutes** without conceding.

(That run was later surpassed by Manchester United's Van der Sar, who reached 1,311 minutes in 2008-09.)

Then came the 2012 Champions League final in Munich.

Bayern vs Chelsea.

Čech saved Robben's extra-time penalty,

then stopped Olić and Schweinsteiger in the shootout to give Chelsea their first European Cup.

Čech's strength was **organisation.**

Organising the defensive line. Commanding the box. Not making mistakes.

# RELIABILITY

---

# 5. The Weakness of the 2000s Goalkeeper — Casillas and Crosses

But goalkeepers of that era had weaknesses too.

In Casillas' case, they were **dealing with crosses and judging when to leave his line.**

In December 2012, Mourinho dropped Casillas, Real Madrid's captain, to the bench.

The decision shook the whole of Spain.

When Casillas later injured his hand, Real Madrid signed Diego López from Sevilla, and López kept the No.1 shirt.

Then came the 2014 Champions League final in Lisbon.

Casillas misjudged Godín's header in front of his line, and Atlético Madrid took the lead.

Real Madrid were rescued by Ramos' stoppage-time header and went on to win 4-1 in extra time.

These moments reveal an important truth:

**Reflexes alone cannot solve everything.**

Judging when to leave the box and how to deal with aerial balls became more and more important as the years went on.

---

# 6. Neuer — Dominating Outside the Box

Neuer answered a completely different question:

**“How far can a goalkeeper defend?”**

If Buffon and Casillas dominated games inside the box,

Neuer dominated them **outside it.**

```text
BUFFON / CASILLAS         NEUER

  ┌──────┐                 ─────────── DEF LINE
  │  GK  │                     ↑
  └──────┘                  GK ZONE
                           ┌──────┐
                           │      │
                           └──────┘
```

Bayern's treble in 2013.

Germany's World Cup win and the Golden Glove in 2014.

Third place in the 2014 Ballon d'Or.

A goalkeeper right behind Ronaldo and Messi.

Neuer was a world-class shot-stopper too.

But what made him special was his **range.**

# RANGE

*(Neuer's revolution is covered in detail in EP03.)*

---

# 7. Ederson — The Goalkeeper Becomes a Playmaker

Ederson represented another direction of evolution.

His question was:

**“How much attacking play can a goalkeeper create?”**

At Guardiola's City, Ederson was effectively the first playmaker of the build-up.

He drew in the first line of pressure with short passes,

and when the press came, he went over it with a 70-metre pass.

City won the league with 100 points in Ederson's first season.

Over the following years he won six league titles with City and the 2023 treble.

# DISTRIBUTION

---

# 8. Comparing the Two Eras — What Changed?

Now let us compare the two eras directly.

```text
                     2000s              2020s
                  BUFFON·CASILLAS    NEUER·EDERSON
────────────────────────────────────────────────
SHOT-STOPPING        ★★★★★            ★★★★
POSITIONING          ★★★★★            ★★★★★
AERIAL COMMAND       ★★★★             ★★★★
SWEEPING             ★★               ★★★★★
SHORT BUILD-UP       ★★               ★★★★★
LONG PASSING         ★★★              ★★★★★
PRESS RESISTANCE     ★★               ★★★★
```

*(These ratings compare the general characteristics of each era's goalkeepers.)*

The biggest difference is not shot-stopping.

It is **the space and the time the goalkeeper is responsible for.**

The 2000s goalkeeper worked mainly **inside the box**, **when the opponent had the ball.**

The modern goalkeeper works **beyond the box**, and **when his own team has the ball too.**

# MORE SPACE + MORE PHASES

---

# 9. The Shooting Environment Changed Too

There is another important change.

**The type of shot** the goalkeeper faces has changed.

As data analysis developed, many teams reduced long-range shooting.

Instead, they try to get into the box and create better chances with cut-backs.

```text
2000s                     2020s
MANY LONG SHOTS           FEWER LONG SHOTS
MANY WIDE CROSSES         MORE CUT-BACKS
                          MORE RUNS IN BEHIND
```

As a result, the modern goalkeeper faces:

**fewer shots,**

but **more dangerous ones.**

And because of high defensive lines, **1v1 situations** happen more often.

So the ability to close the angle and stay on his feet until the last moment —

**1v1 defending** — has become even more important.

# FEWER SHOTS, BIGGER CHANCES

---

# 10. The Way the Goalkeeper Touches the Ball Has Changed

When the 2000s goalkeeper used his feet, it was mostly for goal kicks and clearances.

The modern goalkeeper uses his feet throughout the match.

```text
2000s GK FOOTWORK

GOAL KICK → LONG BALL
BACK-PASS → CLEARANCE

2020s GK FOOTWORK

GOAL KICK → SHORT PASS
BACK-PASS → SWITCH OF PLAY
INVITE PRESS → ESCAPE PASS
LONG PASS → FIND THE RUNNER
```

For the modern goalkeeper, the feet are no longer **a tool for avoiding danger.**

They are **a tool for creating opportunities.**

# FEET: SAFETY → OPPORTUNITY

---

# 11. Buffon Adapted to Modern Football

Here is an interesting fact.

Buffon was a 2000s goalkeeper, yet he survived for a long time inside modern football.

He led Juventus to the 2017 Champions League final,

played for Tuchel's PSG in 2018-19 at the age of 40,

and retired at Parma in 2023, aged 45.

Why could Buffon survive so long?

**Because positioning and composure never go out of date.**

Spectacular reflexes decline with age.

But the ability to read the right position actually improves with experience.

# INTELLIGENCE AGES WELL

---

# 12. So Could Casillas Have Played for Guardiola?

This is the question we asked at the end of EP01.

The honest answer is:

**Not as he was.**

In Guardiola's system, the goalkeeper touches the ball dozens of times a game with his feet,

and has to make accurate decisions under pressure.

With the footwork and sweeping range of the 2000s Casillas, meeting all of those demands would have been difficult.

But we can also look at it the other way.

**What if Casillas had been trained in modern football from childhood?**

His reflexes and 1v1 ability would still be world-class today.

And modern goalkeeper coaching would have taught him to use his feet.

In the end, the key to this comparison is:

# DIFFERENT ENVIRONMENT → DIFFERENT REQUIREMENTS

The goalkeepers of the past were not lacking.

**The football they lived in was different.**

---

# 13. What Has Not Changed

So what has stayed the same over 20 years?

**The save at the decisive moment.**

Buffon's save from Zidane's header in 2006.

Casillas' 1v1 save from Robben in 2010.

Čech's shootout in 2012.

Alisson's save from Milik in 2018.

These moments mean the same thing in any era.

**The goalkeeper is the only player who can change the fate of a season in a single moment.**

# THE BIG SAVE NEVER GOES OUT OF STYLE

---

# Conclusion — Same No.1, Different Mission

Buffon and Casillas perfected the art of protecting the goal.

Neuer and Ederson expanded the goalkeeper's territory.

```text
2000s                          2020s
PROTECT THE GOAL       →       PROTECT THE SPACE
STOP THE SHOT          →       START THE GAME
MASTER OF THE BOX      →       PARTICIPANT ACROSS THE PITCH
```

The modern goalkeeper is not a “better” goalkeeper than the goalkeepers of the past.

**He is a goalkeeper who has to do more.**

> **Buffon and Casillas protected the goal. Neuer and Ederson protected the game.**
