# SERIES 05 — The Evolution of the Winger

# EP04 — Cristiano Ronaldo and the Birth of the Goalscoring Winger

## How Did Ronaldo Change the Scoring Standard for Wingers?

Think about the early Cristiano Ronaldo.

Receive wide.

Face the defender.

Step-over.

Accelerate.

Dribble.

Attack again.

Young Ronaldo was a spectacular winger.

But as his career developed, his most dangerous actions began to look very different.

```text
Early Ronaldo

WIDE
 ↓
1v1
 ↓
DRIBBLE


Later Ronaldo

WIDE
 ↓
MOVE WITHOUT BALL
 ↓
BOX
 ↓
FINISH
```

He no longer needed to stay on the ball for long.

A few touches.

One movement.

One finish.

One of Ronaldo’s greatest evolutions was not simply that he scored more goals.

It was that **he reduced how much he needed to do with the ball in order to score**.

---

# 1. Early Manchester United — The Entertainer

Early Ronaldo was much closer to a classic wide dribbler.

```text
RONALDO ●        ● FB

       1v1
```

His weapons were obvious.

Step-over.

Change of Direction.

Acceleration.

Close Control.

He attacked defenders directly.

```text
Receive
   ↓
Face Defender
   ↓
Dribble
   ↓
Beat
   ↓
Create
```

At this stage, the important space was often **in front of the defender**.

That was where the duel began.

But over time, Ronaldo became increasingly interested in a different space.

Not in front of the defender.

**Behind him.**

---

# 2. The Biggest Change — From Ball to Space

Young Ronaldo often moved to receive the ball.

Mature Ronaldo increasingly moved to attack space.

That difference is fundamental.

```text
Young Ronaldo

BALL → RONALDO
          ↓
        ATTACK
```

Later, the sequence often became:

```text
Mature Ronaldo

RONALDO → SPACE
             ↓
           BALL
             ↓
           FINISH
```

Instead of receiving first and then deciding what to do, he started by finding the right space.

The ball came afterward.

His attacking question changed from:

**“What can I do when I receive?”**

to:

**“Where should I be when the ball arrives?”**

---

# 3. Off-Ball Movement Became a Major Weapon

Calling Ronaldo only an elite shooter misses part of the story.

Many of his goals were already being created before he touched the ball.

Imagine him starting on the left.

```text
RONALDO

LW ●

          ST
```

The ball moves to the opposite side.

The defenders naturally turn toward it.

```text
RONALDO ●

       CB      CB
                  → BALL
```

That is when Ronaldo moves.

```text
RONALDO
    ↘
       ↘
          FAR POST
```

The defender watches the ball.

Ronaldo watches the defender.

That difference creates the opportunity.

**Defender watches the ball.
Ronaldo attacks the blind side.**

---

# 4. Blind-Side Runs — Attack Where the Defender Cannot See

A defender cannot perfectly watch the ball and the attacker at the same time.

As the ball moves, brief blind-side moments appear.

```text
        BALL →

CB
 ↓

       RONALDO
          ↑
     Blind Side
```

Ronaldo became exceptional at attacking those moments.

If he moves directly in front of the defender, the defender can react.

But if he begins behind the defender’s shoulder:

```text
CB ● → watching ball

   RONALDO ●
       ↗
         ↗
          BOX
```

the defender must first locate him.

Then turn.

Then accelerate.

Those fractions of a second matter.

Ronaldo did not only beat defenders with speed.

He often **delayed the moment when the defender could react**.

---

# 5. Far-Post Arrival — When the Winger Becomes a Striker

One of Ronaldo’s signature movements was the far-post run.

Imagine the ball on the right.

```text
                    BALL ●

       CB      CB

RONALDO ●
```

The defensive line naturally shifts toward the ball.

```text
                    BALL ●
               ← DEFENCE SHIFT

       CB   CB

RONALDO ●
```

That creates space on the far side.

Ronaldo attacks it.

```text
                  CROSS
                    ↙

             CB

          RONALDO
             ↓
           FINISH
```

At this moment, he no longer looks like a traditional left winger.

Inside the box, he behaves like a **second striker**.

This creates an important distinction:

**Starting Position = Winger**

but

**Finishing Position = Striker**

---

# 6. His Most Dangerous Space Was No Longer the Touchline

Ronaldo could still start from the left.

```text
START

LW ●
```

But as the attack developed, his destination changed.

```text
FINISH

        ST
         ↓
     RONALDO
         ↓
        BOX
```

So even if the formation listed him as LW, his position at the decisive moment could look almost identical to that of a centre-forward.

This leads to an important tactical idea:

**Starting Position and Finishing Position are not the same thing.**

A player’s real role cannot always be understood from the formation graphic.

---

# 7. The Centre-Back Became a New Opponent

Young Ronaldo’s direct opponent was often the full-back.

```text
RONALDO vs FULL-BACK
```

But once he began attacking the box more frequently, the opponent changed.

```text
        CB     CB

     RONALDO
```

Now he had to compete with centre-backs.

That required a different skill set.

Timing.

Positioning.

Body Strength.

Heading.

Finishing.

Movement inside the box.

Ronaldo did not simply abandon winger skills.

He added **striker skills** on top of them.

```text
WINGER SKILLS
     +
STRIKER MOVEMENT
     +
FINISHING
     ↓
GOALSCORING WINGER
```

---

# 8. Heading — A New Scoring Route for a Winger

Heading became a major part of Ronaldo’s evolution.

Traditionally, we often imagine the winger delivering the cross.

Ronaldo increasingly became the winger **receiving it**.

```text
RIGHT SIDE
   ↓
CROSS
   ↓
             RONALDO
                ↑
              HEADER
```

At the far post, he could also create favourable matchups against full-backs.

```text
RONALDO
   VS
FULL-BACK
```

While the centre-backs were occupied centrally, Ronaldo could attack a smaller defender on the weak side.

So his aerial ability was not just an individual skill.

It could create a **Match-up Advantage**.

---

# 9. Goalscoring Winger Does Not Mean One Type of Goal

Ronaldo’s evolution was not simply:

Cut inside.

Shoot.

Score.

His scoring routes became much more varied.

```text
        RONALDO

   ↙       ↓       ↘

Cut Inside  Box Run  Far Post

   ↓         ↓        ↓

Shot      Finish    Header
```

There were also:

Rebounds.

Cut-back finishes.

Runs onto through balls.

Counterattacks.

Penalty-area combinations.

The key was not one signature move.

It was the ability to score through **multiple mechanisms**.

That variety helped make his output repeatable.

---

# 10. Less Touches, More Danger

Young Ronaldo often created danger through long sequences on the ball.

```text
Receive
 ↓
Touch
 ↓
Step-over
 ↓
Touch
 ↓
Dribble
 ↓
Attack
```

A more mature Ronaldo could make the action much shorter.

```text
Move
 ↓
Receive
 ↓
Shoot
```

Sometimes even:

```text
Move
 ↓
ONE TOUCH
 ↓
GOAL
```

Fewer touches do not mean less influence.

Sometimes they mean **more precise influence at the most valuable moment**.

Ronaldo reduced unnecessary actions and increased the value of the decisive action.

---

# 11. He Attacked Space Before It Became Obvious

Elite goalscorers do not only find open space.

They attack the space **before it fully opens**.

```text
Now

CB      CB
   NO SPACE
```

Then the ball moves.

The defence shifts.

```text
BALL →
      DEFENCE →
```

Ronaldo anticipates what comes next.

```text
Before Space Opens

RONALDO → → →

          FUTURE SPACE
```

This is one of the central skills of elite finishing.

**Attack the space before it becomes obvious.**

A good winger may use an open space.

A great goalscorer often arrives before everyone else realises that space is about to exist.

---

# 12. Ronaldo’s Evolution — From Dribbler to Space Hunter

His development can be simplified into three stages.

### Early Stage

```text
Receive Wide
    ↓
Face Defender
    ↓
Dribble
    ↓
Create
```

### Middle Stage

```text
Receive Wide
    ↓
Attack Inside
    ↓
Shoot / Create
```

### Goalscoring Stage

```text
Start Wide
    ↓
Move Without Ball
    ↓
Attack Box
    ↓
Finish
```

The important point is not that his dribbling disappeared.

It is that **he no longer needed to dribble in order to become dangerous**.

---

# 13. Ronaldo Changed the Questions We Ask About Wingers

For a traditional winger, one major question was:

**“Can he beat the full-back?”**

After Ronaldo, another question became increasingly important:

**“What does he do when the ball is on the opposite side?”**

```text
BALL → RIGHT

RONALDO → BOX
```

Does he stay wide?

Or attack the far post?

Does he deliver the cross?

Or become the player who finishes it?

Can he survive physically against centre-backs?

Can he produce striker-level numbers from a wide starting position?

The winger now carried a much greater **goalscoring responsibility**.

---

# 14. Ronaldo’s Real Legacy

Not every winger after Ronaldo became a Ronaldo-type player.

There are still playmaking wingers.

1v1 specialists.

Touchline wingers.

Wide creators.

But Ronaldo proved something important.

```text
WINGER
  +
ELITE FINISHER
  =
PRIMARY GOALSCORER
```

A team’s main scorer does not have to wear No.9 or start at centre-forward.

A player can begin wide and still become the team’s most important finisher.

That possibility became a major part of how we understand the modern wide forward.

---

# Conclusion — From Winger to Goalscorer

Cristiano Ronaldo’s evolution was not simply about improving his shooting.

He optimised the entire process of scoring.

From beating defenders in front of him...

to attacking the space behind them.

From moving toward the ball...

to moving toward where the ball would arrive.

From delivering crosses...

to finishing them.

```text
Dribbler
   ↓
Inside Threat
   ↓
Space Hunter
   ↓
Box Finisher
```

And through that evolution, he showed a new possibility for the winger.

**The winger could become the team’s primary goalscorer.**

Ronaldo did not just change the number of goals a winger could score.

He changed **how many goals we expected from the position**.

His evolution can be summarized in one line:

**Young Ronaldo attacked defenders.
Prime Ronaldo attacked space.**

And that shift was one of the biggest steps in the evolution of the goalscoring winger.

### Why Is the Line Between LW/RW and CF Disappearing in Modern Football?

Ronaldo added striker-level scoring to a winger’s starting position.

Modern football pushes that idea even further.

Is Mbappé a winger or a striker?

Is Salah a right winger or a forward?

If a player starts wide but repeatedly finishes attacks through the centre, what is his real position?

**Where do you start?**

is becoming less important than:

**Where do you actually attack?**
