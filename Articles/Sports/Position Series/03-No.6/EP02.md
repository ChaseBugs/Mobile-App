# SERIES 03 — The Evolution of the No.6

# EP02 — The Makélélé Role

## Why Did Claude Makélélé Create a Position Named After Himself?

Very few footballers have had an entire tactical role named after them.

Claude Makélélé did.

At some point, coaches, pundits and fans began saying:

**“He plays the Makélélé Role.”**

That is unusual.

Makélélé was not a classic playmaker.

He was not famous for goals.

He was not a spectacular dribbler.

He was not the player usually producing the final pass.

And yet when he disappeared from a team, the structure often looked less stable.

Why?

Because Makélélé's greatest value was not simply what he did with the ball.

It was what he allowed **everyone else to do**.

---

# 1. The Makélélé Role Was Not Just About Tackling

It is easy to describe Makélélé as a defensive midfielder.

But that is not enough.

His role was not simply:

```text
TACKLE
+
INTERCEPT
+
RUN
```

It was closer to:

```text
PROTECT
+
BALANCE
+
COVER
+
RECOVER
+
CONNECT
```

He operated in front of the defensive line.

But his responsibility was larger than marking one opponent.

His real job was:

**Keep the structure alive.**

---

# 2. His Most Important Territory — The Centre

Makélélé usually protected the central zone in front of the centre-backs.

```text
        Opponent AM

             ●

       MAKÉLÉLÉ

LB      CB      CB      RB
```

This is one of the most dangerous areas on the pitch.

A No.10 can receive there.

A striker can drop there.

A winger can move inside.

Second balls often land there.

Counterattacks often begin there.

So Makélélé's first principle was simple:

**Protect the Centre.**

He was not defending space randomly.

He was defending the space that could break the team.

---

# 3. He Did Not Simply Chase the Ball

One of the biggest differences between an average defensive midfielder and an elite one is discipline.

When the ball moves wide, many players instinctively follow it.

Makélélé often asked a different question.

**“If I leave this space, who protects the centre?”**

```text
BALL → LEFT

        CENTRAL SPACE
              ↓
          MAKÉLÉLÉ
```

He did not only watch the ball.

He watched the space created **because the ball moved**.

That distinction is fundamental.

Danger does not always exist where the ball is.

Sometimes the real danger is the space left behind.

---

# 4. His Defending Started With Positioning

Makélélé is remembered as a strong defensive player.

But his best actions were not always sliding tackles.

Often the best action happened before the tackle was even necessary.

```text
Opponent
   ↓

Passing Lane
    X

Makélélé
```

He blocked central routes.

He forced opponents away from dangerous areas.

He reduced the space available to the next attacker.

His sequence was often:

```text
POSITION
   ↓
BLOCK LANE
   ↓
FORCE DECISION
   ↓
INTERCEPT / TACKLE
```

A tackle can repair a problem.

Good positioning can stop the problem from appearing.

That was one of Makélélé's greatest strengths.

---

# 5. He Bought Freedom for Other Players

This is where his role becomes much more interesting.

If Makélélé stayed behind the attack, other players could move forward with more confidence.

```text
        AM      AM

LW                     RW

         MAKÉLÉLÉ
```

An attacking midfielder could make a late run.

A full-back could overlap.

A winger could move inside.

A central midfielder could attack the box.

Why?

Because somebody remained behind the attack.

Makélélé did not use freedom for himself.

He gave freedom to others.

---

# 6. Freedom Has a Cost

Every attacking movement creates defensive risk.

A full-back goes forward.

Space opens behind him.

A midfielder attacks the box.

The central zone becomes thinner.

A winger moves inside.

The shape changes.

```text
PLAYER MOVES FORWARD
        ↓
      SPACE
        ↓
      RISK
```

Someone has to pay for that risk.

Makélélé did.

```text
ATTACKING FREEDOM
        ↑
     MAKÉLÉLÉ
        ↓
DEFENSIVE SECURITY
```

That is the core of the Makélélé Role:

**Freedom for others, responsibility for himself.**

---

# 7. He Controlled the Moment After Losing the Ball

Makélélé's value became especially visible in transition.

Imagine his team attacking with several players ahead of the ball.

```text
        ST

LW      AM      RW

      CM     CM

        MAKÉLÉLÉ
```

Then possession is lost.

The opponent tries to counter.

The first question becomes:

**Who stops the first forward action?**

Makélélé.

```text
BALL LOST
   ↓
COUNTER
   ↓
MAKÉLÉLÉ
   ↓
DELAY / STOP / RECOVER
```

He did not always need to win the ball immediately.

Sometimes slowing the attack was enough.

Two seconds.

Three seconds.

That gave teammates time to recover their positions.

This is **Transition Control**.

---

# 8. Defending Is Not Always About Winning the Ball

A defensive midfielder cannot tackle every counterattack.

Sometimes the opponent has a numerical advantage.

```text
3 ATTACKERS
     ↓

  MAKÉLÉLÉ
```

If he dives in, he may be eliminated.

So another solution is needed.

Slow the attack.

Block the central route.

Force the ball wide.

Delay the next pass.

```text
COUNTER
   ↓
SLOW
   ↓
FORCE WIDE
   ↓
TEAM RECOVERS
```

A great No.6 does not always need to:

**Win the Ball.**

Sometimes he needs to:

**Buy Time.**

---

# 9. He Protected the Centre-Backs

Centre-backs frequently need to step forward.

For example, a striker drops into midfield.

```text
       ST
       ↓

       CB
```

If the centre-back follows him, space opens behind.

That is where the defensive midfielder becomes important.

```text
       ST
       ↓

      CB ↑

   MAKÉLÉLÉ
       ↓
     COVER
```

Makélélé's positioning allowed defenders to be more aggressive.

They could step out.

They could engage.

They could press higher.

Because someone behind them understood the structure.

---

# 10. He Also Protected Advancing Full-Backs

When a full-back attacks, someone needs to manage the space he leaves.

```text
LB ↑↑↑

       SPACE

    MAKÉLÉLÉ
```

Makélélé shifted according to the attacking direction.

If the team attacked left, he could protect that side.

If the team attacked right, he adjusted again.

```text
ATTACK LEFT
   ↓
Makélélé shifts left

ATTACK RIGHT
   ↓
Makélélé shifts right
```

This did not mean abandoning the centre.

It meant constantly adjusting the defensive structure around the team's attacking movement.

His job was not simply:

**Stay in one place.**

It was:

**Protect the shape.**

---

# 11. The Makélélé Role Was an Anchor Role

Modern attacking structures are full of movement.

Wingers move inside.

Full-backs go forward.

Midfielders rotate.

Centre-backs spread wider.

One player often needs to stabilise everything.

```text
      MOVEMENT
   ↗     ↑     ↖

      MAKÉLÉLÉ

   ↘     ↓     ↙
      MOVEMENT
```

That is why Makélélé is often understood as an **Anchor**.

The team moves around him.

He prevents the structure from drifting too far out of balance.

---

# 12. With the Ball — Simple Did Not Mean Limited

Makélélé was not known as a creative passing maestro.

But that does not mean he was poor with the ball.

His priorities were simply different.

```text
WIN BALL
  ↓
SECURE
  ↓
FIND FREE PLAYER
  ↓
RESET STRUCTURE
```

Avoid unnecessary risk.

Use one or two touches.

Give the ball to the player best positioned to create.

His job was not:

**Be the Creator.**

His job was:

**Enable the Creator.**

That distinction explains a lot about his football.

---

# 13. Why Simple Passing Was So Important

The moment after winning the ball can be dangerous.

The team is often still spread out.

Players have not yet reset.

One bad decision can immediately create another transition.

Makélélé reduced that risk.

```text
RECOVER
  ↓
SIMPLE PASS
  ↓
TEAM SPREADS
  ↓
ATTACK AGAIN
```

His simplicity was not passive.

It was intelligent game management.

He removed unnecessary chaos.

---

# 14. His Value Was Hard to See in Traditional Statistics

Players like Makélélé can be difficult to evaluate with attacking numbers.

Goals?

Few.

Assists?

Not many.

Key passes?

Not his main responsibility.

But his real questions were different.

**How many attacks never became dangerous?**

**How many transitions were slowed?**

**How many passing lanes were blocked?**

**How many teammates could move forward because he stayed behind?**

This is why his influence often looked invisible.

He represented a type of footballing value that traditional numbers struggled to show.

**Invisible Value.**

---

# 15. Real Madrid — His Importance Became Clearer After He Left

One of the strongest ways to understand Makélélé's value is to look at Real Madrid.

In 2002/03, Real Madrid won La Liga.

The team contained extraordinary attacking talent.

Zidane.

Figo.

Ronaldo.

Raúl.

Roberto Carlos.

And behind them:

Makélélé.

```text
        RONALDO

FIGO              RAÚL

        ZIDANE

      MAKÉLÉLÉ

       DEFENCE
```

His role was completely different from the stars around him.

Zidane moved forward.

Roberto Carlos attacked aggressively.

Figo created.

Ronaldo stayed high.

Makélélé helped manage what they left behind.

He was not the most glamorous Galáctico.

He helped make the Galácticos possible.

---

# 16. 2003 — Beckham Arrived, Makélélé Left

In the summer of 2003, Real Madrid signed David Beckham.

Makélélé left for Chelsea.

This moment became symbolic.

```text
MORE STAR POWER
      ↑

BECKHAM IN


MAKÉLÉLÉ OUT

      ↓
LESS SPECIALIST BALANCE
```

This does **not** mean Beckham was the problem.

That would be too simplistic.

Beckham and Makélélé solved completely different tactical problems.

Beckham added elite passing, crossing and technical quality.

Makélélé provided balance, cover and transition protection.

The important issue was not:

**Beckham vs Makélélé.**

It was:

**What happens when attacking quality increases, but structural protection decreases?**

---

# 17. At First, Real Madrid Still Looked Strong

The story is more interesting because Madrid did not immediately collapse.

For much of the 2003/04 season, they still looked powerful.

By March, they remained alive in the major competitions.

```text
MARCH 2004

LA LIGA       → ALIVE
CHAMPIONS     → ALIVE
COPA DEL REY  → FINAL

       TREBLE?
```

The attacking quality was still enormous.

That can hide structural weakness.

When a team is winning, balance problems are often difficult to see.

They become visible when momentum changes.

---

# 18. Then the Season Unravelled

Real Madrid lost the Copa del Rey final to Real Zaragoza.

They were then eliminated by Monaco in the Champions League quarter-final despite having held a strong advantage in the tie.

Their league form also deteriorated badly.

The team eventually finished fourth in La Liga.

```text
TREBLE DREAM
     ↓

COPA FINAL LOSS
     ↓

CHAMPIONS LEAGUE EXIT
     ↓

LEAGUE LEAD LOST
     ↓

4TH PLACE
```

One year after winning the league, Madrid finished the next season without a major trophy.

That contrast made Makélélé's departure look even more important.

But the explanation needs nuance.

---

# 19. Real Madrid Did Not Collapse Because of One Player

It would be inaccurate to say:

```text
MAKÉLÉLÉ LEFT
      ↓
REAL MADRID COLLAPSED
```

Football is more complicated.

There were several major changes around the club.

Vicente del Bosque left.

Fernando Hierro also departed.

Carlos Queiroz became manager.

Squad depth became a concern.

The team also suffered physically and structurally late in the season.

So the more accurate explanation is:

```text
LESS BALANCE
     +
STRUCTURAL CHANGE
     +
LESS DEPTH
     +
FATIGUE
     ↓
GREATER FRAGILITY
```

Makélélé's departure was not the only cause.

But it became a powerful symbol of something deeper:

**elite attacking talent still needs protection.**

---

# 20. What Madrid Lost Was Bigger Than Tackles

When Makélélé left, Madrid did not simply lose defensive actions.

They lost several structural functions.

**Central Protection**

**Transition Control**

**Cover**

**Positional Discipline**

And perhaps most importantly:

**Freedom Insurance**

```text
ZIDANE → CREATE

ROBERTO CARLOS → ATTACK

FIGO → CREATE

RONALDO → STAY HIGH


        ↓


Someone must protect
what they leave behind.


        ↓


     MAKÉLÉLÉ
```

That was his tactical value.

He protected the risks created by other players.

---

# 21. What Real Madrid Lost, Chelsea Gained

Makélélé moved to Chelsea in 2003.

Then José Mourinho arrived in 2004.

Mourinho did not need to reinvent Makélélé.

He used his strengths as a central part of the structure.

```text
          DROGBA

DUFF                    ROBBEN

         LAMPARD

       MAKÉLÉLÉ

       BACK FOUR
```

Makélélé protected the centre.

That gave the attacking players more freedom.

The concept was simple:

**Protect the Risk.**

---

# 22. Mourinho Understood Risk Distribution

Mourinho's Chelsea was not strong simply because it had defensive players.

The team was strong because responsibility was clearly distributed.

Some players attacked.

Others protected the consequences.

```text
LAMPARD → FORWARD
ROBBEN  → ATTACK
DUFF    → ATTACK
FB      → SUPPORT

          ↓

      MAKÉLÉLÉ

          ↓

     PROTECT RISK
```

Makélélé's instructions did not need to be complicated.

Protect the centre.

Recover second balls.

Stop transitions.

Stay disciplined.

Move the ball quickly.

Then return to position.

His simplicity gave the rest of the team clarity.

---

# 23. Lampard's Freedom Was Partly Built on Makélélé

Frank Lampard is a perfect example.

Lampard could attack the box aggressively from midfield.

```text
        DROGBA

          ↑
       LAMPARD
          ↑

       MAKÉLÉLÉ
```

Every time Lampard moved forward, space opened behind him.

Makélélé protected that space.

So Lampard could think more about:

**Where can I attack?**

and less about:

**Who covers me if I go?**

The answer was already there.

Makélélé.

This is one of the clearest examples of how a defensive midfielder can increase another player's attacking freedom.

---

# 24. 2004/05 — The Structure Became Historic

In Mourinho's first Premier League season, Chelsea produced one of the strongest defensive campaigns in English football history.

They won the title with:

**95 points**

**29 wins**

**only one league defeat**

and just:

**15 goals conceded**

They also recorded an extraordinary number of clean sheets.

That achievement belonged to the entire defensive structure.

Petr Čech.

John Terry.

Ricardo Carvalho.

William Gallas.

Paulo Ferreira.

Mourinho's organisation.

And, in front of them, Makélélé.

```text
        OPPONENT

            ↓

        MAKÉLÉLÉ

            ↓

 GALLAS  TERRY  CARVALHO  FERREIRA

            ↓

           ČECH
```

Makélélé was not solely responsible for those numbers.

But he was a key part of the system that produced them.

---

# 25. Mourinho Used Makélélé as More Than a Destroyer

At Chelsea, Makélélé's role was not simply to tackle.

It had a rhythm.

```text
RECOVER
   ↓
PASS
   ↓
SUPPORT
   ↓
RESET POSITION
```

Win possession.

Move it quickly.

Allow the next player to create.

Return to the balance point.

His football intelligence was visible not only in what he did.

It was visible in what he **did not try to do**.

He did not force unnecessary passes.

He did not leave his position without reason.

He did not compete with Lampard or the wingers for attacking influence.

He understood his function.

That made the whole structure more efficient.

---

# 26. His Importance Eventually Changed How Opponents Defended Chelsea

One of the clearest signs of Makélélé's influence was that opponents increasingly recognised him as an important part of Chelsea's circulation.

Why mark a defensive midfielder?

Not because he scores.

Because he connects the phases.

```text
DEFENCE
   ↓
MAKÉLÉLÉ
   ↓
MIDFIELD
   ↓
ATTACK
```

If the opponent could stop that first connection, Chelsea's progression became harder.

This showed how far the role had evolved.

Makélélé was not only destroying attacks.

He was also part of the mechanism that restarted Chelsea's own attacks.

---

# 27. Chelsea Won Again

Chelsea followed the 2004/05 title by winning the Premier League again in 2005/06.

Back-to-back champions.

```text
2004/05
PREMIER LEAGUE
      ↓
   CHAMPION


2005/06
PREMIER LEAGUE
      ↓
   CHAMPION
```

Makélélé remained an important part of the structure.

And by then, his role had become so recognisable that people increasingly used his name as tactical shorthand.

That is a remarkable transformation.

A player once criticised for not contributing enough offensively became the reference point for an entire midfield role.

---

# 28. Two Clubs Revealed His Real Value

Makélélé's story becomes especially powerful when we compare Real Madrid and Chelsea.

```text
REAL MADRID

     ↓
Balance became harder

CHELSEA

     ↓

```

It would be simplistic to say:

**Madrid lost because Makélélé left.**

Or:

**Chelsea won because Makélélé arrived.**

Football is never that simple.

But the tactical lesson is clear.

Real Madrid's experience showed how difficult it can be to replace invisible structural work.

Chelsea showed what happens when that same work is treated as a central tactical asset.

---

# 29. Why Did His Name Become the Role?

Makélélé was not the first defensive midfielder in football history.

So why did the role take his name?

Because he made the function extremely clear.

```text
STAY DISCIPLINED
      ↓
PROTECT CENTRE
      ↓
COVER TEAMMATES
      ↓
STOP TRANSITIONS
      ↓
SECURE POSSESSION
      ↓
ALLOW OTHERS TO ATTACK
```

People no longer needed a long description.

They could simply say:

**“The Makélélé Role.”**

And everyone understood the basic idea.

---

# 30. The Core Skills of the Makélélé Role

Makélélé should not be reduced to tackling.

His real skill set included:

### Positioning

Occupy dangerous space before the opponent can use it.

### Anticipation

Read where the next action will happen.

### Spatial Discipline

Do not abandon the centre simply because the ball moves.

### Covering

Protect the space left by teammates.

### Interception

Stop passes before they become dangerous.

### Transition Defence

Delay or break the opponent's first counterattacking action.

### Simple Distribution

Secure possession and connect quickly.

### Tactical Discipline

Prioritise team structure over personal freedom.

His greatest skill was not one technical action.

It was:

**Being where the team needed him most.**

---

# 31. His Real Playmaking Was Freedom

Makélélé was not a traditional playmaker.

He did not dominate matches with through balls.

But in a broader tactical sense, he still helped create attacks.

Not by producing the chance himself.

By creating the conditions in which others could take risks.

```text
MAKÉLÉLÉ
   ↓
SECURITY
   ↓
FREEDOM
   ↓
MORE ATTACKING OPTIONS
```

This is another kind of playmaking.

Not playmaking through the final pass.

**Playmaking through structure.**

---

# Conclusion — The Player Who Made Freedom Possible

Makélélé's greatness was not built on glamour.

It was built on balance.

He protected the centre.

He controlled transitions.

He covered advancing teammates.

He protected centre-backs.

He helped stabilise possession.

And above all:

**He made other players freer.**

At Real Madrid, his departure became a symbol of how easy it is to underestimate balance.

At Chelsea, Mourinho turned that balance into a tactical weapon.

```text
MAKÉLÉLÉ
   ↓
BALANCE
   ↓
SECURITY
   ↓
FREEDOM FOR OTHERS
```

That is why his name became more than the name of a player.

It became the name of a function.

**The Makélélé Role was not about doing everything.
It was about making everything else possible.**

Makélélé did not need to dominate every phase of the game.

He needed to make sure the team could.

And that was enough to redefine the No.6.

# NEXT — EP03

## The Busquets Revolution

### How Did Sergio Busquets Change the Definition of the No.6?

Makélélé showed that a No.6 could protect the freedom of an entire team.

Busquets introduced a different idea.

What if the No.6 did not only protect the team **after danger appeared**?

What if he could manipulate pressure before the danger even existed?

Makélélé's question was:

**“How do I protect the team?”**

Busquets would push the role toward another question:

**“How do I control the opponent?”**

That is where the next revolution begins.
