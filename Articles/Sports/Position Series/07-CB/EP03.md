# SERIES 07 — The Evolution of the Centre-Back

# EP03 — The Rise of the Ball-Playing Defender

## When Did the Centre-Back Become the Starting Point of Build-Up?

**Ball-Playing Defender.**

Today, the term feels completely normal.

But technically gifted defenders are not a modern invention.

Franz Beckenbauer could carry and create from deep.

Ronald Koeman possessed extraordinary distribution.

Later came defenders such as Gerard Piqué, Sergio Ramos and Leonardo Bonucci.

Today, players such as John Stones can even move from centre-back into midfield during possession.

So the important question is not:

**“Who was the first Ball-Playing Defender?”**

The better question is:

# WHEN DID BUILD-UP BECOME A CORE RESPONSIBILITY OF THE CENTRE-BACK?

That is the real evolution.

---

# 1. What Is a Ball-Playing Defender?

It is not simply a centre-back with a high Pass Accuracy.

A true Ball-Playing Defender uses possession to change the opponent's defensive structure.

```text
RECEIVE
   ↓
READ
   ↓
ATTRACT / CARRY / PASS
   ↓
BREAK STRUCTURE
   ↓
PROGRESS
```

The key concept is not simply:

**Passing Technique**

It is:

# PROGRESSION

The objective is to move the team into a better attacking situation.

---

# 2. The Roots — The Libero

To understand the Ball-Playing Defender, we have to look beyond the modern centre-back.

One of its clearest historical roots is the:

**Libero.**

And the most famous example is Franz Beckenbauer.

```text
       ST

    STOPPER

      LIBERO
        ↓
     RECEIVE
        ↓
     ADVANCE
```

A traditional stopper confronted the attacker.

The libero could operate behind him, read the game, recover possession and then move forward.

Beckenbauer could:

**Defend.**

**Carry.**

**Pass.**

**Advance.**

He demonstrated an important possibility:

# DEFEND → CREATE

A defender did not have to disappear from the attack once possession was recovered.

---

# 3. Koeman — A Defender with a Playmaker's Pass

Ronald Koeman represented another direction.

His major weapon was:

# DISTRIBUTION

From a deep defensive position, he could change the direction of an attack or find advanced teammates directly.

```text
KOEMAN
   \
    \
     \ LONG PASS
      \
       FORWARD
```

Instead of requiring several passes through midfield, one delivery could connect:

# DEFENCE → ATTACK

The defender's pass was no longer simply a method of escaping danger.

It could become an attacking weapon.

---

# 4. A Technical CB and a System Built Around CBs Are Different Things

This distinction is essential.

Technically excellent defenders existed in previous generations.

But not every tactical system depended on them.

A traditional progression might look like:

```text
CB
 ↓
CM
 ↓
PLAYMAKER
 ↓
ATTACK
```

The centre-back delivered the ball to midfield.

Then the playmaker took control.

Modern pressing changed that relationship.

```text
        PRESS

     ST      ST
      ↓      ↓

     CB      CB
       \    /
        No.6
```

Opponents increasingly try to stop the ball **before it reaches the midfielder.**

Now simply getting the ball to the playmaker becomes a tactical problem.

The solution?

Move part of the playmaking responsibility deeper.

# PLAYMAKING MOVED BACKWARD

---

# 5. Piqué — The CB Inside the Possession System

Gerard Piqué represents an important stage in this development.

His job was not simply:

**Win Ball → Give Ball to Midfielder**

He was part of the possession structure itself.

```text
      No.8       No.8

          No.6

     CB         PIQUÉ
```

Circulate possession.

Recognise the press.

Find the forward pass.

Change the direction of the build-up.

The centre-back was becoming more than:

**a defender who could pass.**

He was becoming:

# A DEFENDER INSIDE THE POSSESSION SYSTEM

That is a much bigger tactical change.

---

# 6. Ramos — Progress Through Carrying

Passing is not the only way a centre-back can progress the ball.

Sergio Ramos demonstrated another weapon:

# CARRYING

If the opponent refused to press him, he could advance.

```text
RAMOS
  ↑
  ↑ CARRY
  ↑

MIDFIELD LINE
```

Eventually, someone had to react.

If an opposition midfielder stepped forward:

```text
MID → RAMOS

      SPACE
        ↓
    TEAMMATE
```

another player could become free.

This creates an important principle:

# PASS OR CARRY

Both can manipulate the defensive structure.

The purpose is the same:

**force an opponent to make a decision.**

---

# 7. Bonucci — The Quarterback Centre-Back

Leonardo Bonucci showed another version of the Ball-Playing Defender.

His signature weapon was:

**Long Distribution.**

When the opponent compressed midfield:

```text
       O O O
      MIDFIELD

BONUCCI
    \
     \
      \ LONG PASS
       \
       FORWARD
```

he could bypass several lines with one action.

This creates a dilemma for the press.

Press high?

**Space appears behind.**

Drop deeper?

**Short build-up becomes easier.**

Bonucci demonstrated how a centre-back could function almost like a:

# DEEP PLAYMAKER

from the defensive line.

---

# 8. There Is No Single Ball-Playing CB

This is important.

Ball-playing defenders do not all solve the game in the same way.

| Player      | Main Method               | Function             |
| ----------- | ------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Beckenbauer | Carrying + Creation       | Libero               |
| Koeman      | Distribution              | Distributor          |
| Piqué       | Circulation + Connection  | Possession CB        |
| Ramos       | Carrying + Progression    | Progressor           |
| Bonucci     | Long Passing              | Deep Playmaker       |
| Van Dijk    | Distribution + Security   | Controller           |
| Stones      | Positional Transformation | CB/Midfielder Hybrid |

Their styles are different.

Their common quality is simple:

# THEY CAN CREATE A PROBLEM WITH THE BALL.

---

# 9. Van Dijk — Progression with Security

Modern football adds another challenge.

A centre-back may be excellent in possession, but his team still needs protection behind the attack.

This is where a player such as Virgil van Dijk represents another development.

```text
ATTACK
   ↑
MIDFIELD
   ↑
VAN DIJK
   ↓
SPACE BEHIND
```

He can distribute from deep while remaining the defensive reference behind possession.

This introduces another important combination:

# PROGRESSION + SECURITY

The modern Ball-Playing Defender cannot think only about the next pass.

He must also understand what happens if possession is lost.

---

# 10. Stones — When the Centre-Back Becomes a Midfielder

John Stones takes the concept even further.

A traditional defensive structure might begin with:

```text
CB          CB
```

But in possession, Stones can move forward into midfield.

```text
      No.8       No.8

     No.6      STONES

        CB      CB
```

On the team sheet:

**Centre-Back.**

During possession:

**Midfielder.**

This represents an important evolution.

Earlier Ball-Playing Defenders created from the defensive line.

Now the defender can:

# ENTER THE NEXT LINE

The evolution becomes:

**Pass into Midfield**

↓

**Carry into Midfield**

↓

# BECOME PART OF MIDFIELD

---

# 11. Position Can Become a Playmaking Tool

When a CB moves into midfield, the opponent must react.

```text
        ST

CB → STONES ↑
        ↓
     MIDFIELD
```

Who follows him?

If the striker follows, another defender may become free.

If a midfielder steps forward, space can open behind him.

If nobody reacts, Stones can receive freely.

This is a major development in defensive playmaking.

The modern CB can manipulate the opponent not only with:

**Passing**

or

**Carrying**

but also with:

# POSITION

The defender's movement itself can change the structure.

---

# 12. The Goalkeeper Changed the CB Too

The rise of the Ball-Playing Defender cannot be separated from the evolution of the goalkeeper.

When the goalkeeper participates in build-up:

```text
       CB       CB

           GK
```

the team gains another passing option behind the press.

Against two forwards:

```text
       ST       ST
        ↓       ↓

      CB         CB
           GK
```

the goalkeeper can help create numerical superiority.

This means the modern CB does not operate alone.

He increasingly works inside a build-up unit:

# GK + CB + No.6

The first phase of possession has become a coordinated tactical structure.

---

# 13. Why Do Coaches Want Ball-Playing Centre-Backs?

Because every additional player who can create from deep gives the team another solution.

The older division of responsibility could often be simplified as:

```text
DEFENDERS
    ↓
MIDFIELDERS
    ↓
CREATE
```

Modern possession football is more connected.

```text
GK
 ↓
CB
 ↓
No.6
 ↓
No.8
 ↓
FORWARD
```

Every line can contribute to progression.

If the opponent blocks one route, another player can create the solution.

# MORE PLAYMAKERS → MORE SOLUTIONS

This is why build-up ability became so valuable at centre-back.

---

# 14. The Biggest Misunderstanding About Ball-Playing Defenders

A difficult pass is not automatically a good pass.

Ball-playing ability should not mean:

**Take maximum risk.**

Sometimes the best decision is to play forward.

Sometimes it is to carry.

Sometimes it is to switch.

And sometimes:

**the sideways pass is the correct pass.**

The real principle is:

# CONTROLLED PROGRESSION

Progress when the advantage exists.

Keep possession when it does not.

Move the opponent.

Then attack the next opening.

---

# 15. How to Watch a Ball-Playing Centre-Back

Do not focus only on Pass Accuracy.

Watch these qualities instead.

**First Line:** Can he beat the opponent's first press?

**Passing Range:** Can he use short, vertical, diagonal and long passes?

**Carrying:** Does he advance when nobody presses him?

**Press Manipulation:** Can he attract an opponent before releasing the ball?

**Positioning:** Does his movement improve the team's build-up structure?

**Risk Selection:** Does he know when to progress and when to recycle?

The complete concept is:

# TECHNIQUE + TACTICAL PURPOSE

---

The development can be simplified like this:

**Beckenbauer**

↓

**Piqué**

↓

**Ramos**

Defender → Progressor

↓

**Bonucci**

Defender → Deep Playmaker

↓

**Van Dijk**

Progression + Security

↓

**Stones**

Defender → Midfielder

This is not a ranking.

It represents different answers to the same evolving question:

> **How much influence can a centre-back have with the ball?**

---

# 17. From Ball-Playing Defender to Playmaking Defender

The original question was:

**“Can the defender play with the ball?”**

The modern question is more demanding:

# “CAN THE DEFENDER CREATE AN ADVANTAGE?”

There is an important difference.

Basic ball-playing:

```text
CB → SAFE PASS
```

Playmaking from defence:

```text
CB
 ↓
ATTRACT
 ↓
BREAK LINE
 ↓
FREE PLAYER
 ↓
ADVANTAGE
```

The objective is no longer simply:

# PLAY THE BALL

It is:

# CHANGE THE STRUCTURE WITH THE BALL

That is the real meaning of the modern Ball-Playing Defender.

---

# Conclusion — Playmaking Moved Backward

The Ball-Playing Defender did not suddenly appear in modern football.

Beckenbauer showed that a defender could carry and create.

Koeman showed the power of distribution.

Piqué became part of the possession structure.

Ramos added aggressive progression.

Bonucci operated like a deep distributor.

Van Dijk combined progression with defensive security.

Stones pushed the idea even further by moving directly into midfield.

The real evolution was not simply that:

**centre-backs became better passers.**

Something much bigger happened.

# PLAYMAKING MOVED BACKWARD.

There was once a clearer expectation that the defender would find the midfielder, and the midfielder would create.

Now the creative chain can begin with:

**Goalkeeper → Centre-Back → Midfielder → Forward**

The centre-back is no longer simply responsible for delivering the ball to the playmaker.

**He can be the first playmaker himself.**

> **He does not simply pass from the back.
> He creates the first advantage from the back.**
