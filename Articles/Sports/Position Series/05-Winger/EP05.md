# SERIES 05 — The Evolution of the Winger

# EP05 — Winger or Striker?

## Why Is the Line Between LW/RW and CF Disappearing in Modern Football?

Is Kylian Mbappé a winger or a striker?

Is Mohamed Salah a right winger or a goalscorer?

Are Lamine Yamal and Michael Olise wingers or playmakers?

And where exactly should we place Ousmane Dembélé or Désiré Doué?

Modern football is becoming increasingly difficult to explain with three simple labels:

**LW / CF / RW**

A formation may still show this:

```text
LW              CF              RW
```

But that only tells us where the players begin.

Once the ball starts moving, the picture changes.

```text
START
  ↓
RECEIVE
  ↓
MOVE
  ↓
CREATE / PENETRATE
  ↓
ARRIVE
  ↓
FINISH
```

To understand the modern winger, we need to move beyond **Position**.

We need to understand **Function**.

---

# 1. Position Is a Starting Reference, Not an Address

Modern wingers do not have to remain on the touchline.

During one attack:

```text
LW → Half-Space

CF → Drop

RW → Centre

LB → Width
```

One player leaves a space.

Another occupies it.

The structure survives even though the players move.

So the important question is no longer simply:

**“Who is the winger?”**

A better question is:

**“Who is performing the wide function right now?”**

This is also why today's elite wingers can look completely different from one another.

They may start in similar positions.

But they solve different tactical problems.

---

# 2. Mbappé — The Space Hunter

### Core Style

**Wide Start → Attack Behind → Central Finish**

Mbappé's greatest weapon is not simply top speed.

It is his ability to identify **when space is about to become available** and attack it explosively.

```text
MBAPPÉ ●

             CB
              ↓

           SPACE
              ↘
                ↘
                 GOAL
```

He can receive on the left.

But he has little reason to remain there once a gap appears between the full-back and centre-back.

### Player Skills

**Acceleration** — Creates separation within the first few steps.

**Off-Ball Timing** — Begins moving as the passing opportunity develops, not after the pass has already been played.

**Diagonal Runs** — Moves from wide areas toward goal, reducing the distance between himself and the most valuable scoring zones.

**Open-Space Carrying** — Uses longer touches and explosive speed when space is available.

**Finishing** — His penetration has a clear destination: the goal.

Calling Mbappé simply an LW describes his starting point.

His attacking identity is closer to:

**Space Hunter + Central Finisher**

---

# 3. Salah — The Goal-Oriented Winger

### Core Style

**Right Side → Inside Channel → Final Action**

Salah's game is heavily oriented toward the goal.

```text
SALAH ●
       ↙
         ↙
       INSIDE
          ↓
         GOAL
```

He may begin on the right, but his most dangerous actions often happen closer to the centre.

### Player Skills

**Diagonal Movement** — Attacks the channel between full-back and centre-back.

**Left-Foot Finishing** — Quickly creates shooting positions after moving inside.

**Blind-Side Movement** — Attacks the box when defenders are focused on the opposite side.

**Combination Play** — Uses quick exchanges with central attackers to penetrate the defensive line.

**Final-Third Decision-Making** — Quickly chooses between Run, Pass and Shoot.

The label RW explains **where Salah starts**.

It does not fully explain what he becomes.

**RW describes his starting zone.
Finisher describes his function.**

---

# 4. Lamine Yamal — The Decision Creator

### Core Style

**Receive Wide → Manipulate Defender → Decide**

Yamal creates a different problem.

He does not always need to immediately attack the box.

He can receive wide and wait for the defence to reveal its decision.

```text
              PASS
               ↑
YAMAL ● → INSIDE → SHOOT
               ↓
            DRIBBLE
```

His strength is his ability to preserve several attacking options at once.

### Player Skills

**First-Touch Direction** — Uses the first touch to prepare the next action.

**Close Control** — Keeps the ball close enough to change direction in tight spaces.

**Micro-Acceleration** — Uses short bursts rather than relying only on long-distance speed.

**Vision** — Sees central runs and opposite-side movements after coming inside.

**Decision-Making** — Can choose between dribble, through ball, combination and shot.

This is what makes his dribbling particularly valuable.

The objective is not always:

**Dribble → Beat Defender**

It can be:

**Dribble → Force Reaction → Decide**

Yamal starts as a winger but can become a **Wide Playmaker**.

---

# 5. Ousmane Dembélé — The Two-Footed Problem

### Core Style

**Uncertainty → Penetration → Central Threat**

Dembélé creates one of the most difficult problems for a defender.

Against many attackers, the defender can adjust his body position toward the player's stronger foot.

```text
Left-footed attacker
        ↓
Force him right
```

Against Dembélé, that solution becomes much less reliable.

```text
                LEFT
                 ↖
DEFENDER ← DEMBÉLÉ → RIGHT
                 ↘
               CENTRE
```

Both directions can remain dangerous.

### Player Skills

**Two-Footed Dribbling** — Makes it difficult for defenders to force him toward one predictable direction.

**Change of Pace** — Can approach slowly before suddenly accelerating.

**Body Feints** — Separates body movement from the eventual direction of the ball.

**1v1 Penetration** — Can attack either side of the defender.

**Combination Play** — Can move centrally and connect through quick exchanges.

**Central Finishing** — His development has increasingly allowed him to finish attacks rather than only create them from wide areas.

His evolution can be represented as:

```text
WIDE DRIBBLER
      ↓
CHAOS CREATOR
      ↓
CENTRAL THREAT
      ↓
FINISHER
```

Dembélé shows that an inverted winger does not necessarily need one dominant pattern.

His uncertainty itself becomes the weapon.

**Robben created a dilemma with one extraordinary foot.
Dembélé creates the dilemma with both.**

---

# 6. Désiré Doué — The Press-Resistant Multi-Role Attacker

### Core Style

**Receive Under Pressure → Escape → Progress**

Doué's versatility is not simply that he can appear in several positions.

The deeper quality is his ability to **change function while under pressure**.

```text
        PRESSURE
           ↓
         DOUÉ ●
        ↙  ↓  ↘
     Carry Pass Turn
```

He can isolate a defender wide.

He can receive in the half-space.

He can turn centrally.

He can carry the ball through pressure.

### Player Skills

**Press Resistance** — Maintains control when defenders close aggressively.

**Body Manipulation** — Uses shoulder drops and body positioning to shift an opponent's balance.

**Ball Carrying** — Can personally move possession beyond a defensive line.

**Explosive First Steps** — Accelerates immediately after moving the defender.

**Tight-Space Dribbling** — Remains effective when central areas become crowded.

**Progressive Passing** — Can release the ball after attracting pressure rather than dribbling indefinitely.

His dribbling has a clear tactical purpose:

**Dribble → Break Pressure → Progress**

Doué is therefore more than a winger.

He can function as a:

**Press Breaker + Multi-Role Attacker**

---

# 7. Kvaratskhelia — The 1v1 Manipulator

### Core Style

**Isolate → Manipulate → Explode**

Khvicha Kvaratskhelia is particularly dangerous in isolated wide situations.

But he does not always attack the defender at maximum speed.

Sometimes he slows the situation down.

```text
KVARA ●              ● FB

          WAIT

           ↓

        EXPLODE
```

The defender moves closer.

The distance changes.

Then Kvaratskhelia changes rhythm.

### Player Skills

**Static 1v1 Ability** — Can create danger even without receiving at full speed.

**Body Feints** — Moves the defender before moving the ball.

**Change of Rhythm** — Uses Slow → Explode sequences effectively.

**Close Touches** — Allows rapid direction changes near the final third.

**Inside Carrying** — Progresses diagonally from the left toward central areas.

**Release Timing** — Can pass after attracting additional defensive attention.

His dribbling is therefore not simply:

**Beat Defender**

A better description is:

**Attract → Manipulate → Beat → Release**

Kvaratskhelia is both a **1v1 Dribbler and Chance Creator**.

---

# 8. Michael Olise — The Half-Space Playmaker

### Core Style

**Right Wing → Half-Space → Create**

Olise is another left-footed player who can begin from the right.

But reducing his game to **Cut Inside + Shoot** misses much of his value.

```text
RIGHT WING

OLISE ●
      ↙
   HALF-SPACE
      ↓
    CREATE
```

For Olise, the half-space is not simply a shooting position.

It can become a **Passing Platform**.

### Player Skills

**First Touch** — Quickly creates the angle for the next pass or dribble.

**Tight Dribbling** — Uses close control and short steps in crowded areas.

**Change of Direction** — Exploits the defender's approach angle.

**Through Passing** — Finds runners behind the defensive line.

**Combination Play** — Connects midfielders and forwards.

**Finishing Threat** — Prevents defenders from defending only the pass.

His pattern can often look like:

```text
Receive Wide
     ↓
Move Inside
     ↓
Attract Defender
     ↓
Connect
     ↓
Create / Finish
```

Olise is therefore not simply a right winger.

He can become a **Half-Space Playmaker**.

---

# 9. Luis Díaz — The Direct Wide Runner

### Core Style

**Receive → Accelerate → Attack**

Luis Díaz offers another type of winger profile.

His game can be highly direct.

```text
DÍAZ ●
   ↓
ACCELERATE
   ↓
DEFENDER
   ↓
BOX
```

Rather than slowing every attack to manipulate the defensive structure, he can increase the tempo immediately.

But his function changes when the ball moves away from him.

```text
BALL → RIGHT


DÍAZ
   ↘
     ↘
      BOX
```

### Player Skills

**Acceleration** — Quickly turns available space into forward momentum.

**Direct Carrying** — Moves toward the defender rather than automatically recycling possession.

**1v1 Aggression** — Willing to repeatedly challenge the defensive line.

**Off-Ball Box Runs** — Moves centrally when attacks develop from the opposite side.

**Counter-Pressing Intensity** — Can immediately contribute after possession is lost.

With the ball on his side:

**Wide Runner**

Without the ball on the opposite side:

**Box Attacker**

The same player can perform two different functions within the same attack.

---

# 10. Same Position, Completely Different Skill Profiles

Instead of asking which player is the “best winger,” we can ask:

**How does each player create an advantage?**

| Player            | Archetype            | Elite Weapon                | Main Target        |
| ----------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Mbappé**        | Space Hunter         | Acceleration + Timing       | Space Behind       |
| **Salah**         | Goal Winger          | Movement + Finishing        | Box                |
| **Yamal**         | Decision Creator     | Control + Vision            | Defensive Decision |
| **Dembélé**       | Chaos Forward        | Two-Footed 1v1              | Defensive Balance  |
| **Doué**          | Press Breaker        | Carrying + Press Resistance | Pressure Lines     |
| **Kvaratskhelia** | 1v1 Manipulator      | Rhythm + Feints             | Defender           |
| **Olise**         | Half-Space Playmaker | Passing + Technique         | Defensive Line     |
| **Luis Díaz**     | Direct Runner        | Acceleration + Aggression   | Space / Box        |

This is the important difference.

They may all be called wingers.

But they do not create superiority in the same way.

---

# 11. “Good Dribbler” Is No Longer a Deep Enough Description

Yamal, Dembélé, Doué and Kvaratskhelia are all excellent dribblers.

But saying:

**“They are good at dribbling.”**

does not tell us enough.

Their dribbling solves different problems.

```text
YAMAL
Dribble → Create Decision

DEMBÉLÉ
Dribble → Create Uncertainty

DOUÉ
Dribble → Break Pressure

KVARATSKHELIA
Dribble → Manipulate Defender

MBAPPÉ
Carry → Attack Space
```

The same applies to passing.

Olise uses passing to exploit movements around the defensive line.

Yamal can attract a defender before finding the newly opened option.

Doué can escape pressure first and then progress possession into the next line.

Technique should therefore never be analysed in isolation.

**Skill becomes valuable when it solves a tactical problem.**

---

# 12. Six Core Skills of the Modern Winger

Pace and dribbling are no longer enough to describe an elite winger.

A deeper analysis should examine six areas.

### ① Receiving

Where does he receive?

What does his first touch prepare?

### ② 1v1

Can he eliminate a defender directly?

And does he need space to do it?

### ③ Carrying

Can he transport the ball through or beyond a defensive line?

### ④ Creation

After attracting defenders, can he transfer the advantage to a teammate?

### ⑤ Off-Ball Movement

What does he do when he does **not** have the ball?

Can he attack the box, blind side or space behind?

### ⑥ Finishing

Can he personally convert the attack into a goal?

Elite modern wingers do not necessarily dominate all six categories equally.

Instead, they have different combinations.

```text
MBAPPÉ
Movement + Carrying + Finishing

SALAH
Movement + Finishing + Decision

YAMAL
Receiving + 1v1 + Creation

DEMBÉLÉ
1v1 + Two-Footedness + Finishing

DOUÉ
Receiving + Carrying + Press Resistance

KVARATSKHELIA
1v1 + Carrying + Creation

OLISE
Receiving + Creation + Finishing

LUIS DÍAZ
Carrying + Movement + Intensity
```

That combination is the player's real **Skill Profile**.

---

# 13. From Position to Function

So when analysing a modern winger, asking:

**“Where does he play?”**

is only the beginning.

We should also ask:

**Where does he receive?**

**How does he escape pressure?**

**How does he create an advantage?**

**Which defender does he manipulate?**

**Where does he move without the ball?**

**How does he finish the attack?**

```text
POSITION
   ↓
Where he starts


MOVEMENT
   ↓
Where he goes


SKILL
   ↓
How he creates advantage


FUNCTION
   ↓
What tactical problem he solves
```

Together, these four layers reveal the player's actual style.

---

# Conclusion — There Is No Single Modern Winger

Mbappé attacks **Space**.

Salah attacks the **Goal**.

Yamal creates **Decisions**.

Dembélé creates **Uncertainty**.

Doué breaks **Pressure**.

Kvaratskhelia manipulates the **Defender**.

Olise creates through **Connection**.

Luis Díaz increases attacking **Tempo**.

```text
MBAPPÉ        → SPACE

SALAH         → GOAL

YAMAL         → DECISION

DEMBÉLÉ       → UNCERTAINTY

DOUÉ          → PRESSURE BREAK

KVARATSKHELIA → MANIPULATION

OLISE         → CREATION

LUIS DÍAZ     → TEMPO
```

They are all wingers.

But they are not the same type of winger.

That is why the most interesting question in modern football is no longer simply:

**“Is he a winger or a striker?”**

A deeper question is:

**“What tactical problem does this player solve?”**

Position tells us **where he starts**.

Movement tells us **where he goes**.

Skill tells us **how he creates an advantage**.

Function tells us **what he gives the team**.

**The modern winger is no longer defined by where he stands.
He is defined by how he creates danger.**

The winger did not disappear.

The striker did not disappear.

The boundaries between their functions became flexible.

And that may be the most important stage in the evolution of the winger:

**The position remained.
The possibilities became almost limitless.**
