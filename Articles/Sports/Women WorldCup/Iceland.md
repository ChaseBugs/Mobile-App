## Stelpurnar okkar

> Data as of 17 October 2026. The 2027 Women's World Cup will be held in Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027. The final tournament draw has not yet taken place.
>
> ⚠️ **Qualification status:** not yet qualified. Iceland finished **third in League A Group 3** (behind Spain and England, 6 points) and entered the UEFA play-offs. They beat **Croatia 4-0 and 5-0** in round 1 (October 2026). Round 2 is against **Portugal (26 November / 5 December 2026)**; the winner qualifies. Iceland have **never played at a World Cup** — this tie is genuinely open.
>
> **Purpose:** a scouting report for coaching staff — how Iceland play, who decides their games, where they are vulnerable, and a concrete game plan.

**One-line verdict:** A small nation with a **world-class centre-back** (Viggósdóttir, OVR 84) and a **very fast forward** (Sveindís Jane Jónsdóttir, PAC 92). Iceland are organised and dangerous from long throws and set pieces. But against **Spain and England** they scored **one goal in four games** and conceded **12** — including **1-6 at home to Spain**. Most of the squad plays in Scandinavia and mid-table European leagues; if they qualify, they will be among the weakest teams in technical quality.

## 5. Head Coach — Þorsteinn Halldórsson

- In charge since 2021; took Iceland to Euro 2022 (three draws) and Euro 2025.
- **Approach:** a compact 4-4-2 block, direct balls to the forwards, and heavy use of set pieces and long throws.
- **Concern:** Iceland lost all seven games against Euro 2025 opponents, Spain and England (Euro 2025 + four qualifiers), and have not won a game at a major tournament since Euro 2013 (Euro 2017: three defeats; Euro 2022: three draws; Euro 2025: three defeats).

## 6. Tactics and Formation

### 6.1 Shapes

```text
OUT OF POSSESSION (4-4-2 / 4-5-1 LOW BLOCK)

               JESSEN / ZOMERS         SVEINDÍS JANE / H. EIRÍKSDÓTTIR
   PÁLMADÓTTIR    JÓHANNSDÓTTIR     VILHJÁLMSDÓTTIR      ÁSGEIRSDÓTTIR

IN POSSESSION — DIRECT

   Long ball from Viggósdóttir / goalkeeper → Sveindís Jane runs in behind
   Second ball → Vilhjálmsdóttir / Jóhannsdóttir
   Throw-ins near the box → long throws
```

### 6.2 Strengths
- **Glódís Perla Viggósdóttir** — Bayern Munich centre-back (DEF 86, PHY 83); scored a penalty vs Croatia.
- **Sveindís Jane Jónsdóttir** — PAC 92; long throws; the main counter-attack weapon.
- **Karólína Lea Vilhjálmsdóttir** (Inter Milan, 64 caps / 15 goals) — the creative midfielder.
- **Set pieces and long throws** — the traditional Icelandic weapon.
- **Organisation vs weaker teams** — six clean sheets in six games.

### 6.3 Weaknesses in Shape
- **Low technical level in midfield** — Spain controlled both games completely.
- **Slow second centre-back** (Sigurðardóttir PAC 69) and full-backs from mid-level clubs.
- **Few goal sources** — 1-0 twice vs Ukraine.

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

1. **Keep the ball and move it quickly** — Spain scored nine goals in two games this way.
2. **Attack the full-backs 1v1** — Heiðarsdóttir (67) and the right-back are the weakest links.
3. **Avoid Viggósdóttir — attack the other centre-back** (Sigurðardóttir PAC 69).
4. **Defend set pieces and long throws carefully** — Iceland's main goal source. No cheap throw-ins or corners in your third.
5. **Keep a covering defender for Sveindís Jane** (PAC 92) — don't leave a high line open to long balls.
6. **Score in the first half** — once ahead, Iceland have little to come back with.

```text
POSSESSION + QUICK PASSING
        +
1v1 ON THE FULL-BACKS
        +
ATTACK AWAY FROM VIGGÓSDÓTTIR
        +
DEFEND LONG THROWS / SET PIECES
        +
COVER SVEINDÍS JANE IN BEHIND
        ↓
SPAIN 6-1 IN REYKJAVÍK
```

## 13. Conclusion

Iceland are organised, physical and tough against teams of their level — **six clean sheets in six games** against Ukraine, Northern Ireland and Croatia. They have a world-class captain and one of the fastest forwards in Europe.

But the gap to elite teams is large: **one goal and twelve conceded in four games against Spain and England**. If Iceland reach their first World Cup, any team with technical quality and wide 1v1 players should beat them.

> **Keep the ball, attack Iceland's full-backs, and respect only their set pieces.**
