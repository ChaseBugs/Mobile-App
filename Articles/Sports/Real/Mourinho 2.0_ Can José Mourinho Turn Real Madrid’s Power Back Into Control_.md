# Mourinho 2.0: Can José Mourinho Turn Real Madrid’s Power Back Into Control?

When José Mourinho returned to Real Madrid in 2026, he faced a challenge almost opposite to the one he inherited in 2010.

During his first era, Madrid needed more intensity, more verticality, more competitive aggression and more speed.

Mourinho gave them exactly that.

His Real Madrid became one of the most devastating transition teams in Europe, built around Cristiano Ronaldo, Ángel Di María, Mesut Özil, Xabi Alonso and Sami Khedira.

But Mourinho 2.0 has inherited something very different.

Modern Real Madrid already possess enormous power.

They have Kylian Mbappé.

Vinícius Júnior.

Jude Bellingham.

Federico Valverde.

Eduardo Camavinga.

Aurélien Tchouaméni.

Arda Güler.

Bernardo Silva.

Yan Diomande.

Marc Cucurella.

Denzel Dumfries.

Trent Alexander-Arnold.

The problem is no longer creating speed.

The problem is controlling it.

The central question of Mourinho’s second Real Madrid era is therefore simple:

> **Can he turn the most powerful version of Madrid into a controlled one?**

---

# 1. Mourinho 1.0 Was About Speed

Mourinho’s first Madrid could be simplified like this:

```text
                 Benzema

Ronaldo           Özil           Di María

          Xabi Alonso   Khedira
```

The idea was devastating.

Recover the ball.

Find Xabi.

Play vertically.

Release Özil or Di María.

Find Ronaldo.

Attack before the opponent reorganized.

```text
BALL RECOVERY
      ↓
XABI ALONSO
      ↓
VERTICAL PASS
      ↓
ÖZIL / DI MARÍA
      ↓
RONALDO
      ↓
GOAL
```

That team was built around:

**Speed**

**Transition**

**Verticality**

**Intensity**

**Directness**

Mourinho taught Madrid how to accelerate.

Mourinho 2.0 may need to teach them something different.

When not to accelerate.

---

# 2. The Modern Madrid Already Has More Than Enough Power

The current squad contains an extraordinary amount of athleticism.

Consider the profiles:

**Mbappé** — explosive depth and finishing.

**Vinícius** — one-versus-one dribbling and acceleration.

**Bellingham** — vertical running and box attacking.

**Valverde** — stamina, speed and long-distance coverage.

**Camavinga** — press resistance through carrying.

**Tchouaméni** — physical control and defensive protection.

**Dumfries** — explosive overlap and penalty-area arrival.

**Diomande** — width, directness and acceleration.

This team does not need more energy.

It needs structure.

Mourinho’s job is not to reduce that power.

His job is to decide **when to release it**.

---

# 3. The Post-Kroos Problem

The biggest structural issue after Toni Kroos left was never simply losing a passer.

Madrid lost their internal clock.

Kroos controlled tempo.

He controlled positioning.

He controlled when Madrid accelerated.

He controlled when they slowed the game down.

He could move the opponent without moving very much himself.

```text
Opponent Press
      ↓

     KROOS
   ↙   ↓   ↘

CB    CM    Full-back

      ↓

Opponent shifts

      ↓

SWITCH PLAY
```

The younger midfielders solve problems differently.

Camavinga can turn and carry.

Valverde can run through open space.

Bellingham can attack the box.

But that is not the same as controlling the game.

That is why the new signings are so important.

---

# 4. Bernardo Silva Could Become Mourinho’s New Control Mechanism

Bernardo Silva may be one of the most important signings of the Mourinho 2.0 project.

Not because he adds more athleticism.

He adds something Madrid badly needed:

**calmness in possession.**

Bernardo can receive under pressure.

Protect the ball.

Play short combinations.

Recycle possession.

Slow the match down.

Then accelerate it again.

```text
PRESS
 ↓
BERNARDO
 ↓
PROTECT
 ↓
SHORT PASS
 ↓
RECEIVE AGAIN
 ↓
CONTROL
```

Valverde sees space and runs.

Camavinga sees pressure and carries through it.

Bellingham sees the box and attacks it.

Bernardo often asks a different question:

> **Do we really need to attack now?**

That question matters.

He is not Kroos.

But he can help restore part of the control Kroos once provided.

---

# 5. Arda Güler May Become the Long-Term Playmaking Project

Arda Güler gives Mourinho something else.

Vision.

Creativity.

Final-ball quality.

A different kind of technical imagination.

Güler should not be viewed only as a right winger or classic No.10.

He has the potential to evolve into a deeper creative midfielder.

```text
        Mbappé
           ↑
           │
Vini ←── GÜLER ──→ Diomande
           │
           ↓
       Bellingham
```

The most interesting question is not whether Güler can score.

It is whether he can eventually help organize the game.

If Mourinho develops him as a midfielder rather than only an attacker, Madrid could build combinations such as:

```text
          Bellingham

Güler                 Valverde

          Tchouaméni
```

or:

```text
           Bernardo

Güler                 Valverde

          Tchouaméni
```

In that sense, Güler is not the new Özil.

He could become something broader:

**a creative midfielder capable of influencing the entire structure.**

# 7. Yan Diomande Gives Madrid Another Explosive Wide Threat

Diomande is also strategically important.

One of Madrid’s recurring structural problems has been left-side concentration.

Vinícius naturally prefers the left.

Mbappé is also extremely dangerous entering from the left half-space.

That can produce this:

```text
VINI
  ↘
    ↘

     MBAPPÉ
       ↓

```

Opponents can overload that zone defensively.

Diomande changes the geometry.

```text

 ↓                          ↓

          Mbappé
             ↓
           GOAL
```

Now the opponent cannot simply shift heavily toward Vinícius and Mbappé.

They must defend both sides.

That creates more central space.

And that central space can be attacked by:

**Bellingham**

**Valverde**

**Mbappé**

or even **Dumfries** arriving late.


Cucurella can overlap when needed.

But he can also remain deeper.

Move inside.

Press aggressively.

Cover the channel.

Support the midfield.

His function can be summarized like this:

```text

      ↓

      ↓
```

This is classic Mourinho logic.

Give the superstar freedom.

But build the structure that pays for that freedom.

# Conclusion: Mourinho Does Not Need Another Kroos

That may be the most important idea of Mourinho’s second Madrid project.

Madrid should not search desperately for one player to replace Toni Kroos.

A direct replacement may not exist.

Instead, Mourinho can distribute Kroos’s old functions across the squad.

**Bernardo Silva** can provide rhythm and control.

**Arda Güler** can provide creativity and progressive passing.

**Trent Alexander-Arnold** can provide deep distribution and switches.

**Tchouaméni** can protect the structure.

**Camavinga** can escape pressure through carrying.

**Valverde** can cover enormous spaces.

**Bellingham** can connect midfield and attack.

**Cucurella** can protect the left side.

**Dumfries** can provide vertical power on the right.

**Diomande** can stretch the pitch and provide direct wide threat.

**Vinícius** can destroy defenders one-on-one.

And **Mbappé** can finish everything.

That gives Mourinho an extraordinary tactical equation:

```text
BERNARDO + GÜLER + TRENT
          ↓
       CONTROL

VALVERDE + CAMAVINGA + JUDE
          ↓
        POWER

TCHOUAMÉNI + CUCURELLA
          ↓
       BALANCE

DUMFRIES + DIOMANDE
          ↓
     VERTICALITY

VINÍCIUS + MBAPPÉ
          ↓
       EXPLOSION
```

Mourinho 1.0 was about:

# **Release the Chaos.**

Mourinho 2.0 could become something more sophisticated:

# **Control the Game. Choose the Moment. Then Release the Chaos.**

And that may be the solution Real Madrid have been searching for since the end of the Kroos era.
