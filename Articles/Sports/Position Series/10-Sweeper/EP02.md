# SERIES 10 — The Sweeper

# EP02 — The Birth of the Libero

## The Verrou and Catenaccio — Why Did Football Need “the Free Man”?

25 May 1967, Lisbon. European Cup final, Celtic vs Inter.

In the 7th minute, Inter won a penalty, and Mazzola converted it.

1-0.

Then Inter dropped back.

Helenio Herrera's Inter were the most feared defensive team in Europe.

A team that had won the European Cup twice in three years.

A team that believed one goal was enough.

But that day, Celtic attacked without end.

Gemmell's equaliser. Then, near the end, Chalmers' winner.

2-1. Celtic became the first British club to win the European Cup.

The match is remembered as the one that exposed **the limits of catenaccio.**

But to understand those limits, we first need to understand why catenaccio was born.

**And at the heart of it stood one free defender: the Libero.**

---

# 1. Where the Problem Began — Not Enough Defenders

In the 1920s and 30s, the standard system was the **W-M (3-2-2-3).**

```text
THE W-M SYSTEM

  FW   FW   FW   FW   FW     ← 5 ATTACKERS
     MF        MF
        MF  MF
  DF        DF        DF     ← 3 DEFENDERS
            GK
```

There were many attackers and few defenders.

Each defender took one opposing forward.

But if one of them was beaten, there was nobody behind.

**How could a weaker team survive against a stronger one?**

That question created the Libero.

# THE WEAKER TEAM NEEDS AN EXTRA DEFENDER

---

# 2. Karl Rappan — The Swiss Bolt

In the 1930s, the Austrian coach Karl Rappan took charge of the Swiss club Servette and the Swiss national team.

Switzerland were not a strong side.

So Rappan found a solution:

**Drop one midfielder behind the defensive line.**

This player did not mark a specific forward.

When a teammate was beaten, he closed the gap.

```text
RAPPAN'S VERROU

  DF        DF        DF     ← EACH MARKS A MAN
            ↓
       VERROUILLEUR          ← COVERS FREELY BEHIND
            GK
```

The system was called the **“verrou”** — French for **“the bolt.”**

The player at the back was called the **“verrouilleur,”** the one who slides the bolt.

Switzerland took this system to the 1938 World Cup and knocked out Germany in the first round.

**For the first time, the way a weaker team could beat a stronger one had become a system.**

# THE BOLT

---

# 3. Into Italy — Nereo Rocco and the Small Clubs

In the 1940s and 50s, the idea crossed into Italy.

Italy's small clubs used the verrou to survive against the big ones.

Nereo Rocco achieved remarkable results with this defensive approach at small clubs such as Triestina and Padova.

Then, in the early 1960s, he took charge of AC Milan.

1963, Wembley. European Cup final, Milan vs Benfica.

Milan won 2-1 to become **the first Italian club to win the European Cup.**

Leading that Milan defence as captain was **Cesare Maldini** —

the man who would later become Paolo Maldini's father.

---

# 4. Helenio Herrera — Catenaccio Perfected

In 1960, Helenio Herrera became coach of Inter.

He refined the verrou even further.

```text
HERRERA'S CATENACCIO

      FW         FW
   MF    MF    MF
FB  STOPPER  STOPPER  FB    ← MAN-MARKING
         LIBERO              ← PICCHI
           GK
```

**Armando Picchi** guarded the space behind the defensive line as Libero.

Because of that, left-back **Giacinto Facchetti** was free to join the attack boldly.

Facchetti showed remarkable goal-scoring ability for a full-back and became one of the prototypes of the modern attacking full-back. (SERIES 06)

**Because the Libero protected the space behind, the full-back was set free.**

Inter won:

**The 1964 European Cup** — Vienna, Real Madrid 3-1.

**The 1965 European Cup** — San Siro, Benfica 1-0.

And three Serie A titles.

The team became known as **“La Grande Inter.”**

# THE LIBERO FREES THE OTHERS

---

# 5. The Principle of Catenaccio — Give No Space

Catenaccio was not simply “football that only defends.”

Its principle was this:

```text
1. TIE UP THE OPPOSING FORWARDS WITH MAN-MARKING
2. THE LIBERO COVERS EVERY MISTAKE BEHIND
3. WHEN THE BALL IS WON, COUNTER-ATTACK QUICKLY
```

The opponent could keep the ball for a long time.

But they could hardly ever find **space in front of goal.**

And then one counter-attack would bring a goal.

This became part of the identity of Italian football.

# DEFEND, THEN STRIKE

---

# 6. The Libero's First Definition — “The Last Safety Net”

In this era, the Libero's role was clear:

**He does not mark.**

**He stands behind the defensive line.**

**When a teammate is beaten, he stops the attacker.**

**He clears the ball.**

Stepping forward with the ball was not his job.

When Picchi won the ball, he usually moved it on quickly to a midfielder or forward.

# LIBERO 1.0 = THE SWEEPER

The English name “sweeper” comes from this role:

**The one who sweeps up** — who tidies every ball his teammates miss.

---

# 7. Lisbon, 1967 — The Limits of Catenaccio

Let us return to Lisbon.

Celtic manager Jock Stein kept attacking to break down Inter's defence.

Crosses from the flanks, long-range shots, full-backs joining the attack.

To protect a single goal, almost every Inter player dropped back towards the edge of their own box.

In the end, Gemmell's long-range shot brought the equaliser, and Chalmers scored the winner.

```text
INTER LEAD 1-0
       ↓
EVERYONE DEFENDS
       ↓
CELTIC PRESS WITHOUT END
       ↓
2-1 COMEBACK
```

The match left one lesson:

**A bolt can lock a door — but not forever.**

And a few years later, a German player would take the Libero in a completely different direction.

# A BOLT CAN BE BROKEN

---

# Conclusion — From the Weaker Team's Weapon to the Stronger Team's Weapon

The Libero began as **a survival strategy for weaker teams.**

```text
1930s   RAPPAN   — SWITZERLAND'S VERROU
1950s   ROCCO    — ITALY'S SMALL CLUBS
1963    ROCCO    — MILAN, ITALY'S FIRST EUROPEAN CUP
1964–65 HERRERA  — INTER, CATENACCIO PERFECTED
1967    CELTIC   — THE LIMITS OF CATENACCIO
```

The Libero of this era was **the defence's last safety net.**

But the seed of the next revolution was already inside it:

**A player with nobody to mark is free to do something else.**

> **The Libero was born to defend. But from the very beginning, his freedom was bigger than defending.**
