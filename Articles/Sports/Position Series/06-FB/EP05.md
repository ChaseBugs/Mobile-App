# SERIES 06 — The Evolution of the Full-Back

# EP05 — Full-Back, Centre-Back or Midfielder?

## Why Do Modern Defenders Perform Three Positions in the Same Game?

Is he a Full-Back?

A Centre-Back?

A Midfielder?

In the past, one answer was usually enough.

In modern football, it often is not.

**John Stones — CB → MF**

**David Alaba — FB ↔ CB ↔ MF**

**Joshua Kimmich — FB ↔ MF**

**Kyle Walker — FB ↔ CB**

**Joško Gvardiol — CB ↔ FB → Attack**

What makes these players important is not simply that they can play in several positions.

The deeper value is this:

**They can change function inside the same match.**

That is why the modern hybrid defender is best understood through one sequence:

# POSITION → PHASE → FUNCTION → TRANSFORMATION

---

# 1. Modern Teams Do Not Play One Formation for 90 Minutes

A team may be listed as a 4-3-3.

But during the game, the structure can constantly change.

```text
DEFENDING
4-4-2
   ↓
BUILD-UP
3-2-5
   ↓
ATTACK
2-3-5
   ↓
BALL LOST
3-2 REST DEFENCE
```

The players have not changed.

Their functions have.

This is why hybrid players are so valuable.

They allow a coach to change the structure without making a substitution.

---

# 2. Why Did the Full-Back Evolution Return Toward the Defender?

The evolution of the full-back looks almost circular.

At first:

**Defender**

Then:

**Attacker**

Then:

**Midfield Function**

And now:

**Hybrid Defender**

```text
DEFENDER
   ↓
ATTACKER
   ↓
MIDFIELD FUNCTION
   ↓
HYBRID DEFENDER
```

But this is not a return to the old defensive full-back.

The old defensive full-back was mainly asked to:

**Defend.**

The modern hybrid defender may need to:

**Defend + Build + Connect + Progress + Transform.**

The defensive skill did not replace the attacking evolution.

It was added back into a much larger tactical package.

---

# 3. The Main Reason — The More You Attack, the More You Must Protect Behind

Elite possession teams often push many players high.

Wingers stay advanced.

No.8s enter the final third.

Forwards occupy the defensive line.

That creates attacking numbers.

It also creates risk.

```text
LW      CF      RW

    CM      CM

        DM

     CB     CB
```

If possession is lost, there can be a huge amount of space behind the attack.

So the modern question is no longer only:

**How can the full-back help the attack?**

It is also:

**How can we attack with numbers without becoming vulnerable?**

This is where the hybrid defender becomes essential.

---

# 4. Rest Defence — A Defender Can Give Attackers More Freedom

A strong attacking structure must also prepare for the possibility of losing the ball.

This is the idea of:

**Rest Defence.**

```text
       ATTACK

   CM        CM

      DM    FB

   CB   CB   FB
```

The exact shape varies from team to team.

The principle is more important:

**Attack with enough players, but leave enough structure behind to control the counterattack.**

A defender who can play as a full-back, centre-back, or midfielder gives the coach more ways to create that balance.

---

# 5. JOHN STONES — Manchester City

## CB → MF

### STRUCTURE

Stones is one of the clearest examples of the modern transformation.

The original inverted-full-back question was:

**Can a full-back become a midfielder?**

Stones introduces a new question:

**Why does it have to be a full-back?**

A centre-back can also step into midfield.

---

# 6. Stones in the Defensive Phase — Centre-Back

When the opponent has possession, Stones can operate as a conventional defender.

```text
        OPPONENT

LB      CB    STONES      RB
```

His responsibilities are familiar:

Protect the box.

Defend the striker.

Win aerial duels.

Hold the defensive line.

Cover space.

At this moment:

**He is a Centre-Back.**

---

# 7. Stones in Build-Up — The Extra Midfielder

When Manchester City gains possession, the picture can change.

Stones can step forward into midfield.

```text
          STONES
             ↑
            RODRI

      CB             CB
```

Now the opponent has another central player to deal with.

If Rodri is tightly marked:

```text
RODRI MARKED
     ↓
STONES AVAILABLE
     ↓
CB → STONES
     ↓
MIDFIELD ACCESS
```

The value is not simply that Stones gets more touches.

His movement changes the:

**Build-Up Structure.**

---

# 8. Match Example — 2023 Champions League Final

## Manchester City vs Inter

The 2023 Champions League Final is a useful example of the Stones role.

Inter defended the centre aggressively and tried to restrict City's normal central access.

Stones did not remain a passive centre-back.

He stepped into midfield and, when space appeared, carried the ball higher.

Conceptually:

```text
INTER MIDFIELD

O       O       O

       RODRI

       STONES ↑
          ↑
        FROM CB
```

Now Inter had a decision to make.

Who follows Stones?

If a midfielder steps toward him, another City player may become free.

If nobody steps out, Stones can advance.

This is why his role was powerful.

**He changed the opponent's reference points.**

---

# 9. What Stones Gives His Teammates

His movement creates benefits for others.

Rodri receives support.

Centre-backs gain another passing angle.

Advanced midfielders can stay higher.

Attackers do not need to drop as often.

```text
STONES MOVES
     ↓
EXTRA MIDFIELDER
     ↓
RODRI GETS SUPPORT
     ↓
ADVANCED PLAYERS STAY HIGH
```

That is why Stones represents:

# STRUCTURE

He is not simply changing position.

He is changing the team around him.

# 10. DAVID ALABA — Bayern Munich

## FB ↔ CB ↔ MF

### ADAPTABILITY

Alaba represents a different kind of hybrid player.

His importance is not just that he has played multiple positions.

It is that those positions require different spatial languages.

As a Full-Back:

**Width**

As a Centre-Back:

**Depth + Protection**

As a Midfielder:

**Pressure + Connection**

```text
        MIDFIELD
       Connection
           ↑
           |
FB ←─── ALABA ───→ CB
Width            Protection
```

Few players understand all three environments naturally.

---

# 11. Alaba as a Full-Back

## Width + Progression

When playing at left-back, Alaba can provide the outside lane.

```text
LW
 ↑

ALABA
 ↑

LCB
```

If the winger moves inside, Alaba can overlap.

If the winger stays wide, Alaba can move into a more supportive or interior position.

The important idea is not:

**Always overlap.**

It is:

**Read the winger and occupy the space the structure needs.**

---

# 12. Alaba as a Centre-Back

## Build-Up Defender

When Alaba moves into central defence, the same technical qualities gain a different meaning.

```text
        MIDFIELD

ALABA           CB
    ↖          ↗
       GK
```

His passing and carrying are no longer mainly about reaching the wing.

Now they help connect:

**First Line → Midfield**

If the forward presses him aggressively:

```text
PRESS → ALABA
          ↓
        CARRY
          ↓
   MIDFIELD SPACE
```

He can step forward with the ball and break the first line.

Same skill.

Different position.

Different function.

---

# 13. Why Alaba Was So Valuable at Bayern

A coach like Guardiola could use Alaba in several structures because he understood different zones of the pitch.

He could be:

LB.

CB.

A central support player.

A player who steps forward from the back.

This creates:

```text
ONE PLAYER
   ↓
MULTIPLE STRUCTURES
```

This is more than positional versatility.

It is:

# ADAPTABILITY

The ability to understand different tactical environments.

---

# 14. JOSHUA KIMMICH — Bayern Munich

## FB ↔ MF

### CONTROL

Kimmich represents another direction.

He does not simply enter midfield.

He can actually control the game from there.

Passing.

Tempo.

Switches of play.

Build-up connection.

Progression.

He becomes:

**Full-Back → Midfield Controller**

---

# 15. Kimmich as a Right-Back

Starting from right-back, he can still support the wing.

```text
                 RW

            KIMMICH

        CB          CB
```

But he does not always need to overlap.

If the winger provides width:

```text
RW → WIDTH

     KIMMICH → INSIDE
```

Kimmich can move into a central support role.

Now the winger keeps the flank wide while Kimmich helps the team control possession.

---

# 16. Kimmich as a Midfielder

In midfield, the responsibility becomes broader.

```text
LW        CF        RW

     CM        CM

        KIMMICH
```

If one side is crowded:

```text
LEFT CROWDED
     ↓
KIMMICH
     ↓
SWITCH
     ↓
RIGHT SIDE
```

If the centre opens:

```text
KIMMICH
   ↓
VERTICAL PASS
   ↓
BETWEEN LINES
```

He does not only keep possession.

He chooses:

**Where the next phase of the game should happen.**

That is:

# CONTROL THROUGH DISTRIBUTION

---

# 17. Club Example — Bayern and the Value of Full-Back Experience

Kimmich's experience as a full-back can also help him as a midfielder.

```text

   |
   ↓
CENTRE-BACK
```

From central midfield he can connect:

Centre-back.

Full-back.

It expands his:

**Perspective.**

Kimmich represents:

# CONTROL

# 18. KYLE WALKER — Manchester City

## FB ↔ CB

### SECURITY

Walker is completely different.

His hybrid value is not based on becoming a midfield controller.

His value is:

**Defensive Security.**

---

# 19. Walker as a Right-Back

Against a dangerous winger, Walker can defend the flank directly.

```text
OPPONENT LW
     ↓

   WALKER
```

He can defend outside.

Recover inside.

Track runs.

Protect the channel.

This is traditional full-back defending at an elite athletic level.

But he can also perform a second role.

---

# 20. Walker as a Right Centre-Back

When City attacks, Walker can effectively become part of a back three.

```text
          MIDFIELD

CB       CB       WALKER
```

Now his main purpose may not be overlap.

It may be:

**Protection.**

If possession is lost:

```text
BALL LOST
    ↓
FAST FORWARD
    ↓
SPACE BEHIND
    ↓
WALKER RECOVERY
```

His speed becomes part of the team's Rest Defence.

---

# 21. Match Example — Manchester City vs Real Madrid

## The Vinícius Problem

When facing Real Madrid, one obvious danger is giving Vinícius Júnior space to attack in transition.

Vinícius can punish space with:

Acceleration.

Carrying.

1v1 ability.

Direct running.

For City, Walker's position can therefore become part of the attacking plan.

```text
CITY ATTACK

       MANY PLAYERS ↑

WALKER
   ↓
PROTECT VINÍCIUS SPACE
```

He may appear to be doing less offensively.

But tactically, he is allowing others to do more.

---

# 22. The Walker Paradox

Sometimes not attacking is an attacking contribution.

```text
WALKER STAYS
     ↓
TRANSITION SECURITY
     ↓
MIDFIELDERS ATTACK HIGHER
     ↓
WINGERS TAKE MORE RISK
```

This is one of the key ideas of modern positional football:

**Defensive Security creates Attacking Freedom.**

Walker represents:

# SECURITY

# 23. JOŠKO GVARDIOL — Manchester City

## CB ↔ FB → Attack

### PROGRESSION

Gvardiol shows another direction of the hybrid-defender evolution.

His base qualities are strongly associated with centre-back play.

Physicality.

Duels.

Aerial defence.

Box protection.

Transition defending.

But his role does not end there.

He can play from the left side, carry forward, and appear much higher in possession.

```text
LCB
 ↓
LB
 ↓
CARRY
 ↓
FINAL THIRD
```

He can move from:

**Defender → Progressor → Attacking Support**

inside one possession sequence.

---

# 24. Gvardiol as a Centre-Back-Type Defender

When possession is lost, he may need to behave like a centre-back immediately.

```text
BALL LOST

      CF →
            SPACE

GVARDIOL
    ↓
DEFEND
```

At this moment, the important qualities are:

Body contact.

Covering.

Aerial ability.

Box defence.

Defensive timing.

He gives the team:

**Centre-Back Security.**

---

# 25. Gvardiol When City Controls Possession

The role changes when the opponent drops deeper.

If space appears in front of him:

```text
OPPONENT BLOCK

O   O   O   O

       SPACE

     GVARDIOL
         ↑
       CARRY
```

He can advance with the ball.

If an opponent steps out:

```text
O → GVARDIOL

     SPACE OPENS
```

new passing lanes can appear.

So carrying is not simply a dribbling skill.

It is a:

**Progression Tool.**

His movement can force defenders to leave their original position.

---

His height on the pitch can change depending on the phase.

In one moment:


In another:

```text

     GVARDIOL ↑
```

Different function.

# 29. One Match, Multiple Roles

The modern hybrid defender may change role several times in less than a minute.

### 00:00 — Opponent Has the Ball

```text
LB   CB   CB   RB
```

**Defender**

↓

### 00:08 — Ball Recovered

He moves into the build-up shape.

**Connector**

↓

### 00:15 — Press Broken

He enters midfield.

**Midfielder**

↓

### 00:22 — Attack Established

He supports a higher zone.

**Progressor / Support Player**

↓

### 00:27 — Possession Lost

He protects the dangerous space behind.

**Transition Defender**

This is why modern positional labels can be misleading.

**Position is temporary. Function changes with the phase.**

---

# 30. Why Coaches Value These Profiles

Different opponents create different tactical problems.

High Press?

**Stones-type solution → Extra Midfielder**

Need positional flexibility?

**Alaba-type solution → Adaptability**

Need possession control?

**Kimmich-type solution → Controller**

Dangerous transition opponent?

**Walker-type solution → Security**

Deep defensive block?

**Gvardiol-type solution → Progression**

```text
TACTICAL PROBLEM
       ↓
PLAYER FUNCTION
       ↓
STRUCTURAL SOLUTION
```

A hybrid player is not valuable merely because he can fill several positions.

He is valuable because he gives the coach:

**Several tactical answers.**

---

# 31. How to Analyse a Hybrid Defender During a Match

Instead of asking only:

“Is Stones a centre-back?”

“Is Gvardiol a left-back?”

Ask four better questions.

### 1. Where does he start?

**Starting Position**

### 2. Where does he move when his team has possession?

**Possession Position**

### 3. What does that movement allow teammates to do?

**Structural Effect**

### 4. Where is he when the ball is lost?

**Transition Role**

These four questions reveal the player's real tactical function.

# Conclusion — The Defender Became a Shape-Shifter

The story of SERIES 06 began with the full-back as a wide defender.

Then the role changed.

### Traditional Full-Back

**Defend Wide**

↓

### Cafu & Roberto Carlos

**Attack Wide**

↓

### Lahm

**Move Inside**

↓

### Inverted Full-Back

**Change Structure**

↓

### Modern Hybrid Defender

**Transform Between Structures**

The final stage is not about one perfect full-back model.

It is about creating more possible roles.

```text
STONES
CB → MF
STRUCTURE

ALABA
FB ↔ CB ↔ MF
ADAPTABILITY

KIMMICH
FB ↔ MF
CONTROL

WALKER
FB ↔ CB
SECURITY

GVARDIOL
CB ↔ FB → ATTACK
PROGRESSION
```

So the most useful modern question is no longer only:

**“What position does this player play?”**

It is:

**“What function does he perform in this phase?”**

Need another midfielder?

He becomes one.

Need another centre-back?

He becomes one.

Need to protect the flank?

He becomes a full-back.

Need progression?

He carries forward.

Need transition security?

He drops and protects.

That is why the evolution of the modern defender can be summarised as:

# POSITION → PHASE → FUNCTION → TRANSFORMATION

The traditional full-back protected his position.

The attacking full-back left it to attack.

The inverted full-back moved inside to change the structure.

The modern hybrid defender goes one step further:

**He changes his role according to the problem the game creates.**

> **The modern full-back is not defined by where he starts.
> He is defined by what he becomes when the game changes.**
