# SERIES 06 — The Evolution of the Full-Back

# EP06 — The Most Dangerous Full-Backs in Modern Football

## How Modern Full-Backs Became Different Kinds of Attacking Weapons

The modern full-back is no longer just one type of player.

Some carry the ball.

Some attack space.

Some create from deep.

Some protect the attack.

Some overload midfield.

Some deliver the final ball.

That is why six players can all be called full-backs while creating danger in completely different ways:

**Nuno Mendes**

**Achraf Hakimi**

**Jules Koundé**

**Marc Cucurella**

**Federico Dimarco**

**Trent Alexander-Arnold**

Same broad position.

Very different functions.

---

# 1. Nuno Mendes

## POWER + CARRYING

### The Explosive Progressor

The biggest threat in Mendes' game is not simply pace.

It is:

**Pace with the ball.**

He can receive deep and move the entire team forward himself.

```text
CB
 ↓
MENDES
 ↓
CARRY
 ↓
BREAK PRESS
 ↓
FINAL THIRD
```

A midfielder may beat a pressing line with a pass.

Mendes can beat it by carrying the ball through it.

That makes him a powerful:

# TRANSITION ACCELERATOR

---

# 2. Why Mendes Is Difficult to Press

Imagine the opponent presses PSG's left side.

```text
        PRESSER
           ↓

CB → MENDES
```

The pressing team wants to force him backward.

But if Mendes can turn forward:

```text
PRESS
  ↓
FIRST TOUCH FORWARD
  ↓
ACCELERATE
  ↓
PRESS BROKEN
```

Now a second defender must leave his zone.

```text
SECOND DEFENDER → MENDES

             SPACE OPENS
```

That space can then be attacked by a winger or midfielder.

So his carrying does more than gain distance.

It:

**Moves the opponent.**

---

# 3. Mendes Can Attack Outside or Inside

Traditional relationship:

```text
WINGER → INSIDE

FULL-BACK → OUTSIDE
```

Mendes can still do that.

But he can also attack inside.

### Option A

```text
LW ↘

MENDES ↑
OUTSIDE
```

### Option B

```text
LW → WIDTH

MENDES ↗
HALF-SPACE
```

### Option C

```text
LW ↓ TO BALL

MENDES ↑ BEHIND
```

This creates:

# MOVEMENT UNCERTAINTY

The defender cannot prepare for only one pattern.

---

# 4. Achraf Hakimi

## SPEED + TIMING

### The Full-Back Who Attacks Like a Forward

Hakimi creates danger differently.

Mendes is devastating with the ball.

Hakimi can be devastating without it.

```text
RW        CF

      HAKIMI ↑
```

He may start as a right-back.

But in the final third, his movement can resemble that of a forward.

---

# 5. Hakimi's Greatest Weapon — Timing

Speed is obvious.

Timing is more important.

A bad run starts too early.

The defender sees it.

Tracks it.

The threat disappears.

Hakimi can wait until the defender looks at the ball.

```text
DEFENDER → BALL

HAKIMI
   ↓
WAIT
   ↓
SPRINT
   ↓
BLIND SIDE
```

That is:

# BLIND-SIDE TIMING

He attacks at the moment the opponent is least prepared to react.

---

# 6. Hakimi Can Attack Several Lanes

Traditional overlap:

```text
RW → INSIDE

HAKIMI → OUTSIDE
```

But he can also move inside:

```text
RW → WIDTH

HAKIMI ↗
HALF-SPACE
```

Or continue directly into the box:

```text
CF

     HAKIMI ↑
        BOX
```

This makes him more than a supporting full-back.

He can become:

**A final-line runner.**

---

# 7. Jules Koundé

## SECURITY + SUPPORT

### The Full-Back Who Protects the Attack

Koundé creates danger in a less obvious way.

He does not always need to become the attacker himself.

His value can come from giving others freedom.

```text
RW ↑

CM ↑

KOUNDÉ
   ↓
STAY / SUPPORT

CB      CB
```

If he remains deeper, the winger can take more risk.

The midfielder can move higher.

The team can attack with more confidence.

---

# 8. Koundé's Attacking Value Can Be Indirect

This is a very important modern idea.

A full-back can help the attack without constantly overlapping.

```text
KOUNDÉ STAYS
     ↓
RIGHT SIDE SECURE
     ↓
WINGER ATTACKS HIGHER
     ↓
MIDFIELDER TAKES MORE RISK
```

So his function is:

# BALANCE

He protects the structure so others can attack.

---

# 9. Marc Cucurella

## AGGRESSION + ROTATION

### The Rotational Full-Back

Cucurella is different again.

His value often comes from movement and positional rotation.

He does not have to remain fixed to the touchline.

```text
LB
 ↓
INSIDE
 ↓
PRESS
 ↓
SUPPORT
 ↓
ATTACK SPACE
```

He can move between outside and inside areas depending on what the team needs.

---

# 10. Cucurella and Positional Rotation

If the winger stays wide:

```text
LW → WIDTH

CUCURELLA ↗
INSIDE
```

If the winger comes inside:

```text
LW → INSIDE

CUCURELLA ↑
OUTSIDE
```

Now the opponent must constantly adjust.

Who follows Cucurella?

Who protects the winger?

Who closes the half-space?

This is why his movement can disrupt man-oriented defending.

His main concept is:

# AGGRESSION

Not only physical aggression.

**Positional aggression.**

---

# 11. Federico Dimarco

## CROSSING + FINAL BALL

### The Wide Creator

Dimarco represents another attacking weapon.

He does not always need to dribble past defenders.

His delivery can create danger before he reaches the byline.

```text
DIMARCO
   ↓
HEAD UP
   ↓
CROSS / CUTBACK
   ↓
BOX
```

His attacking value comes from:

**Quality of delivery.**

---

# 12. The Power of the Early Cross

Traditional full-back:

```text
RUN TO BYLINE
    ↓
CROSS
```

Dimarco can attack earlier.

```text
RECEIVE
   ↓
EARLY CROSS
   ↓
SPACE BEHIND CB
   ↓
FORWARD RUN
```

This matters because the defence may not yet be fully organised.

The centre-backs are forced to turn and defend toward their own goal.

That is one of the hardest situations to control.

Dimarco represents:

# DELIVERY

---

# 13. Trent Alexander-Arnold

## PASSING + VISION

### The Deep Playmaker Full-Back

Trent is one of the clearest examples of a full-back who creates danger through passing.

Mendes can move through space.

Hakimi can run into space.

Trent can:

**Attack space with the ball.**

```text
TRENT
  ↓
SCAN
  ↓
DIAGONAL PASS
  ↓
WINGER
  ↓
ATTACK
```

He does not need to run 30 metres.

The ball can travel 40 metres instead.

---

# 14. Trent Attacks the Weak Side

Imagine the opponent overloads one side.

```text
O O O O
LEFT SIDE
```

Trent receives and looks across the pitch.

```text
TRENT
   ↓
SWITCH
   ↓
FREE PLAYER
```

One pass can change:

The pressing direction.

The point of attack.

The opponent's defensive shape.

This is why long passing can be a form of speed.

# BALL SPEED > RUNNING SPEED

Trent represents:

# CREATION

---

# 15. Six Full-Backs, Six Different Threats

| Player                     | Main Weapon           | Main Role                    | Core Threat    |
| -------------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------- |
| **Nuno Mendes**            | Pace + Carrying       | Ball-Carrying Full-Back      | **Explosion**  |
| **Achraf Hakimi**          | Speed + Timing        | Off-Ball Attacking Full-Back | **Timing**     |
| **Jules Koundé**           | Defence + Positioning | Security Full-Back           | **Balance**    |
| **Marc Cucurella**         | Movement + Rotation   | Rotational Full-Back         | **Aggression** |
| **Federico Dimarco**       | Crossing + Technique  | Wide Creator                 | **Delivery**   |
| **Trent Alexander-Arnold** | Passing + Vision      | Playmaking Full-Back         | **Creation**   |

This is the key point.

There is no longer one model of the dangerous full-back.

# 18. How to Watch Mendes During a Match

Do not only watch what happens after he receives the ball.

Watch:

Where is the space before he receives?

How quickly does he turn forward?

Who steps out to stop him?

What space appears after that defender moves?

The important sequence is:

```text
RECEIVE
 ↓
CARRY
 ↓
DRAW DEFENDER
 ↓
SPACE OPENS
```

That is where his real tactical value appears.

---

# 19. How to Watch Hakimi

Watch him without the ball.

Ask:

Where is the defender looking?

When does Hakimi start his run?

Does he attack outside?

Inside?

The half-space?

The box?

His value often appears before the pass is played.

```text
DEFENDER WATCHES BALL
        ↓
HAKIMI RUNS
        ↓
DEFENSIVE LINE STRETCHES
```

---

# 20. How to Watch Koundé

Do not judge him only by how often he overlaps.

Watch what happens when others attack.

Where does he stay?

Does he form a back three?

Does he protect the wide transition?

Does he allow the winger to remain higher?

His contribution often begins with:

**Positioning behind the attack.**

---

# 21. How to Watch Cucurella

Track his positional changes.

```text
LB
 ↓
INSIDE
 ↓
HIGHER SUPPORT
 ↓
PRESS
 ↓
BACK TO LB
```

If you only look at his starting position, you may miss his real role.

Watch how his movement changes the structure around him.

---

# 22. How to Watch Dimarco

Do not only count crosses.

Look at:

Where the cross comes from.

How early it is played.

Where the striker starts the run.

Whether the defence is facing the ball or facing its own goal.

The relationship is:

# DELIVERY + RUN TIMING

A good cross is not only about technique.

It is also about when the receiver attacks the space.

---

# 23. How to Watch Trent

Watch his body orientation.

Before receiving:

Does he scan the far side?

After receiving:

Does he play short?

Vertical?

Diagonal?

Switch?

```text
RECEIVE
 ↓
SCAN
 ↓
SELECT SIDE
 ↓
PASS
```

His danger often comes from choosing the part of the pitch the opponent is least able to defend.

---

# 24. Modern Full-Backs Speak Different Attacking Languages

### Mendes

**Beat pressure through carrying.**

### Hakimi

**Beat the defensive line through timing.**

### Koundé

**Give teammates attacking freedom through security.**

### Cucurella

**Break marking structures through movement.**

### Dimarco

**Attack the box through delivery.**

### Trent

**Attack space through passing.**

This is why the modern full-back cannot be reduced to:

**Defender + Overlap + Cross**

anymore.

---

# 25. The Full-Back Can Now Become the Main Attacking Weapon

Traditional structure:

```text
WINGER
  ↑
FULL-BACK SUPPORT
```

Modern structure can reverse that relationship.

```text
FULL-BACK ATTACKS

WINGER CONNECTS
```

Or:

```text
WINGER → WIDTH

FULL-BACK → HALF-SPACE
```

Or:

```text
WINGER → BALL

FULL-BACK → BEHIND
```

The full-back can now become a primary attacking threat.

Not just a support player.

---

# 26. Why Team Structure Still Matters

None of these freedoms exist without protection.

If a full-back attacks high:

```text
FB ↑

SPACE ↓
```

someone must protect the space behind.

That can be:

A centre-back.

A holding midfielder.

Another full-back.

A hybrid defender.

A strong counterpressing structure.

So:

# PLAYER FREEDOM REQUIRES TEAM STRUCTURE

A dangerous full-back is never completely independent from the system around him.

# 28. The Most Important Modern Skill — Role Selection

The key question is no longer:

**Can the full-back run forward?**

It is:

**Should he run forward now?**

```text
SPACE OPEN
   ↓
CHECK TEAM SHAPE
   ↓
CHECK WINGER
   ↓
CHECK OPPONENT
   ↓
GO / STAY
```

Modern full-back play is built on decision-making.

Speed matters.

Technique matters.

But first comes:

# ROLE SELECTION

---

# Conclusion — There Is No Longer One Dangerous Full-Back

Modern football no longer has one perfect full-back archetype.

Nuno Mendes creates danger through:

# EXPLOSION

Achraf Hakimi through:

# TIMING

Jules Koundé through:

# BALANCE

Marc Cucurella through:

# AGGRESSION

Federico Dimarco through:

# DELIVERY

Trent Alexander-Arnold through:

# CREATION

Different players.

Different weapons.

Different solutions.

That gives us the final lesson of SERIES 06:

# ONE POSITION → MANY FUNCTIONS → DIFFERENT THREATS

The old question was:

**“Can this full-back defend the flank?”**

Then it became:

**“Can he help the attack?”**

The modern question is:

# “How does he create an advantage?”

That advantage may come from carrying.

Running.

Protecting.

Rotating.

Crossing.

Passing.

The modern full-back is no longer defined only by how far forward he moves.

> **He is defined by how he changes the game from his side of the pitch.**
