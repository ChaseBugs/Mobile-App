## 6. Tactics and Formation

### Base Formation — 4-4-2 (High-Intensity Press)

```text
OUT OF POSSESSION (4-4-2 HIGH PRESS)

        KIM KYONG-YONG     HONG SONG-OK      ← FIRST PRESSING LINE (2)
 CHAE UN-YONG  MYONG YU-JONG  KIM SONG-GYONG  JON RYONG-JONG
   DF        DF (AN KUK-HYANG)     DF        DF
                   PAK JU-MI
```

```text
IN POSSESSION (FLUID 4-4-2 / PASSING TRIANGLES)

        KIM KYONG-YONG ←→ HONG SONG-OK        ← STRIKERS SWAP POSITIONS
   CHAE ↘      MYONG ↔ KIM SONG-GYONG     ↙ JON
        [WIDE MIDFIELDERS MOVE INSIDE → FULL-BACKS OVERLAP]
   DF          DF           DF          DF
```

### ① Pressing — A "Full-Throttle" 4-4-2
- ABC's pre-match analysis described the DPRK as a **"high-pressing 4-4-2."**
- Against China, they were "jumping and sliding into tackles from the outset" — "ferocious, full-throttle stuff that left China squeezed for space and time in midfield."
- **The two strikers press the opposing centre-backs**, and the four midfielders follow up tightly.

### ② Build-Up and Possession — "Evolving Triangles"
- Against Uzbekistan: "slick through midfield, passing and moving in neat, **evolving triangles.**"
- They out-possessed strong opponents too:
  - Asian Cup QF vs Australia: **62%** possession, **21 shots to 4**
  - Asian Games SF vs China: **over 60%** possession
- Australia coach Montemurro: "North Korea were relentless."

### ③ Attack — Crosses and the Front Two
- Wide midfielders and full-backs deliver crosses, and the front two finish in the box:
  - Asian Cup vs China, 32': Han Jin-hong's cross → Kim Kyong-yong goal
  - Asian Cup vs Australia, 65': Kim Kyong-yong's cross → Chae Un-yong goal
- **Long-range shooting:** Against Australia, captain An Kuk-hyang shot from around 35 metres and forced a save from GK Arnold.

### ④ Counter-Attacks — Proven at Club Level
- In the AFC Women's Champions League final, Naegohyang conceded possession to Tokyo Verdy — and won the title with **one quick counter-attack**, Jong Kum breaking down the left and Kim Kyong-yong finishing.
- Possession is their default, but **they can play on the counter too.**

### ⑤ Set Pieces
- **Taker:** Jon Ryong-jong
- 2026 Asian Games vs Korea Republic, 36': Jon Ryong-jong corner → Chae Un-yong goal
- 2026 Asian Games SF vs China: forced an own goal from an aerial duel.

### ⑥ Second-Half Intensity
- In the Asian Games final against Japan, they scored in the **47th and 69th minutes** to turn the game around.
- Against China at the Asian Cup, however, ABC noted that "energy and pressing discipline dropped after half-time."
- **👉 Their level varies a lot from game to game.**

## 12. How to Beat This Team

### Strategy ① — Go Over the Press With Long Balls and Attack the Space Behind

```text
DPRK 4-4-2 HIGH PRESS ↑↑
          ↓
OUR GK / CB → LONG BALL ────→ FAST FORWARD
          ↓
1v1 WITH A DPRK CENTRE-BACK (SPACE BEHIND)
```

- **Evidence:** Australia scored twice from four shots. ABC noted that "the high press leaves space behind the defence."
- Do not try to build short against the DPRK press — play **long passes over the first pressing line.**

### Strategy ② — Concede Possession, Protect the Area in Front of the Box

- The DPRK keep the ball but **lack the final pass** — 21 shots, one goal against Australia.
- Set **a two-line block** in front of the box and deal only with the long shots (An Kuk-hyang) and crosses (Kim Kyong-yong, Han Jin-hong).
- Press **the wide midfielders who deliver the crosses** first.

### Strategy ③ — Attack Straight After Half-Time

- **Evidence:** They conceded in the 47th minute against Australia and the 46th against Korea Republic.
- Use **a rehearsed attacking pattern** prepared at half-time to strike in the first five minutes of the second half.
- At the same time, be ready for **the DPRK's own early second-half press** (the 47th-minute equaliser against Japan). The start of the second half is **decisive for both sides.**

### Strategy ④ — Test Their Emotions and Discipline

- **Evidence:** Against China, there were protests, a booking for the coach and a reckless tackle.
- Keep the ball for long spells to draw **rash tackles** from DPRK players.
- **Dribble at them near the box** to win free kicks and penalties.
- Equally important: **do not lose our own composure** in response to their hard tackles early on.

### Strategy ⑤ — Tie Up the Three Key Players

| Player | Response |
|---|---|
| Kim Kyong-yong | One centre-back **marks her tight** + cut out the crosses |
| Myong Yu-jong | The defensive midfielder **tracks her late runs all the way** |
| Jon Ryong-jong | At set pieces, **protect the near post first**; block her crosses from wide |

### Strategy ⑥ — Exploit the Gap in Squad Depth

- The DPRK's substitutes are much weaker than their starters.
- Keep the tempo high, and use **fresh substitutes after 60–70 minutes** to decide the game.
- The tighter the schedule, the better our chances — especially **in the third group game and the early knockout rounds.**

### Strategy ⑦ — Use Physicality and Speed to Expose the Experience Gap

- Almost no DPRK player has faced European or US levels of physicality.
- Create an unfamiliar game with **strong physical duels, fast transitions and aerial balls.**

### Summary — The Formula for Beating the DPRK

```text
GO OVER THE PRESS WITH LONG BALLS → 1v1 IN THE SPACE BEHIND
                    +
A TWO-LINE BLOCK IN FRONT OF THE BOX (IT IS FINE TO CONCEDE POSSESSION)
                    +
ATTACK STRAIGHT AFTER HALF-TIME AND LATE IN THE GAME
                    +
TEST THEIR EMOTIONS AND DISCIPLINE · DRAW SET PIECES
                    ↓
EVEN IF DOMINATED, WIN WITH FEW CHANCES (THE AUSTRALIA MODEL)
```

## 13. Conclusion

The DPRK are **a golden generation that swept the youth World Cups** now arriving at senior level.

In **possession, pressing and intensity**, they are level with — or better than — Asia's top teams. They took revenge on China within six months, and scored twice against Japan.

But three weaknesses are clear:

1. **Turning dominance into wins — finishing**
2. **Concentration straight after half-time and late in games, and emotional control**
3. **Experience against the world's top teams, and squad depth**

> **Fight the DPRK on intensity, and it will be hard. Let them dominate — and seize the one moment they let slip.**
