# SERIES 09 — The Second Striker

# EP03 — The Death of the Strike Partnership

## How Did the Lone-Striker System Kill the Second Striker?

In the summer of 2004, José Mourinho arrived at Chelsea.

He did not use the 4-4-2, the basic formula of English football.

He used a **4-3-3** instead.

Up front, only Didier Drogba.

In midfield, Makélélé, Lampard and one more midfielder.

Chelsea won the league in 2004-05 with 95 points, a Premier League record at the time.

They conceded just **15 goals** in 38 games — still the fewest in Premier League history.

And English football learned one fact:

**Three midfielders beat two.**

---

# 1. A Problem of Numbers — 3 vs 2

The biggest weakness of the two-striker system was midfield.

```text
4-4-2 vs 4-3-3

  4-4-2 CENTRAL MIDFIELD:     CM    CM          ← 2 PLAYERS
  4-3-3 CENTRAL MIDFIELD:   CM    No.6    CM    ← 3 PLAYERS
```

When it is 2 vs 3 in central midfield,

the 4-4-2 team struggles to keep the ball,

and the opponent always has one free midfielder.

There was one solution:

**Drop one of the forwards into midfield.**

And that forward was usually the Second Striker.

# ONE MORE MIDFIELDER = ONE LESS STRIKER

---

# 2. 4-4-2 → 4-4-1-1 → 4-2-3-1

The change did not happen overnight.

A study of Premier League tactics from 2003 to 2014 found that the 4-4-2 declined steadily —

replaced first by the **4-4-1-1**, and finally by the **4-2-3-1.**

```text
4-4-2          4-4-1-1          4-2-3-1

ST  SS          ST                ST
                SS             W  10  W
M M M M       M M M M           6  6
```

In the **4-4-1-1**, the Second Striker still existed.

But he had dropped from **beside** the striker to **behind** him.

And in the **4-2-3-1**, that position took a new name: **the No.10.**

The Second Striker did not disappear. **He took one step back.**

# THE SECOND STRIKER STEPPED BACK

---

# 3. The Problem Without the Ball

The two-striker system had another problem:

**What does the Second Striker do when his team does not have the ball?**

In a 4-2-3-1 or 4-3-3, every player has a defensive position.

But in a classic 4-4-2, the Second Striker often became far less useful out of possession.

```text
4-2-3-1 OUT OF POSSESSION

       ST         ← PRESSES THE OPPOSING CB
  W    10    W    ← BLOCKS THE No.6 / FULL-BACKS
     6    6
  D  D  D  D
```

The No.10 in a 4-2-3-1 could mark the opponent's defensive midfielder.

The wingers could track the opposing full-backs.

Modern football became **football in which all eleven players defend.**

# EVERY PLAYER MUST DEFEND

---

# 4. Rooney — From Second Striker to Centre-Forward

Wayne Rooney's career shows this change perfectly.

Rooney was originally closer to a Second Striker.

He dropped deep to receive the ball, dribbled, shot from distance and created space for his teammates.

In 2007-08, Manchester United won the Premier League and the Champions League with an attack in which Ronaldo, Rooney and Tevez constantly swapped positions.

There was no fixed No.9.

### 21 May 2008, Moscow

Champions League final, Manchester United vs Chelsea.

Ronaldo headed the opener, and after a 1-1 draw, United won on penalties.

**An attack in which Ronaldo, Rooney and Tevez constantly swapped positions made the question “who is the Second Striker?” meaningless.**

Then, in the summer of 2009, Ronaldo left for Real Madrid.

Ferguson moved Rooney up front as the lone striker.

In 2009-10, Rooney scored **26 league goals** — the best scoring season of his career — and won both the PFA and FWA Player of the Year awards.

```text
2007-08  ROONEY = A FLUID ATTACKING FORWARD
2009-10  ROONEY = A LONE CENTRE-FORWARD (26 LEAGUE GOALS)
```

A player with the abilities of a Second Striker survived by becoming the lone striker.

# ADAPT OR DISAPPEAR

---

# 5. Gerrard — The Midfielder Becomes the Second Striker

The change also went the other way.

In 2008-09, Benítez's Liverpool played a 4-2-3-1.

Up front: Fernando Torres.

Right behind him: **Steven Gerrard.**

Gerrard, a central midfielder, effectively played as Torres' Second Striker.

He scored 16 league goals that season and was named FWA Footballer of the Year.

Liverpool finished second, behind Manchester United.

```text
       TORRES
         ↑
      GERRARD   ← A MIDFIELDER PLAYING AS SECOND STRIKER
   MF        MF
```

This case reveals an important truth:

**The Second Striker's space did not disappear. The type of player who occupied it changed.**

Instead of a forward, **an attacking midfielder** began to fill it.

# THE MIDFIELDER TOOK THE SPACE

---

# 6. National Teams Changed Too

The change appeared in international football as well.

At the 2006 World Cup, Italy won with Toni up front and Totti right behind him.

It was still the era of the Second Striker.

But at the 2010 World Cup, Spain won with a 4-2-3-1 and a midfield-driven game.

Villa was up front, but the games were controlled by Xavi, Iniesta, Busquets and Xabi Alonso.

In the Euro 2012 final, Spain even played without a recognised striker, using Fàbregas at the top of the attack.

```text
2006 ITALY  →  TONI + TOTTI (CLOSE TO A FRONT TWO)
2010 SPAIN  →  VILLA + MIDFIELD CONTROL
2012 SPAIN  →  AN ATTACK WITHOUT A STRIKER (FÀBREGAS)
```

### The Spain That Won With Eight Goals

At the 2010 World Cup, Spain scored only **eight goals** in seven games — and won the tournament.

It was the lowest total ever by a World Cup winner.

All four of their knockout games ended 1-0.

Spain won not through their strikers' goals, but through **midfield possession.**

Then, in the Euro 2012 final, with Fàbregas as a False 9, they beat Italy **4-0.**

```text
2010 WORLD CUP:   8 GOALS, ALL FOUR KNOCKOUT GAMES WON 1-0
EURO 2012 FINAL:  4-0 WITHOUT A RECOGNISED STRIKER
```

**Spain proved that a team could win without a striker at all.**

# MIDFIELD BECAME THE CENTRE OF EVERYTHING

---

# 7. The Age of Pressing — The End of the Luxury Role

In the 2010s, under the influence of coaches such as Klopp and Guardiola, **high pressing** became the standard in European football.

The press starts with the forwards.

```text
PRESSING SYSTEM

FW → PRESSES THE OPPOSING CB
FW → BLOCKS PASSING ANGLES
MF → SECOND WAVE OF PRESSURE
```

A forward who rests when his team does not have the ball creates a hole in the whole pressing structure.

It is the same reason the classic No.10 declined, as we saw in SERIES 02.

**The “genius who does not defend” was no longer allowed.**

# PRESSING ENDED THE LUXURY

---

# 8. But the 4-4-2 Never Completely Died

Interestingly, the 4-4-2 did not vanish entirely.

**Simeone's Atlético Madrid** kept a defensive 4-4-2 and won La Liga in 2013-14.

### 17 May 2014, Camp Nou

The final day of La Liga. Barcelona vs Atlético Madrid.

Atlético needed only a draw to win the title.

Alexis Sánchez put Barcelona ahead.

Then, in the 49th minute, Godín headed in the equaliser.

1-1. With 90 points, Atlético won La Liga for the first time in 18 years.

**In the era of Barcelona and Real Madrid, a 4-4-2 had won the league.**

Then, in 2015-16, something nobody expected happened.

**Leicester City** won the Premier League with a 4-4-2.

Jamie Vardy ran in behind, and Okazaki pressed and linked play around him.

Vardy scored 24 league goals that season.

Between August and November 2015, he scored in **11 consecutive Premier League games** — breaking Van Nistelrooy's record of 10 from 2003.

Before the season, Leicester had been **5,000-1** to win the title.

And they won it.

```text
LEICESTER 2015-16

  VARDY    OKAZAKI   ← FRONT TWO (PRESSING + COUNTER-ATTACK)
M    M    M    M
D    D    D    D
```

But this 4-4-2 was different from the 4-4-2 of the 2000s.

**Both forwards defended and pressed.**

It was the beginning of the **modern front two** that we will look at in EP05.

# THE 4-4-2 SURVIVED BY CHANGING

---

# 9. The Change in Numbers

According to the Premier League's own analysis, around **30 per cent** of starting formations in 2019-20 had two forwards up front.

And the 4-4-2, which was the third most-used formation in 2021-22, has since seen its usage fall by **around 40 per cent.**

The front two was no longer the default.

**It had become one option among many.**

# FROM DEFAULT TO OPTION

---

# Conclusion — The Front Two Did Not Die. It Lost Its Monopoly.

No single cause killed the Second Striker.

```text
3 vs 2 IN MIDFIELD
        +
DEFENSIVE DUTIES WITHOUT THE BALL
        +
HIGH PRESSING
        +
LESS SPACE BETWEEN THE LINES
        ↓
THE FALL OF THE FRONT TWO
```

As a result, the Second Striker had to choose one of three paths:

**Become the lone striker. (Rooney)**

**Step back and become a No.10. (4-2-3-1)**

**Hand the space over to a midfielder. (Gerrard)**

> **The front two did not die. It simply lost its status as football's default.**
