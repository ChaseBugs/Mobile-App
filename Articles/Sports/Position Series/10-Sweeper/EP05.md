# SERIES 10 — The Sweeper

# EP05 — Why the Libero Died

## Sacchi, Zonal Marking and the Offside Trap — How Did the Libero's Place Disappear?

19 April 1989, San Siro. European Cup semi-final, second leg. AC Milan vs Real Madrid.

Real Madrid, led by Hugo Sánchez and Butragueño, were the strongest team in Spain.

Milan tore them apart **5-0.**

Ancelotti, Rijkaard, Gullit, Van Basten and Donadoni all scored.

Then the final at Barcelona's Camp Nou. Milan vs Steaua București.

**4-0.**

Milan were champions of Europe.

This Milan had no Libero.

**Just four defenders standing in a single line.**

---

# 1. Arrigo Sacchi — The Coach Who Had Never Been a Footballer

Sacchi had never played professional football.

He had worked as a salesman for a shoe factory.

When he became Milan's coach, many people questioned his background.

Sacchi replied:

> **“I never realised that to become a jockey you have to have been a horse first.”**

And he challenged the oldest common sense of Italian football:

**Man-marking. And the Libero.**

# THE OUTSIDER

---

# 2. Sacchi's Four Principles

Sacchi's Milan was built on four principles.

### 1. Zonal Marking

Defenders do not follow men. **They protect space.**

### 2. A Flat Back Four

Four defenders stand in a single line. There is no Libero.

### 3. A High Line

The defensive line is pushed up to shrink the distances across the whole team.

### 4. Pressing

When the ball is lost, the team presses immediately to win it back.

```text
SACCHI'S MILAN (4-4-2)

        VAN BASTEN   GULLIT
 DONADONI ANCELOTTI RIJKAARD COLOMBO
 TASSOTTI  COSTACURTA  BARESI  MALDINI   ← ONE LINE
                GK
```

# ZONE + LINE + HEIGHT + PRESS

---

# 3. Zonal Defending Does Not Need a Libero

As we saw in EP01, the Libero was **the insurance policy for man-marking.**

```text
MAN-MARKING
DF → FORWARD A
DF → FORWARD B
LIBERO → COVERS IF BEATEN

ZONAL DEFENDING
DF → LEFT ZONE
DF → LEFT-CENTRE ZONE
DF → RIGHT-CENTRE ZONE
DF → RIGHT ZONE
→ EVERYONE COVERS EVERYONE
```

In zonal defending, when the ball moves, the whole line moves together.

When one defender steps out, the player beside him tucks in to cover the space.

**Cover was no longer the job of one special player. It became the job of all four.**

# COVER BECAME EVERYONE'S JOB

---

# 4. The Offside Trap — No Room for a Libero

Sacchi's Milan used the offside trap aggressively.

When Baresi gave the signal, all four stepped up at the same moment.

```text
BARESI'S SIGNAL
        ↓
D ── D ── D ── D  → STEP UP TOGETHER
        ↓
OPPOSING FORWARD OFFSIDE
```

But the tactic had one condition:

**Every defender must stand on the same line.**

If even one Libero stays behind, the opposing forward is onside.

**The offside trap and the Libero could not exist together.**

For Sacchi, offside was not merely a defensive tool. It was **the first step in winning the ball back.**

# ONE LINE OR NOTHING

---

# 5. The Training-Ground Test

There is a famous story from Sacchi's training sessions.

He set up a goalkeeper and four defenders, led by Baresi and Maldini,

**against a full team of attackers.**

There was one condition: **they could defend using zonal marking only.**

```text
GK + 4 DF (ZONAL ONLY)
          vs
     A FULL ATTACK
```

According to the story, the attacking side could hardly score.

The test shows what Sacchi believed:

**Organised defending of space is stronger than individual ability.**

---

# 6. 1990 — The Second Title

23 May 1990, Vienna. European Cup final, Milan vs Benfica.

A Rijkaard goal won it **1-0.**

Milan had won back-to-back European Cups.

It would be a long time before any team won the competition twice in a row again.

Sacchi's Milan is still regarded as **one of the greatest club teams in history.**

And that team sent one message across Europe:

**You can win without a Libero. In fact, you can win better without one.**

# THE PROOF

---

# 7. Other Changes — The World Around the Libero

Sacchi was not the only cause.

Several changes in 1990s football made life harder for the Libero.

### The 1990 Offside Law Change

An attacker **level** with the last defender was now onside. That favoured forwards, and made the precision of a flat line even more important.

### The 1992 Back-Pass Rule

As we saw in SERIES 08, goalkeepers could no longer pick up back-passes. The space behind the defensive line came to be protected by the goalkeeper's feet and his sweeping.

### The Spread of Pressing

From the late 1990s, pressing spread across Europe. Pressing teams needed short distances between their lines, and a Libero behind the defence stretched those distances.

```text
SACCHI'S ZONAL DEFENDING
          +
THE OFFSIDE LAW CHANGE
          +
THE BACK-PASS RULE
          +
THE SPREAD OF PRESSING
          ↓
THE FALL OF THE LIBERO
```

---

# 8. Germany — The Country That Kept the Libero Longest

While most of Europe moved to zonal defending,

Germany kept the Libero for a long time.

It was, after all, the country of Beckenbauer, Sammer and Matthäus.

But as we saw in EP04, the Germany that fielded a 39-year-old Matthäus as Libero at Euro 2000 went out in the group stage.

After that, German football reformed itself from the youth system upwards.

And the result of that reform was the 2014 World Cup.

**Behind that Germany's defensive line stood not a Libero, but a sweeper keeper: Neuer.** (SERIES 08)

```text
2000  LIBERO MATTHÄUS         → GROUP-STAGE EXIT
         ↓
      GERMAN FOOTBALL REFORM
         ↓
2014  SWEEPER KEEPER NEUER    → WORLD CHAMPIONS
```

# THE LAST HOLDOUT

---

# 9. The Last Exception — Greece, 2004

Yet even after the era of the Libero had ended, there was one astonishing exception:

**Greece at Euro 2004.**

Their German coach, Otto Rehhagel, brought **man-marking** back in the 2000s.

Greece's defenders each took one of the opponent's key attackers,

and behind them, **Traianos Dellas** organised the defensive line.

Many analysts saw the Greek defence as, in effect, **a Libero system.**

Greece beat Portugal in the opening game and France in the quarter-final.

The semi-final against the Czech Republic went to extra time at 0-0.

Just before the end of the first period of extra time — **a Dellas header.**

It was **the last silver goal** in the history of the European Championship.

Then, in the final, Greece beat Portugal again, 1-0, and won the tournament.

**When everyone had forgotten the Libero, the Libero won one more time.**

# THE LAST SURPRISE

---

# Conclusion — The Libero Was Killed by an Idea

What killed the Libero was not a single player.

**It was an idea:**

> **“Covering is not one man's job. It is everyone's job.”**

Sacchi's Milan proved it.

And, ironically, the player who carried out that idea on the pitch

was Baresi — a former Libero.

> **The Libero did not lose. Every defender simply became one.**
