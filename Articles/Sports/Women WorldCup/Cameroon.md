**One-line verdict:** The **new champions of Africa**, built on a goalkeeper (Michaely Bihina, Golden Glove), a defensive structure that **kept clean sheets against Nigeria, hosts Morocco and Malawi** in the knockout rounds, and a lethal striker (Marie Ngah Manga, five goals including four on the decisive days). They are hard to score against — but they **create little in open play** against organised opponents, and lost **twice to Algeria** (1-2, 0-1) and 1-3 to Nigeria in the last 12 months.

## 5. Head Coach — Valentine Nguele

- Took the team to its **first continental title** with a defensive, disciplined plan.
- **Knockout-round model:** score early (QF 19', final 20' and 29'), then sit in a compact block and rely on Bihina. Against hosts Morocco there was no early goal, so Cameroon defended for 120 minutes and won the shoot-out.
- **Uses young players:** Nyadjou (19, scored the QF winner), Eto (21), Bihina (Benfica).

### 6.2 Defensive Strengths
- **300 minutes without conceding in the knockout rounds**, against Nigeria, Morocco and Malawi (whose Chawinga sisters had scored nine goals before the final).
- **Monique Ngock** (Fleury) screens the back four — Player of the Tournament.
- **Michaely Bihina** (Benfica) — Golden Glove; acrobatic late saves vs Nigeria; won the semi-final shoot-out 3-1.
- **Physical duels:** neutralised Tabitha and Temwa Chawinga (EA FC 27 PAC 94 and 91) in the final.

### 6.3 Attacking Routes
1. **Early direct attacks and penalties for Ngah Manga** — five WAFCON goals, two of them penalties.
2. **Set pieces** — Nyadjou's free kick vs Nigeria.
3. **Eto's late runs from midfield** — goal and assist in the final.
4. **Counter-attacks** after winning the ball in midfield.

## 10. Signature Tactics — Analysis of Five Games

### Game ① 9 August 2026, WAFCON QF — Cameroon 1-0 Nigeria

| Minute | Event |
|---|---|
| 19' | **Nyadjou free kick — 1-0** |
| 20'–70' | Cameroon drop into a 4-5-1 |
| 70'–90+' | Nigeria push for an equaliser; **Bihina's acrobatic saves** |

**How they won:** an early set-piece goal, then a deep, compact block and an excellent goalkeeper.
**Lesson:** **Do not concede first against Cameroon.** Once ahead, they defend in numbers.

### Game ② 12 August 2026, WAFCON SF — Morocco 0-0 Cameroon (Cameroon 3-1 pens)

| Phase | Event |
|---|---|
| 0'–90' | Morocco control possession in front of a home crowd; Cameroon defend deep |
| 90'–120' | Extra time; no goals |
| Shoot-out | **Cameroon 3-1** — Bihina decisive |

**How they won:** 120 minutes of defending, then the shoot-out.
**Lesson:** Cameroon **are happy to play for penalties** against stronger opponents. A team that dominates the ball must find a way to break the block inside 90 minutes.

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

### Strategy 1: Score first — or at least don't concede first
- **Evidence:** Cameroon scored first in five of six WAFCON games, then defended. When Algeria and Nigeria (in March) got ahead, Cameroon lost.
- **How:** start with full concentration; avoid fouls in the first 30 minutes (two of their goals were penalties, one a free kick).

### Strategy 2: Take Ngah Manga out of the game
- **Evidence:** 5 of 8 goals.
- **How:** a physical centre-back on her at all times; defend the box without fouling; win the first ball.

### Strategy 3: Keep the ball and move it patiently
- **Evidence:** Algeria won 2-1 and 1-0 with a possession-based, French-coached side.
- **How:** circulate the ball, pull Ngock out of position, attack the space between full-back and centre-back with overlaps.

### Strategy 4: Use pace and 1v1 skill against the full-backs
- **Evidence:** the full-backs play in Cameroon, Japan and Morocco; elite wingers are rare in African football.
- **How:** isolate the wide defenders 1v1 with your fastest players.

### Strategy 5: Win inside 90 minutes — avoid penalties
- **Evidence:** Bihina won the semi-final shoot-out 3-1.
- **How:** stay aggressive in the last 20 minutes and extra time; bring attacking substitutes on early.

### Strategy 6: Concentrate around half-time
- **Evidence:** Ngah Manga 45+3' in the final.

```text
DON'T CONCEDE FIRST (no fouls near the box)
              +
MARK NGAH MANGA PHYSICALLY
              +
PATIENT POSSESSION + FULL-BACK OVERLAPS
              +
1v1 PACE ON THE WINGS
              +
WIN IN 90 MINUTES — NOT ON PENALTIES
              ↓
CAMEROON HAVE NO PLAN B WHEN THEY ARE BEHIND
```

## 13. Conclusion

Cameroon are the **champions of Africa** — a team built on a superb goalkeeper, a disciplined midfield and a striker who takes her chances. They kept four clean sheets at WAFCON 2026, and beat Nigeria and hosts Morocco without conceding in 210 minutes.

But the model is narrow: **score early, defend, survive.** Teams that kept the ball and scored first beat them (Algeria twice, Nigeria 3-1), and the attack depends on one player.

> **Don't give Cameroon the first goal — make them chase the game, and the champions of Africa have no answer.**
