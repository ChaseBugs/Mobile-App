# SERIES 09 — The Second Striker

# EP02 — The Golden Age of the Second Striker

## Baggio, Del Piero, Bergkamp, Totti, Raúl, Zola — Why Were There So Many Great Second Strikers in One Era?

17 July 1994, Pasadena. World Cup final, Brazil vs Italy.

0-0. No goals, even after extra time.

The last penalty taker in the shootout was **Roberto Baggio.**

His kick flew over the crossbar.

Brazil were champions.

That moment became the most famous of Baggio's career.

But Baggio was also the reason Italy had reached the final at all.

Two goals against Nigeria in the round of 16.

The winner against Spain in the quarter-final.

Two goals against Bulgaria in the semi-final.

**Five goals in three knockout games.**

Italy had reached the final largely on the strength of one player.

He was not a classic striker.

He was a player who dominated games behind the forwards and in front of the midfielders.

**This is how the golden age of the Second Striker began.**

---

# 1. Why That Era? — Three Conditions for a Golden Age

In the 1990s and early 2000s, many great Second Strikers existed at the same time.

It was not a coincidence.

### Condition 1 — The 4-4-2

Almost every team played with two forwards. The Second Striker's place existed inside the system from the start.

### Condition 2 — Space Between the Lines

The gap between the defensive line and the midfield line was far wider than today. An attacking player had time to receive the ball and turn.

### Condition 3 — Serie A

In the 1990s, Serie A was the best league in the world. And Italian football had a unique culture of attacking players it called the **“fantasista.”**

```text
4-4-2 + WIDE SPACE BETWEEN THE LINES + SERIE A'S FANTASISTA CULTURE
                            ↓
           THE GOLDEN AGE OF THE SECOND STRIKER
```

# THE SYSTEM CREATED THE STARS

---

# 2. Roberto Baggio — The Divine Ponytail

Baggio was called **“Il Divin Codino”** — the Divine Ponytail.

In 1993, he won both the Ballon d'Or and FIFA World Player of the Year.

Baggio's game was the prototype of the Second Striker of that era.

**He beat defenders with his dribbling,**

**scored goals himself,**

**or played the final pass.**

He played for Juventus, AC Milan, Inter, Bologna and Brescia.

And late in his career, at little Brescia, he kept scoring well into his mid-thirties.

But one question followed Baggio throughout his career:

**“Where do we play him?”**

Many coaches struggled to fit him into their systems.

That question would later decide the fate of the Second Striker as a whole.

### 5 July 1994, Foxboro

1994 World Cup, round of 16. Italy vs Nigeria.

Nigeria led 1-0 through Amuneke's first-half goal.

Zola came off the bench in the second half — and was sent off, controversially, just 12 minutes later.

Down to ten men, Italy were on the brink of elimination.

Then, in the 88th minute, Baggio equalised.

In extra time, Baggio also scored the penalty that won the game.

2-1. Italy survived.

```text
0-1 (NIGERIA LEAD)
       ↓
ZOLA SENT OFF (10 MEN)
       ↓
88' BAGGIO EQUALISES
       ↓
102' BAGGIO PENALTY WINNER
```

**In the same match, the fates of two Italian Second Strikers split apart.** Zola was sent off. Baggio saved his team.

# GENIUS WITHOUT A FIXED POSITION

---

# 3. Gianfranco Zola — The Little Giant

Zola stood just 1.68 metres tall.

But after moving from Parma to Chelsea in 1996, he became one of the most loved foreign players in Premier League history.

His most famous moment came on 13 May 1998 in Stockholm.

The Cup Winners' Cup final, Chelsea vs Stuttgart.

Starting on the bench because of injury, Zola came on in the second half.

**Around 20 seconds after coming on,** he ran on to a Wise pass and scored the winner.

1-0. Chelsea were champions.

```text
ZOLA COMES ON
       ↓
~20 SECONDS
       ↓
WISE PASS → ZOLA SHOT
       ↓
THE WINNING GOAL
```

Zola showed another side of the Second Striker:

**A small frame, a low centre of gravity, a quick first touch — and free kicks.**

Through him, the Premier League discovered the charm of the Italian-style attacking forward.

### 16 January 2002 — The Backheel Against Norwich

FA Cup third-round replay, Chelsea vs Norwich.

Le Saux's corner came in low towards the near post.

The ball was already travelling behind Zola.

He turned and, **with his heel, flicked the ball up** and into the net.

Chelsea won 4-0.

Zola himself later called it one of those goals you could try a hundred times and never repeat.

**That is the imagination of the Second Striker: not a planned solution, but a solution visible only in that moment.**

# SMALL BODY, BIG MIND

---

# 4. Raúl — The Second Striker Who Scores

Raúl was a different type.

He was not a spectacular dribbler.

But he knew exactly **when and where to be in the box.**

At Real Madrid, he recorded:

**741 appearances — a club record**

**323 goals**

**Three Champions League titles (1998, 2000, 2002)**

And he scored in both the 2000 and 2002 Champions League finals.

```text
2000 FINAL  Real Madrid 3-0 Valencia    → RAÚL SCORES
2002 FINAL  Real Madrid 2-1 Leverkusen  → RAÚL SCORES
```

Morientes handled the aerial balls and the physical battles,

and Raúl scored in the space around him.

Raúl showed that a Second Striker does not have to be a playmaker.

# THE SECOND STRIKER WHO FINISHES

---

# 5. Del Piero — The Artist Inside the System

Del Piero, whom we met in EP01, is the classic example of a front-two partnership.

At Juventus in the early 2000s, his partner was **David Trezeguet.**

Trezeguet was a pure finisher in the box.

Del Piero cut in from the left, dropped deep to receive the ball, and created chances for Trezeguet.

And he scored plenty himself.

```text
DEL PIERO                       TREZEGUET
DROPS DEEP TO RECEIVE     →     RUNS INTO THE BOX
CUTS IN FROM THE FLANK    →     FINISHES
FREE KICKS / LONG SHOTS
```

In 2001-02, Trezeguet finished as Serie A's joint top scorer with 24 goals, and Juventus won the league.

### 26 November 1996, Tokyo

Intercontinental Cup, Juventus vs River Plate.

In the 81st minute, Del Piero scored the only goal of the game.

Juventus, who had won the Champions League that spring, were now champions of the world.

Del Piero was named man of the match. He was 22 years old.

# THE CREATOR AND THE FINISHER

---

# 6. Bergkamp — A New Language for England

When Bergkamp arrived at Arsenal in 1995, English football was still built on long balls and physical battles.

Bergkamp brought a different language:

**The first touch.**

**Spatial awareness.**

**Passes made for forwards.**

1998 World Cup quarter-final. The Netherlands vs Argentina.

Moments before the end, Frank de Boer hit a long pass from near the halfway line.

Bergkamp killed it in the air with his first touch, beat the defender with his second, and scored with his third.

**Three touches. One decision.**

### August 1997 — The Filbert Street Hat-Trick

Early in the 1997-98 season, away at Leicester. A 3-3 draw.

Bergkamp scored a hat-trick, and his three goals took first, second and third place in BBC *Match of the Day*'s Goal of the Month for August.

The third was the most famous: he controlled a dropping ball with his first touch, beat the defender, and finished.

**That season, Bergkamp was named PFA Players' Player of the Year.**

Then Henry arrived at Arsenal.

Henry attacked the space behind the defence with his pace.

Bergkamp received the ball in the space in front of it and found him.

That combination became the heart of the 2003-04 “Invincibles,” who went the whole league season unbeaten.

Henry was the league's top scorer that season with 30 goals.

*(The Henry–Bergkamp partnership is covered in detail in the EP06 SPECIAL.)*

# THE PASS BEFORE THE GOAL

---

# 7. Totti — Roma Itself

Totti played 25 seasons, all for Roma.

He scored 250 goals in Serie A alone — the second-highest total in the league's history.

In 2000-01, Roma signed Batistuta.

Batistuta took on the opposing centre-backs, and Totti built the game in the space behind them.

Roma won Serie A that season.

It was only the third league title in the club's history.

### 17 June 2001, the Olimpico

The final day of the Serie A season. Roma vs Parma.

Roma were two points clear of second-placed Juventus.

19 minutes: Totti. 39 minutes: Montella. 78 minutes: Batistuta.

3-1. Roma were champions.

```text
19'  TOTTI       ← SECOND STRIKER
39'  MONTELLA    ← THE THIRD FORWARD
78'  BATISTUTA   ← TARGET / FINISHER
```

**In the game that decided the title, all three of Roma's forwards scored.** It showed the depth of attack in the age of the front two.

Totti was one of the most complete Second Strikers of the era.

**Passing, shooting, vision, leadership.**

And as we saw in EP01, he later became the first great symbol of the Second Striker turning into a False 9.

# ONE CLUB, EVERY ROLE

---

# 8. The Formula of the Front Two — Big and Small

The most common formula for a front two in that era was **“Big Man + Little Man.”**

```text
BIG MAN (TARGET)              LITTLE MAN (SECOND STRIKER)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WINS AERIAL DUELS             WINS THE SECOND BALL
HOLDS UP PLAY                 RUNS BETWEEN THE LINES
DRAGS CENTRE-BACKS AWAY       USES THE EMPTY SPACE
```

In England, the Heskey–Owen partnership was famous for the 5-1 win over Germany in Munich in 2001, when Owen scored a hat-trick.

Manchester United won the 1998-99 treble with Yorke and Cole up front.

And in Italy, pairings such as Batistuta–Totti, Trezeguet–Del Piero and Inzaghi–Shevchenko dominated Serie A and Europe.

# TWO PLAYERS, ONE ATTACK

---

# 9. The Weakness of the Golden Age

But even the golden age had a problem:

**Defending.**

Many Second Strikers barely defended when they did not have the ball.

In a 4-4-2, when the ball was lost, the four midfielders and four defenders did the defending.

The front two waited up the pitch.

```text
4-4-2 OUT OF POSSESSION

  ST    SS     ← WAITING
  M  M  M  M   ← DEFENDING
  D  D  D  D   ← DEFENDING
```

That made it easy to end up **2 vs 3 in central midfield.**

As opponents began to use three central midfielders, this weakness grew bigger and bigger.

And that was the beginning of the Second Striker's decline.

# THE WEAKNESS INSIDE THE GOLDEN AGE

---

# Conclusion — A Golden Age Built by the System

Baggio, Zola, Raúl, Del Piero, Bergkamp, Totti.

Each of them was a different type of Second Striker.

```text
BAGGIO     → DRIBBLING + CREATION
ZOLA       → TECHNIQUE + SET PIECES
RAÚL       → MOVEMENT + FINISHING
DEL PIERO  → CUTTING INSIDE + ARTISTRY
BERGKAMP   → FIRST TOUCH + VISION
TOTTI      → EVERYTHING
```

But they were all born into the same world:

**The 4-4-2, wide space between the lines, and two forwards.**

> **They were born inside a system — and when the system changed, they began to lose their place.**
