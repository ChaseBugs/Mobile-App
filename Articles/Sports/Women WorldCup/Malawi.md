**One-line verdict:** A **two-player team with world-class pace**. The Chawinga sisters scored **9 of Malawi's 12 WAFCON goals** and are rated 89 and 87 in EA FC 27, above every player in most squads at this World Cup. Behind them is a **domestic-league defence** that conceded **10 goals in six WAFCON games** and **five to Australia**. Stop the sisters and Malawi have very little; leave them space and they will beat anyone.

- **153rd** (16 June 2026) — the lowest-ranked team at the World Cup. Highest-ever 110th (2017–18); lowest 159th (2023).
- The ranking badly understates their tournament strength. They reached the WAFCON final on debut and beat Nigeria (the record champions) along the way.

## 5. Head Coach — Lovemore Fazili

- In charge since 2023; took Malawi from 159th in the world to a WAFCON final.
- **The plan is simple:** a compact 4-4-2 or 4-5-1 without the ball, then fast transitions to the Chawinga sisters.
- **Tactical flexibility is limited** — against Cameroon, who blocked the transition, Malawi had no other route to goal.

## 6. Tactics and Formation

### 6.1 Shapes

```text
IN POSSESSION (4-4-2 / 4-2-3-1)

           TABITHA CHAWINGA            TEMWA CHAWINGA
   CHIKUPIRA / HENRY      KADZERE        CHINZIMU      SIMWAKA
              (Kadzere and Chinzimu are the link midfielders)
  PHIRI       KHUMALO      MADISE        CHAVULA / PHIKANI
                          SIKELO

OUT OF POSSESSION (4-5-1 / 4-4-2 LOW BLOCK)

                     TEMWA (stays high)
   TABITHA (left)  KADZERE   CHINZIMU   NGULUBE   RIGHT MF
  PHIRI     KHUMALO     MADISE     CHAVULA
                          SIKELO
```

### 6.2 Attacking Routes
1. **Ball into space for Temwa** (EA PAC 91) — long passes over a high line or balls down the channel.
2. **Tabitha's 1v1 dribbling from the left** (PAC 94, DRI 86) — she cuts inside to shoot and scored a bicycle kick vs Algeria.
3. **Combination play between the sisters**.
4. **Second-half surges** — 8 of 12 goals came after half-time, when opponents tire.
5. **Kadzere's late runs** (Montpellier) — two goals including the QF winner (79').

### 6.3 Defensive Structure
- A deep block, with a domestic-league back four (Silver Strikers, Kukoma Ntopwa, TP Mazembe).
- **Weak against early pressure:** conceded at 2' (Zambia), 7' (Ghana) and 20' (Cameroon).
- **Concentration at the end:** conceded twice in stoppage time vs Nigeria (90+2' pen, 90+9').

### 6.4 Game-State Behaviour

| State | What Malawi do | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0-0 early | Defend deep; look for counters to the sisters | Every WAFCON game |
| Trailing early | **Can come back** — the sisters can produce a goal from nothing | Ghana: 0-1 at 7' → 2-1 win |
| Trailing by two | **No answer** when the transition is blocked | Zambia 1-2, Cameroon 0-3 |
| Leading late | Defend deep, invite pressure, concede late | Nigeria: 3-0 → 3-2 (90+2', 90+9') |

**Lesson:** **The sisters are most dangerous after the 70th minute.** But Malawi also conceded twice in stoppage time, so their own concentration fades.

**Lesson:** **This is the blueprint for beating Malawi.** Score first, defend deep so there is no space in behind, double-mark the sisters, and they produce nothing.

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

### Strategy 1: Score early
- **Evidence:** Zambia (2') and Cameroon (20', 29') beat them; Malawi's plan collapses when they must attack.
- **How:** start at maximum intensity; press the back four, which is uncomfortable on the ball.

### Strategy 2: Defend deep — never leave space behind
- **Evidence:** PAC 94 and 91. Cameroon defended deep and kept them scoreless; Algeria's exposed full-back was sent off.
- **How:** a mid-to-low line, no offside trap against the sisters, a sweeper-style covering centre-back.

### Strategy 3: Double-mark both sisters
- **Evidence:** 9 of 12 goals.
- **How:** the full-back plus a holding midfielder on Tabitha (left side); a centre-back plus a screening midfielder on Temwa. Never 1v1.

### Strategy 4: Attack the space they leave
- **Evidence:** the sisters' DEF ratings are 46 and 36; they do not track back.
- **How:** overload Malawi's left side (behind Tabitha) with your right-back and right winger.

### Strategy 5: Stay focused after the 70th minute
- **Evidence:** 73', 76', 79', 90+5' — the sisters' best moments.
- **How:** fresh defensive substitutes around 65'; keep the defensive shape when ahead.

### Strategy 6: Don't foul near the box — but don't let them run
- **Evidence:** Algeria's red card in the semi-final.
- **How:** tactical fouls in midfield, never last-man challenges.

      +

      +

      +

      ↓

## 13. Conclusion

Malawi are the World Cup's biggest outlier: ranked 153rd, but with **two of the most dangerous forwards in the tournament**. The Chawinga sisters beat Nigeria and Algeria almost by themselves.

Cameroon showed how to stop them: **score early, sit deep, and double up on the sisters.** Do that, and a domestic-league defence that conceded 10 goals at WAFCON and five against Australia will crack.

> **Respect the sisters, ignore the ranking — and attack the rest of the team.**
