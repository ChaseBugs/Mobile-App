# From Control to Power  
## How Real Madrid’s New Midfield Generation Is Replacing the Kroos–Casemiro–Modrić Era

Real Madrid’s transition from **Casemiro–Toni Kroos–Luka Modrić** to a new generation built around **Federico Valverde, Eduardo Camavinga, Aurélien Tchouaméni, and Jude Bellingham** is much more than a generational change.

It represents a change in football philosophy.

The legendary KCM midfield was designed to **control matches**.

The new generation is increasingly designed to **attack space directly**.

KCM controlled opponents through passing, positioning, rhythm, press resistance, and experience.

The new generation offers something different:

**speed, power, athleticism, verticality, ball carrying, and enormous spatial coverage.**

In simple terms:

> **KCM moved the ball into space. The new generation can carry the ball into space themselves.**

This represents the next stage of Real Madrid’s midfield evolution:

# **Speed → Balance → Control → Power**

---

# 1. KCM: Move the Ball, Move the Opponent

At their peak, Real Madrid’s midfield structure appeared relatively simple:

```text id="u15fxl"
          Kroos        Modrić

               Casemiro
```

But the three players possessed remarkably complementary qualities.

**Casemiro = Protection**

**Kroos = Control**

**Modrić = Progression**

Casemiro protected the defensive structure and stopped transitions.

Kroos controlled possession and dictated the rhythm of matches.

Modrić connected phases, escaped pressure, and progressed the ball.

Together, they formed one of the most balanced midfield structures in modern Champions League history.

But the key principle was this:

> **Madrid could move the opponent by moving the ball.**

Kroos was the perfect example.

Imagine an opponent aggressively pressing Madrid's left side:

```text id="od2i4v"
Opponent Press
    ↓ ↓ ↓

Marcelo — Kroos
            \
             \
              \ 40m pass
               \
                → Carvajal
```

Kroos himself barely needed to move.

Yet one pass could completely change the geography of the match.

The opponent now had to shift 30 or 40 metres across the pitch.

That was the essence of Kroos-era control.

---

# 2. The New Generation Is Fundamentally Different

Now consider Madrid’s next midfield generation:

**Federico Valverde**

**Eduardo Camavinga**

**Aurélien Tchouaméni**

**Jude Bellingham**

There is an obvious pattern.

All four possess exceptional combinations of:

- athleticism
- acceleration
- physical strength
- ball carrying
- transition ability
- large-space coverage
- defensive mobility
- positional flexibility

These are not direct replacements for KCM.

Madrid did not simply find another Casemiro, another Kroos, and another Modrić.

Instead, they assembled midfielders capable of solving the same football problems through completely different methods.

The contrast can be simplified:

```text id="w4cnra"
OLD GENERATION

Kroos
  ↓
PASS
  ↓
Ball moves into space


NEW GENERATION

Camavinga / Valverde / Bellingham
  ↓
CARRY
  ↓
Player moves into space
```

That distinction is fundamental.

```text id="h1szgj"
  ↓

  ↓
```

Madrid beat the press through positioning, passing angles, and circulation.

Camavinga can solve the same problem differently.

```text id="xyglg5"
  ↓

  ↓
```

The method is completely different.

Kroos manipulated pressure by moving the ball.

Camavinga can physically escape it.

And once he escapes, the opponent faces a serious problem.

If Camavinga beats one midfielder:


One successful carry can immediately change the numerical structure of an attack.

That is why ball carrying has become such an important weapon for the new Madrid.

# 4. Valverde: The Midfielder Who Can Attack an Entire Side of the Pitch

Federico Valverde may be the clearest symbol of this new philosophy.

Calling him simply a central midfielder does not fully explain his tactical value.

Depending on the situation, Valverde can function as:

```text id="q9khr4"
Central Midfielder
       ↓
Right Midfielder
       ↓
Right Winger
       ↓
Full-back Cover
       ↓
Box-to-Box Runner
```

His extraordinary stamina and acceleration allow him to influence enormous areas of the pitch.

On the right:

```text id="d9h2ft"
             Rodrygo
                 ↑

        Valverde ─────→

                 ↑
              Carvajal
```

Valverde can support the winger, cover the full-back, press the opposition, and still arrive in the final third.

And when Madrid recover possession:

```text id="rd5prq"
Ball Recovery
      ↓
Valverde
      ↓
RUN
      ↓
RUN
      ↓
RUN
      ↓
Final Third
```

This is a fundamentally different type of midfield progression.

Kroos often moved the game through the ball.

Valverde can move the game through **physical acceleration**.

# 5. Camavinga: Press Resistance Through Acceleration

Camavinga shares one important quality with Modrić:

**press resistance.**

But their methods are different.

Modrić often escapes pressure through technique, body orientation, subtle changes of direction, and extraordinary first-touch control.

```text id="6dxh1y"
Pressure
   ↓
Modrić
   ↓
First Touch
   ↓
Body Feint
   ↓
Turn
   ↓
Pass
```

Camavinga can be more explosive.

```text id="i3g90k"
Pressure
   ↓
Camavinga
   ↓
Turn
   ↓
Acceleration
   ↓
Carry
   ↓
Line Broken
```

Modrić often **dismantles pressure**.

Camavinga can sometimes **run through pressure**.

This makes him especially dangerous when the game becomes stretched.

Give Camavinga 20 metres of open grass and the opponent suddenly has a transition problem.

# 6. Tchouaméni Is Not Simply the New Casemiro

When Aurélien Tchouaméni arrived, the obvious comparison was:

**Casemiro → Tchouaméni.**

But stylistically, the comparison is incomplete.

Casemiro was extraordinary at destroying opposition attacks.

His game could be simplified as:

```text id="ut47eu"
DANGER
  ↓
READ
  ↓
INTERCEPT
  ↓
DUEL
  ↓
DESTROY
```

Tchouaméni offers a somewhat different profile.

He can defend physically, compete aerially, intercept passes, and protect the centre-backs.

But he is also comfortable participating in the first phase of build-up.

```text id="1qbh1d"
Centre-Back
     ↓
Tchouaméni
 ↙     ↓      ↘
Pass  Carry  Switch

       +

 Positioning
 Duels
 Interceptions
```

Casemiro was closer to a specialist defensive destroyer.

Tchouaméni has the potential to operate as a more hybrid:

**Pivot + Physical No.6.**

Madrid therefore did not simply clone Casemiro.

They changed the profile of the position.

# 7. Bellingham Changed the Entire Structure

Then Jude Bellingham arrived.

This was perhaps the most important development of the new generation.

Bellingham is difficult to classify using traditional midfield labels.

He can behave like:

**No.8**

**No.10**

**Box-to-box midfielder**

**Second striker**

**Late penalty-area runner**

That flexibility became extremely important after Karim Benzema left Madrid.

Ancelotti could construct a midfield diamond:

```text id="8etnhx"
          Vinícius     Rodrygo

               Bellingham

          Kroos       Valverde

              Tchouaméni
```

Bellingham was not simply asked to create chances.

He attacked the penalty area.

```text id="4yijx4"
Midfield
   ↓
Bellingham
   ↓
RUN
   ↓
Between Defenders
   ↓
Penalty Area
   ↓
FINISH
```

Madrid had lost Benzema's central attacking presence.

Instead of finding an identical replacement, they changed the structure.

A midfielder began supplying part of the missing goal threat.

That is an important characteristic of Madrid’s recent squad building:

> **Do not necessarily replace the player. Replace the function through a new system.**

---

# 8. Open Space Has Become Madrid’s Greatest Weapon

The KCM Madrid was already extremely dangerous in transition.

But the new generation makes open-field situations even more interesting because the midfielders themselves become transition weapons.

Imagine Madrid recovering possession against a high defensive line:

```text id="qvxscz"
           BALL RECOVERY
                 ↓

────────────────────────────
       40 METRES OF SPACE
────────────────────────────

Vinícius      →→→→→

Bellingham    →→→→→

Valverde      →→→→→

Camavinga     →→→→→
```

This is an extraordinary amount of speed and power.

In earlier Madrid teams, Ronaldo and Bale provided much of the transition explosion.

Now the midfielders themselves can attack 30 or 40 metres of space.

The transition is no longer:

**Midfielder passes → Forward runs.**

It can become:

**Midfielder wins → Midfielder carries → Forward attacks → Another midfielder arrives.**

That creates waves of runners.

# 12. Kroos’s Retirement Marked the End of the Old Control System

Toni Kroos’s retirement in 2024 represented another major turning point.

Even while Madrid were introducing younger, more athletic midfielders, Kroos remained the strongest connection to the previous philosophy.

Around him were:

```text id="tgw3dn"
Bellingham
Valverde
Camavinga
Tchouaméni

       +

     KROOS

       ↓

Tempo Control
Distribution
Game Management
```

Kroos allowed Madrid to slow the game down.

He could decide when not to attack.

He could escape pressure through passing rather than running.

He could move opponents without physically moving himself very far.

Once he disappeared, a major tactical question emerged:

> **Who controls the tempo now?**

Who determines when Madrid should accelerate?

Who determines when they should slow down?

Who manipulates an opponent's press through passing?

Who controls the entire match rather than simply winning individual moments?

These are questions athleticism alone cannot answer.

# 13. The New Generation Is Not Automatically Better Than KCM

This distinction is essential.

The new midfield may possess superior physical characteristics in several areas.

But that does not automatically make it superior to KCM.

Kroos and Modrić possessed something extremely difficult to replicate:

# **Decision-Making**

Imagine Madrid leading 2–1 after 80 minutes.

The opponent begins pressing aggressively.

A young, vertical midfielder may see space and think:

```text id="0v32mx"
SPACE
  ↓
ATTACK
  ↓
RUN
```

But Kroos might see exactly the same space and decide:

```text id="qjtvjs"
SPACE
  ↓
DON'T ATTACK
  ↓
KEEP BALL
  ↓
DRAW PRESS
  ↓
SWITCH PLAY
  ↓
WIN FOUL
  ↓
KILL 60 SECONDS
```

That is Champions League game management.

Sometimes the correct decision is not to attack the available space.

Sometimes the best attack is to deny the opponent another attack.

That intelligence was one of KCM's greatest strengths.

# 16. Speed → Balance → Control → Power

Real Madrid’s tactical evolution can therefore be summarized in four words:

# **Speed → Balance → Control → Power**

Mourinho gave Madrid **speed**.

Ancelotti combined that speed with **balance**.

Zidane’s KCM midfield perfected **control**.

The new generation introduces extraordinary **power**.

But there is one final stage still to be completed.

And it may be the most difficult.

---

# 17. The Final Challenge: Power + Control

The future of Madrid's midfield is not simply about replacing KCM with younger athletes.

The real challenge is combining the advantages of both generations.

Imagine:

```text id="us4f0n"
Valverde
   ↓
Power + Running


Camavinga
   ↓
Press Resistance + Carrying


Tchouaméni
   ↓

Bellingham
   ↓

           +

KCM-Level

Tempo Control

           ↓

      COMPLETE MIDFIELD
```

That is the next tactical challenge.

Valverde, Camavinga, Tchouaméni, and Bellingham can run faster, cover huge spaces, carry the ball, attack transitions, and dominate physically.

But Kroos's control of rhythm and Modrić's understanding of game states cannot simply be replaced by athleticism.

The younger generation must develop its own version of control.

Not by becoming Kroos.

Not by becoming Modrić.

But by learning when to use its extraordinary physical power — and when **not** to use it.

---

# Conclusion: Real Madrid Are Not Replacing KCM — They Are Building Something Different

Real Madrid did not replace Casemiro, Kroos, and Modrić with three younger copies.

They changed the architecture.

KCM dominated through:

**positioning, passing, rhythm, press resistance, protection, and experience.**

The new generation increasingly dominates through:

**speed, strength, vertical running, ball carrying, athletic coverage, and positional flexibility.**

The difference can be summarized simply:

> **KCM moved the ball to attack space.**

> **The new generation can attack the space themselves.**

That is why the evolution from:

**Casemiro–Kroos–Modrić**

to:

**Valverde–Camavinga–Tchouaméni–Bellingham**

is not merely a changing of the guard.

It represents another transformation in Real Madrid's football philosophy.

The club's modern midfield evolution can be traced as:

# **Mourinho: Speed**
# **Ancelotti: Balance**
# **Zidane: Control**
# **New Generation: Power**

But the ultimate objective should not be **Control → Power**.

It should be:

# **Control + Power**

If this generation can combine the athletic superiority of Valverde, Camavinga, Tchouaméni, and Bellingham with the decision-making and game management that defined Kroos and Modrić, Real Madrid will not have recreated KCM.

They will have built something entirely different.

And potentially something just as formidable.
