# SERIES 08 — The Evolution of the Goalkeeper

# EP05 — Shot-Stopper vs Ball-Playing Goalkeeper

## What Makes a Good Goalkeeper?

28 May 2022, Paris, Stade de France. Champions League final, Liverpool vs Real Madrid.

Liverpool pushed Real Madrid back for most of the match.

Salah, Mané and Díaz kept shooting.

But in front of every shot stood one goalkeeper:

**Thibaut Courtois.**

Courtois made nine saves that night — according to Opta, the most by any goalkeeper in a Champions League final since 2003-04.

Real Madrid won 1-0 through a single Vinícius goal.

Courtois was named player of the match.

Courtois is not a goalkeeper who starts attacks with 70-metre passes like Ederson.

Real Madrid did not need that kind of goalkeeper.

**What they needed was a goalkeeper who stopped goals at the decisive moment.**

Three years later, another Champions League winner made the opposite decision.

PSG let their title-winning goalkeeper Donnarumma go and chose the ball-playing Chevalier.

And one year after that, PSG won the Champions League again — with a third goalkeeper, Safonov.

So the question is:

> **What makes a good goalkeeper?**

---

# 1. Two Types of Goalkeeper

The modern goalkeeper debate usually divides keepers into two types.

### Shot-Stopper

A goalkeeper whose greatest strengths are shot-stopping, 1v1 situations, aerial balls and command of the box.

Examples: Courtois, Oblak, Donnarumma, De Gea, Safonov

### Ball-Playing Goalkeeper

A goalkeeper whose greatest strengths are build-up, press resistance, long passing and sweeping.

Examples: Ederson, ter Stegen, Chevalier

```text
SHOT-STOPPER              BALL-PLAYING GK
SHOT-STOPPING ★★★★★      SHOT-STOPPING ★★★★
FOOTWORK      ★★★          FOOTWORK      ★★★★★
```

Of course, real goalkeepers are not divided so simply.

And some goalkeepers, like Alisson and Raya, are elite at both.

But each goalkeeper's **greatest weapon** is clearly different.

---

# 2. Real Cases — Same Question, Different Answers

Over the last decade, elite clubs have given different answers to this question.

```text
YEAR   CLUB          OUT                       IN
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2016   Man City      Hart (SAVES)          → Bravo (FEET)
2017   Man City      Bravo                 → Ederson (FEET + SAVES)
2018   Liverpool     Karius                → Alisson (FEET + SAVES)
2023   Man United    De Gea (SAVES)        → Onana (FEET)
2023   Arsenal       Ramsdale              → Raya (FEET + SAVES)
2025   PSG           Donnarumma (SAVES)    → Chevalier (FEET)
2025   Man City      Ederson (FEET)        → Donnarumma (SAVES)
2026   PSG           Chevalier (FEET)      → Safonov (SAVES)
```

Most clubs moved in the direction of **footwork.**

But the results were different.

**Bravo** failed.

**Ederson and Alisson** succeeded.

**Onana** had a difficult start.

**Raya** won three consecutive Golden Gloves and the league title.

**Chevalier** lost his place within a season.

And in 2025, Guardiola's City actually chose a shot-stopper.

This table reveals one fact:

**There is no single right answer.**

---

# 3. The Team's Style Decides the Goalkeeper

Why do teams make different choices?

The key is **what kind of football the team plays.**

```text
POSSESSION TEAMS (City, Barcelona, PSG)
→ FACE FEW SHOTS
→ GK SPENDS A LOT OF TIME ON THE BALL WITH HIS FEET
→ VALUE OF THE BALL-PLAYING GK ↑

COUNTER-ATTACKING / LOW-BLOCK TEAMS
→ FACE MANY SHOTS
→ GK HAS MANY MOMENTS WHERE HE MUST MAKE SAVES
→ VALUE OF THE SHOT-STOPPER ↑
```

The goalkeeper of a team with 70% possession may face only two or three shots in a game.

But he may touch the ball with his feet dozens of times.

The goalkeeper of a team that sits in a low block, on the other hand, may face seven or eight shots on target.

# THE SYSTEM DEFINES THE GOALKEEPER

---

# 4. Real Madrid's Answer — Saves Win Trophies

Real Madrid have long given an answer built around Courtois.

Ancelotti's Real Madrid were not a team that controlled possession perfectly.

They were a team that survived difficult moments and won at the decisive ones.

For a team like that, Courtois was the best insurance policy.

The nine saves in the 2022 Champions League final proved it.

In August 2023, Courtois ruptured his anterior cruciate ligament.

Lunin kept goal for most of that season, and Real Madrid, with Courtois back for the final weeks, beat Dortmund 2-0 in the 2024 final at Wembley to win again.

Dortmund created several good chances in the first half, but could not get past Courtois.

Then came 2025-26.

Real Madrid had a difficult season.

Xabi Alonso was dismissed in January, they were knocked out of the Champions League by Bayern in the quarter-finals, and they finished a distant second to Barcelona in the league.

Yet even in that season, Courtois kept 17 clean sheets in 45 appearances.

In a 1-0 defeat at Anfield, he made eight saves.

**He showed that when a team starts to fall, the goalkeeper is the last player to collapse.**

# BIG SAVES WIN BIG GAMES

---

# 5. PSG's Answer — And What Actually Happened

PSG's decision went in the opposite direction.

Straight after winning the Champions League, Luis Enrique chose Chevalier over Donnarumma for the sake of build-up.

But Chevalier looked vulnerable on crosses and set pieces, and by the middle of the season he had lost his place to Safonov.

Then PSG won back-to-back Champions League titles with Safonov in goal.

```text
PSG 2025-26

PLAN:    A FOOTWORK GK (CHEVALIER)
REALITY: SHOT-STOPPING ERRORS → LOSS OF TRUST
RESULT:  A SHOT-STOPPING GK (SAFONOV) → CL CHAMPIONS
```

This case is one of the most important lessons of this series.

**Footwork cannot replace shot-stopping.**

**A club may change its goalkeeper for the build-up — but a goalkeeper keeps his place with his saves.**

# FIT MATTERS — BUT THE THRESHOLD COMES FIRST

---

# 6. The Value of Shot-Stopping Can Be Measured

Modern analysis evaluates a goalkeeper's shot-stopping like this:

**PSxG (Post-Shot Expected Goals)**

The probability that a shot becomes a goal once it is struck, given where it is heading and how hard it is hit.

**PSxG − Goals Conceded**

If this number is positive, the goalkeeper prevented more goals than an average goalkeeper would have.

```text
EXAMPLE

PSxG:            40
GOALS CONCEDED:  32
→ +8 (8 GOALS PREVENTED ABOVE AVERAGE)
```

A goalkeeper who prevents eight extra goals over a season can bring his team a significant number of points.

That was at the heart of Liverpool's calculation when they invested so heavily in Alisson.

---

# 7. The Value of Footwork Is Hard to Measure

The value of footwork, on the other hand, is much harder to measure.

A goalkeeper's good pass can produce results like:

**Escaping the press**

**Keeping possession**

**The opponent abandoning its press**

**A 1v1 chance behind the defensive line**

But many of these never appear directly on the stat sheet.

In particular, the greatest value is often **an invisible effect.**

If the opponent knows that pressing the goalkeeper is useless,

it stops pressing altogether.

```text
GOOD BALL-PLAYING GK
         ↓
OPPONENT'S PRESS FAILS AGAIN AND AGAIN
         ↓
OPPONENT ABANDONS THE PRESS
         ↓
OUR TEAM CONTROLS POSSESSION
```

# THE INVISIBLE VALUE

---

# 8. The Mistakes Are Different

The two types of goalkeeper also make different kinds of mistakes.

**The shot-stopper's risk**

Under pressure, he only kicks long balls and keeps losing possession.

The team has to keep defending.

**The ball-playing goalkeeper's risk**

He makes a mistake during build-up and concedes immediately.

```text
SHOT-STOPPER MISTAKE  → LOSS OF POSSESSION (REPEATED, LESS VISIBLE)
BALL-PLAYING MISTAKE  → IMMEDIATE GOAL (RARE, VERY VISIBLE)
```

A ball-playing goalkeeper's mistakes are more dramatic, and remembered more.

But the meaningless long balls of a shot-stopper also add up to a big cost over a season.

# DIFFERENT ERRORS, DIFFERENT COSTS

---

# 9. The Threshold — Both Must Be Good Enough

In modern elite football, there are almost no goalkeepers who can do only one of the two.

Courtois can do the basic build-up that modern football requires.

Ederson can make world-class saves.

```text
CONDITIONS OF THE ELITE GOALKEEPER

SHOT-STOPPING: ABOVE THE THRESHOLD (ESSENTIAL)
FOOTWORK:      ABOVE THE THRESHOLD (ESSENTIAL)
+ WORLD-CLASS IN AT LEAST ONE AREA
```

Bravo failed because his shot-stopping fell below the threshold.

Onana struggled because of his reliability at decisive moments.

The same is true of Chevalier: his build-up suited the system, but his reliability at decisive moments did not reach the threshold.

On the other hand, a goalkeeper who can barely use his feet, like a 2000s keeper, would find it very hard to survive at a modern elite club.

# THRESHOLD + SPECIALTY

---

# 10. If You Had to Choose One?

If you really had to choose one?

This series' answer is:

**Saves come first.**

The reason is simple.

Build-up mistakes can be reduced by the system.

The centre-backs can drop deeper, the No.6 can help, and the team can choose to go long.

But the decisive shot in front of goal **can be stopped by nobody except the goalkeeper.**

```text
BUILD-UP WEAKNESS → THE TEAM CAN HELP
SAVING WEAKNESS   → NOBODY CAN HELP
```

But that does not mean “footwork does not matter.”

**Shot-stopping is the goalkeeper's foundation. Footwork is the goalkeeper's extension.**

# SAVES ARE THE FOUNDATION. FEET ARE THE EXTENSION.

---

# 11. Five Questions for Evaluating a Modern Goalkeeper

When you evaluate a goalkeeper, ask these five questions.

### 1. Does he save the shots he should save? (Shot-Stopping)

### 2. Does he rescue his team at the decisive moments? (Big Moments)

### 3. Can he protect the space behind the defence? (Sweeping)

### 4. Does he make the right choice under pressure? (Distribution)

### 5. Does he fit our team's system? (System Fit)

The last of these five questions is becoming more and more important.

But as PSG learned, it can never replace the first.

---

# 12. Series Summary — The Evolution of the Goalkeeper

Now let us summarise the whole of SERIES 08.

```text
1992  BACK-PASS RULE
        ↓
# SHOT-STOPPER (Buffon · Casillas · Čech)
        ↓
HIGH DEFENSIVE LINES
        ↓
# SWEEPER KEEPER (Neuer)
        ↓
GOAL-KICK LAW CHANGE · MAN-TO-MAN PRESSING
        ↓
# BALL-PLAYING GOALKEEPER (Ederson · ter Stegen)
        ↓
SAVES + SWEEPING + BUILD-UP
        ↓
# COMPLETE GOALKEEPER (Alisson · Raya)
        ↓
# SYSTEM GOALKEEPER (2025 — THE SYSTEM CHOOSES THE GOALKEEPER)
        ↓
# THE THRESHOLD RETURNS (2026 — SAVES STILL COME FIRST)
```

The goalkeeper was the position that stayed the same the longest.

But over the last 30 years, it has become one of the positions that changed most dramatically.

---

# Conclusion — A Good Goalkeeper Stops Goals. A Great Goalkeeper Changes the Team.

There is no single definition of a good goalkeeper.

For Real Madrid, Courtois was the answer.

For Manchester City, Ederson was the answer for many years.

For Arsenal, Raya was the answer — and he brought them the league title.

PSG chose Chevalier, then won the Champions League with Safonov. City chose Donnarumma.

But every answer has one thing in common:

**The goalkeeper cannot be separated from the team's football.**

And the final idea of the whole series is this:

> **A good goalkeeper stops goals.
> A great goalkeeper allows his team to attempt more.**
