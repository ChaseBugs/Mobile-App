## 5. Head Coach — Christian Wück

```text
Long career in German youth football — won the men's U-17 Euro and U-17 World Cup (2023)
Oct 2024  Appointed women's national coach (after Horst Hrubesch)
Jul 2025  Euro semi-finals despite two red cards
Dec 2025  Nations League final
Jun 2026  Qualified unbeaten
```

### Profile
- **Shape-shifter:** 4-2-3-1 base; switched to 4-1-4-1 at half-time vs France (Oct 2025); late **3-2-4-1 "push"** with no recognised right-back.
- **In-game problem-solver:** vs Japan he changed two players at half-time (Minge, Kössler on) and "slightly adjusted the midfield shape" — Germany turned 0-1 into 2-1.
- **Rewards hunger and depth:** "You can see how hungry the girls are" (after Australia); gave debuts to Küver and Kössler, who both scored.
- **Risk:** his attacking changes **open counter space** — after the 60' double substitution vs France, "the French made more of the space."

                    ANYOMI / SCHÜLLER

  KETT      KLEINHERNE     MINGE      GWINN

### 6.2 Pressing — Winning the Ball High

| Element | Evidence |
|---|---|
| High press on the opponent's build-up | vs Japan: "Germany's first threat came from quick transitions after winning the ball high up the pitch"; **73' winner came after Japan lost the ball building from the back** |
| Mid-block when the press is bypassed | 4-1-4-1 with Senß screening |

### 6.3 Attacking Patterns

```text
PATTERN A — BÜHL CUT-INSIDE               PATTERN B — BÜHL CUT-BACK
  receives wide left → cuts in → 18 m        runs down the left → cut-back to Dallmann
  shot (France 79', Oct 2025)                (Japan 28', Oct 2026)

PATTERN C — RUN IN BEHIND                 PATTERN D — SET PIECES
  regain → Kössler/Brand through ball        Nüsken from a corner (Australia 90+4')
  → Bühl behind (Japan 73')                  tall CBs (Minge PHY 81, Doorsoun 82)
```

### 6.4 Late Goals
- 79' vs France; 73' vs Japan; 83' and 90+4' vs Australia; 45+1' and 45+4' vs Norway.

### 6.5 Resilience and Goalkeeping
- Held France with ten players for 107 minutes and won the shootout (Euro 2025).
- **Ann-Katrin Berger** (Gotham, EA FC 27: 87) — saved two penalties and scored one; **Stina Johannes** made the decisive saves vs France (Oct 2025); **Ena Mahmutovic** started in October 2026.

### 6.6 Game-State Behaviour

| State | What Germany do |
|---|---|
| 0-0 | High press on the opponent's build-up; wide 1v1s |
| Trailing | Push numbers forward, move to 3-2-4-1 — **counter space opens** |
| Leading | Mid-block; still concede late (Australia 90+3') |
| Down to ten | Very organised (France) — or collapse (Sweden 1-4) |

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

### Strategy ① — Keep the Ball, Make Germany Chase (the Spain model)
- **Evidence:** Spain's 3-0 (three goals in 13 minutes once Germany chased).
- Germany's best weapons are transitions and set pieces — **possession takes both away.**

### Strategy ② — Do Not Build Short Under Their Press
- **Evidence:** Japan's 73' concession came from losing the ball while building out.
- When Germany press high, go long to a target forward.

### Strategy ③ — Double Up on Bühl and Brand
- Winger + full-back on each; show Bühl outside.

### Strategy ④ — Counter Behind Gwinn, Especially After 60'

```text
WÜCK'S LATE CHANGES → 3-2-4-1, NO RIGHT-BACK
           +
BÜHL/SCHÜLLER/DALLMANN DON'T TRACK BACK (DEF 33–46)
           ↓
WIN THE BALL → ATTACK GERMANY'S RIGHT SIDE
```

### Strategy ⑤ — Attack the Gap Between the Centre-Backs
- **Evidence:** Tanikawa's 15' goal for Japan.
- Runs from midfield between Küver/Kleinherne/Minge.

### Strategy ⑥ — Concentrate at the End of Each Half
- Germany score at 45+ and 90+; they also concede then — **both boxes matter.**

### Strategy ⑦ — Test Their Discipline in the Box
- **Evidence:** two Euro 2025 red cards for fouls in or around the box.
- Get crosses and shots into the box; Germany's defenders have given away handball and foul penalties.

### Summary

```text
KEEP THE BALL — MAKE THEM CHASE
            +
NO SHORT BUILD-UP UNDER PRESSURE
            +
DOUBLE-TEAM BÜHL AND BRAND
            +
COUNTER BEHIND GWINN AFTER THEIR 60' CHANGES
            +
FULL CONCENTRATION AT 40–45' AND 85–90+'
            ↓
TAKE AWAY TRANSITIONS AND SET PIECES — GERMANY'S TWO MAIN WEAPONS
```

---

## 13. Conclusion

Germany are deep, fast, physical, and coached by a man who wins games with half-time changes. They have a world-class shootout goalkeeper and match-winning wingers.

Their proven weaknesses:

1. **No answer to possession teams like Spain**
2. **Counter space after their own attacking substitutions**
3. **Red cards in big games**
4. **Concentration at the end of halves**

> **Germany win transitions and set pieces. Take those away with the ball, and they run out of ideas.**
