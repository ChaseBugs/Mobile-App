# SERIES 08 — The Evolution of the Goalkeeper

# EP04 — Why Goalkeepers Must Be Good Passers

## Why the Modern Goalkeeper Is the First Playmaker of the Build-Up

19 January 2020, Anfield. Liverpool vs Manchester United.

Liverpool led 1-0 deep into stoppage time.

United had pushed almost everyone forward in search of an equaliser.

Alisson caught a United corner and immediately looked up.

He sent a long pass over the halfway line towards Salah, who was running alone.

Salah collected it and scored.

2-0.

Alisson sprinted the length of the pitch to celebrate with Salah.

It was the first assist by a Premier League goalkeeper in almost ten years.

Klopp later revealed that the move had been practised in training for 18 months.

```text
UNITED CORNER (EVERYONE FORWARD)
            ↓
ALISSON CATCHES
            ↓
IMMEDIATE LONG PASS ─────────────→ SALAH
            ↓
          GOAL
```

What that moment shows is simple:

**A goalkeeper's pass can become a goal.**

# THE GOALKEEPER CAN CREATE

---

# 1. Why Must a Goalkeeper Be a Good Passer?

Many modern teams begin their build-up with the goalkeeper.

Since the 2019 goal-kick law change, starting goal kicks with a short pass has become the norm.

And the opponent sees that moment as a chance to press.

```text
OPPONENT PRESS ↓↓↓

   ST    ST    ST

 CB    No.6    CB

        GK  ← THE START OF THE PRESS — AND ITS SOLUTION
```

In this situation, the goalkeeper has three options:

**Avoid the press with a short pass.**

**Play a mid-range pass that breaks the first pressing line.**

**Play a long pass that goes over the entire press.**

Whatever he chooses, the starting point is the goalkeeper's decision.

# THE BUILD-UP STARTS WITH THE GOALKEEPER

---

# 2. The Age of Man-to-Man Pressing — The Only Player Who Cannot Be Marked

One of the major trends in modern pressing is **man-to-man pressing.**

Each of the opponent's ten outfield players is marked by one player.

But there is a structural problem.

```text
PRESSING TEAM:  10 OUTFIELD PLAYERS
BUILDING TEAM:  10 OUTFIELD PLAYERS + GK

10 vs 10 + 1
```

For the pressing team to mark the goalkeeper as well, it has to commit every player except its own goalkeeper.

But then there is nobody left behind its defence.

So in man-to-man pressing, the goalkeeper becomes **the only player who is always free.**

# THE GOALKEEPER = THE FREE MAN

If that free player is comfortable on the ball,

the structure of the pressing team collapses.

---

# 3. The Goalkeeper Who Invites the Press — De Zerbi's Brighton

Between 2022 and 2024, Roberto De Zerbi's Brighton became famous for a distinctive build-up.

Brighton's goalkeeper and centre-backs would wait with the ball at their feet.

They deliberately allowed the opposing forwards to come close.

```text
GK STOPS THE BALL
        ↓
OPPONENT PRESS STEPS FORWARD
        ↓
SPACE APPEARS BEHIND THE PRESS
        ↓
QUICK PASS → ESCAPE THE PRESS
        ↓
ATTACK WITH A NUMERICAL ADVANTAGE
```

This is **“baiting the press.”**

The further the opponent comes forward, the more space opens behind them.

The goalkeeper was the key that opened that space.

# ATTRACT → RELEASE

---

# 4. Ederson — When the Long Pass Becomes a Weapon

Not every team escapes pressure with short passes alone.

Ederson's long passing created a different problem for the opponent.

If the opponent pressed high:

```text
6–7 OPPONENTS PRESS FORWARD
          ↓
EDERSON LONG PASS
          ↓
1v1 BEHIND THE OPPONENT'S DEFENSIVE LINE
```

If the opponent did not press:

```text
NO PRESS
    ↓
CITY'S CALM SHORT BUILD-UP
    ↓
CONTROL OF POSSESSION
```

Whichever option the opponent chose, the problem was hard to solve.

That is the power of **a goalkeeper with two weapons.**

# SHORT OR LONG = DILEMMA FOR THE OPPONENT

---

# 5. The Four Types of Goalkeeper Distribution

Modern goalkeeper distribution can be divided into four broad types.

### 1. Short Pass — Escaping the Press

A short pass to a centre-back or the No.6. The aim is to keep possession.

### 2. Line-Breaking Pass — Breaking the First Line

A pass between the opposing forwards directly to a midfielder.

### 3. Long Pass — Bypassing the Entire Press

A pass straight to a forward or winger. Most effective when the opponent presses high.

### 4. Quick Throw — Starting the Counter

After catching a cross or corner, a fast throw to launch a counter-attack.

```text
TYPE          PURPOSE         RISK
──────────────────────────────────────
SHORT         KEEP THE BALL   MEDIUM
LINE-BREAK    PROGRESS        HIGH
LONG          BYPASS          LOW–MEDIUM
THROW         COUNTER         LOW
```

A good goalkeeper can do all four.

**A great goalkeeper knows when to choose which one.**

---

# 6. When the Goalkeeper Becomes a Back Three

In modern build-up, the goalkeeper often stands between the centre-backs and effectively becomes **a third centre-back.**

```text
NORMAL POSITION

   CB         CB
        GK

BUILD-UP POSITION

  CB    GK    CB  ← EFFECTIVELY A BACK THREE
```

This means:

**The centre-backs can spread wider.**

**The full-backs can push higher.**

**The No.6 can receive the ball further forward.**

The position of one goalkeeper changes the build-up structure of the whole team.

# GK POSITION → TEAM SHAPE

---

# 7. ter Stegen — Barcelona's First Midfielder

Barcelona's **Marc-André ter Stegen** was for many years the model of the ball-playing goalkeeper.

Barcelona's football starts with possession.

And the first player of that possession was the goalkeeper.

ter Stegen received the ball calmly under pressure,

and used both feet to find his centre-backs and midfielders accurately.

For Barcelona, he was a goalkeeper — and at the same time **their first midfielder.**

---

# 8. Raya — A Signing Made for Build-Up

Think back to Arsenal's choice, which we covered in EP01.

The reason Arteta chose Raya over Ramsdale was build-up.

Since his Brentford days, Raya had been rated as a goalkeeper with highly accurate long passing.

At Arsenal too, when the press came, he found teammates on the flanks or up front with precise long passes.

And then he won the Golden Glove three seasons in a row.

In 2025-26, he kept 19 clean sheets and led Arsenal to their first league title in 22 years.

In the first leg of the Champions League quarter-final against Sporting, he made five saves to protect a 1-0 win,

and his teammate Havertz called him **“the best goalkeeper in the world”** after the game.

This case shows most clearly that **build-up ability and defensive results can go together.**

---

# 9. Goalkeeper Distribution in the Data

Modern analysis evaluates goalkeeper distribution with metrics like these:

**Pass completion** — the percentage of all passes completed

**Long-pass completion** — the accuracy of passes over 40 yards

**Launch %** — the share of all passes that are long

**Average pass length**

But there is a trap here too.

A goalkeeper who only plays short passes will have a high completion rate.

A goalkeeper who goes long often will have a lower one.

```text
95% PASS COMPLETION ≠ GOOD DISTRIBUTION
70% PASS COMPLETION ≠ BAD DISTRIBUTION
```

What matters is:

**“What did that pass create for the team?”**

# PASS COMPLETION ≠ PASS VALUE

(It is the same principle we described for centre-backs in SERIES 07, EP01.)

---

# 10. The Risk of Footwork — Every Pass Has a Price

A goalkeeper's passing mistake is the most expensive mistake there is.

As with Onana in EP01,

when a ball-playing goalkeeper makes a mistake under pressure, the result can be an immediate goal.

And Chevalier's struggles at PSG in 2025-26 showed that a goalkeeper chosen for his feet still has to win trust with his saves.

```text
BUILD-UP BENEFIT: KEEP THE BALL, ESCAPE THE PRESS, NUMERICAL ADVANTAGE
BUILD-UP RISK:    MISTAKE → IMMEDIATE GOAL
```

So a good ball-playing goalkeeper must know:

**When the press is too strong, going long is also a good decision.**

**Preventing a goal comes before keeping the ball.**

# CONTROLLED RISK

---

# 11. A Goalkeeper's Passing Is the Result of the Whole Team's Training

A goalkeeper cannot build up well on his own.

The position of the centre-backs, the movement of the No.6, the height of the full-backs —

everyone must move together for the goalkeeper's pass to mean something.

```text
GK PASS QUALITY
       =
GK TECHNIQUE + TEAMMATE MOVEMENT + TEAM STRUCTURE
```

That is why the same goalkeeper can look like a very different passer in different teams.

# DISTRIBUTION IS A TEAM SKILL

---

# Conclusion — The Attack Begins the Moment the Goalkeeper Has the Ball

The goalkeeper of the past kicked the ball long whenever he had it.

The modern goalkeeper **thinks** whenever he has it.

Where is the space?

Who is free?

Should he invite the press, or go over it?

```text
1992   HE MUST USE HIS FEET
2010s  HE MUST ESCAPE THE PRESS WITH HIS FEET
2020s  HE MUST CREATE ATTACKS WITH HIS FEET
```

> **The modern goalkeeper's first pass is his team's first attacking decision.**
