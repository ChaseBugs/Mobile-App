# SERIES 03 — The Evolution of the No.6

# EP04 — Busquets vs Casemiro

## The No.6 Who Prevents the Turnover vs the No.6 Who Protects You After It

Sergio Busquets and Casemiro.

Same position.

Same basic responsibility:

**Protect the team from the base of midfield.**

But they approached that responsibility in very different ways.

Busquets tried to control the game so dangerous situations happened less often.

Casemiro was exceptional when those dangerous situations happened anyway.

```text
BUSQUETS
Control
   ↓
Prevent Danger


CASEMIRO
Danger
   ↓
Intervene
```

One reduced chaos.

The other gave his team protection inside chaos.

That is why comparing them as simply:

**Passer vs Tackler**

misses the real tactical difference.

---

# 1. Same Position, Different Defensive Philosophy

Both players occupied the No.6 zone.

But the important question is not:

**Where did they play?**

It is:

**At what moment did they solve the problem?**

Imagine the lifecycle of an attack:

```text
POSSESSION
    ↓
BUILD-UP
    ↓
TURNOVER
    ↓
TRANSITION
    ↓
DEFENSIVE DUEL
    ↓
BOX
```

Busquets had enormous influence toward the beginning of this chain.

Casemiro was especially valuable toward the dangerous end of it.

Busquets:

**Reduce the probability of danger.**

Casemiro:

**Reduce the damage when danger arrives.**

---

# 2. Busquets — Prevent the Bad Turnover

Busquets' first defensive weapon was not necessarily a tackle.

It was control.

Keep possession secure.

Maintain good distances.

Choose the correct passing option.

Keep teammates connected.

Avoid losing the ball in the worst possible zone.

```text
GOOD POSSESSION
      ↓
GOOD STRUCTURE
      ↓
FEWER BAD TURNOVERS
      ↓
FEWER COUNTERS
```

This is easy to underestimate.

Every dangerous turnover you prevent is a counterattack you never have to defend.

So Busquets could protect Barcelona without making a defensive action at all.

**Technical Security became Defensive Security.**

---

# 3. Casemiro — Protect the Bad Turnover

Now imagine the opposite situation.

Possession has already been lost.

The full-back is high.

A midfielder is ahead of the ball.

The opponent escapes the first press.

```text
BALL LOST

     ↓

OPPONENT → → →

        SPACE

      CASEMIRO

      CB    CB
```

The tactical problem has changed.

There is no time to rebuild the perfect structure.

Someone must intervene.

This was Casemiro's territory.

```text
READ DANGER
     ↓
CLOSE SPACE
     ↓
DELAY / DUEL
     ↓
RECOVER
```

Busquets often prevented the emergency.

Casemiro was exceptionally equipped to handle the emergency.

---

# 4. Control vs Recovery

This gives us the cleanest distinction between the two.

### BUSQUETS

```text
CONTROL
   ↓
SECURE POSSESSION
   ↓
GOOD POSITION
   ↓
REDUCE TRANSITIONS
```

### CASEMIRO

```text
TURNOVER
   ↓
READ DANGER
   ↓
RECOVER POSITION
   ↓
STOP TRANSITION
```

Busquets gave his team:

**Control Security.**

Casemiro gave his team:

**Recovery Security.**

Both are forms of protection.

They simply operate at different moments.

---

# 5. Busquets Wanted the Game Compact

Busquets was at his best when the team remained connected.

Short distances.

Clear passing triangles.

Players positioned around the ball.

```text
       LW      RW

      8        8

       BUSQUETS

     CB       CB
```

When the distances are short, losing possession becomes less dangerous.

Several players can immediately pressure the ball.

Passing lanes can be closed.

The opponent has less open grass to attack.

Busquets helped maintain this environment.

His ideal game was:

**Controlled Space.**

---

# 6. Casemiro Could Survive Bigger Spaces

Casemiro had a different strength.

He could defend when the pitch became larger.

```text
       OPPONENT
           ↓

SPACE ← CASEMIRO → SPACE

           ↓

         CB   CB
```

He could move horizontally.

Recover vertically.

Track runners.

Challenge for second balls.

Protect the half-space.

Drop toward the centre-backs.

This is **Defensive Range**.

Casemiro did not need the game to remain perfectly clean.

He could solve messy situations.

---

# 7. Prevent the Duel vs Win the Duel

Busquets and Casemiro also differed in how they approached defensive confrontation.

Busquets often wanted:

```text
POSITION
   ↓
CLOSE LANE
   ↓
FORCE DIFFERENT PASS
   ↓
NO DUEL NEEDED
```

Casemiro was more comfortable with:

```text
IDENTIFY THREAT
   ↓
CLOSE
   ↓
CONTACT
   ↓
WIN DUEL
```

Busquets' intelligence frequently appeared **before contact**.

Casemiro's intelligence frequently determined **which contact mattered most**.

That distinction is important.

Casemiro was not simply running around tackling people.

His best defensive actions started with reading the danger correctly.

---

# 8. Busquets Defended With Geometry

Busquets' defensive game depended heavily on angles.

Where is the ball?

Where is the next receiver?

Where is the passing lane?

Where are my centre-backs?

```text
BALL ●

       \ Passing Lane

        X
     BUSQUETS

     CB      CB
```

Move several metres.

Close one angle.

Force the opponent somewhere else.

Sometimes that tiny positional adjustment removed the dangerous pass completely.

Busquets often defended by changing the opponent's available options.

---

# 9. Casemiro Defended With Range

Casemiro could also read passing lanes.

But his physical profile allowed another solution.

**Go to the danger.**

```text
THREAT LEFT
    ← CASEMIRO

THREAT CENTRAL
        ↑
     CASEMIRO

RUNNER BEHIND
        ↓
     CASEMIRO
```

His defensive range allowed Real Madrid to survive situations in which the midfield had become stretched.

That was especially important in a team containing so much attacking freedom.

---

# 10. Why Casemiro Fit Real Madrid So Well

Consider the attacking talent around him.

Cristiano Ronaldo.

Benzema.

Bale.

Marcelo.

Kroos.

Modrić.

```text
       BENZEMA

RONALDO         BALE

    KROOS   MODRIĆ

      CASEMIRO

    CB       CB
```

Marcelo could attack aggressively.

Modrić could carry forward.

Kroos could control possession.

Ronaldo could remain high for the next attack.

All of that created enormous attacking power.

But freedom creates risk.

Casemiro was the insurance.

```text
ATTACKING FREEDOM
       ↓
      RISK
       ↓
    CASEMIRO
       ↓
   PROTECTION
```

This is where his profile overlaps with the historical Makélélé archetype.

But Casemiro's particular strength was his ability to defend aggressively when the game became stretched.

---

# 11. Kroos — Modrić — Casemiro Was a Division of Labour

Real Madrid's midfield was powerful because the three players did not need to perform identical functions.

Kroos could organise.

Modrić could manipulate pressure and progress.

Casemiro could protect.

```text
KROOS
Control / Distribution

       +

MODRIĆ
Escape / Progression

       +

CASEMIRO
Protection / Recovery
```

This created complementary roles.

Casemiro did not need to become Busquets.

Real Madrid already had elite technical controllers around him.

His value came from solving a different problem.

---

# 12. Barcelona Asked Something Different From Busquets

Barcelona's structure placed greater responsibility for circulation through the pivot.

Busquets was not simply waiting behind Xavi and Iniesta.

He was part of the mechanism connecting the whole team.

```text
         ATTACK

      XAVI   INIESTA

        BUSQUETS

       CB     CB
```

When Barcelona were pressed, Busquets could become the escape route.

When the opponent stayed deep, he could recycle possession.

When one side became crowded, he could help redirect the attack.

His security with the ball was part of Barcelona's defensive system.

If Barcelona controlled possession correctly,

the opponent had fewer opportunities to attack.

---

# 13. Different Ways to Protect Creative Midfielders

Both players helped elite midfield partners.

But again, they did it differently.

### Busquets

```text
BUSQUETS
   ↓
Secure Circulation
   ↓
Xavi / Iniesta
Receive in Better Structure
```

He helped create the environment in which the creators operated.

### Casemiro

```text
Kroos / Modrić
Take Risk
   ↓
Ball Lost
   ↓
CASEMIRO
Protects Behind
```

He gave creators insurance after risk.

One protected creativity through **control**.

The other protected creativity through **coverage and recovery**.

---

# 14. The Biggest Difference Appears After Losing the Ball

This is the most useful moment for comparing them.

Imagine both teams lose possession.

### BUSQUETS

The ideal situation is:

```text
BALL LOST
   ↓
TEAM ALREADY COMPACT
   ↓
LANES CLOSED
   ↓
IMMEDIATE PRESSURE
   ↓
BALL RECOVERED
```

The defensive solution begins with the structure that existed **before the turnover**.

### CASEMIRO

If the opponent escapes:

```text
BALL LOST
   ↓
FIRST PRESS BROKEN
   ↓
SPACE OPENS
   ↓
CASEMIRO READS DANGER
   ↓
INTERVENTION
```

The solution comes through the No.6's ability to repair the situation.

This gives us two different concepts:

**Prevention**

vs

**Recovery.**

---

# 15. Technical Security vs Defensive Insurance

Busquets offered something we can call:

## Technical Security

```text
SECURE RECEIVING
      +
SECURE PASSING
      +
POSITIONAL CONTROL
      ↓
FEWER DANGEROUS LOSSES
```

Casemiro offered:

## Defensive Insurance

```text
BALL LOST
    +
SPACE OPEN
    +
RUNNER FREE
      ↓
CASEMIRO
      ↓
SECOND CHANCE
```

Busquets reduces how often you need the insurance.

Casemiro makes the insurance stronger.

---

# 16. Box Defence Changes the Comparison

Casemiro also offered something Busquets was less specialised for:

**deep box protection.**

When the opponent reached the penalty area, Casemiro could drop alongside the centre-backs.

```text
        CROSS
          ↓

ATTACKER     ATTACKER

       CASEMIRO

      CB     CB
```

Now his physical qualities mattered.

Heading.

Second balls.

Body contact.

Blocking central space.

Tracking late runners.

Casemiro could temporarily become almost an additional centre-back.

This gave Real Madrid another layer of emergency defence.

---

# 17. Busquets Wanted to Stop the Attack Earlier

Busquets' ideal defensive contribution happened further from that emergency.

```text
OPPONENT
   ↓
CENTRAL PASS
   X
BUSQUETS
```

Close the route.

Intercept.

Force the attack sideways.

Allow teammates to recover.

Prevent a clean progression toward the box.

This explains an important difference:

**Casemiro was exceptional at defending danger.**

**Busquets was exceptional at reducing the quality of danger before it developed.**

---

# 18. Their Intelligence Looked Different

Both players possessed elite tactical intelligence.

But it appeared differently.

## Busquets — Predictive Intelligence

```text
SCAN
 ↓
READ
 ↓
POSITION
 ↓
PREVENT
```

Where will the next pass go?

Where should I stand?

Which space will become dangerous?

How can I remove that option before it appears?

## Casemiro — Threat Intelligence

```text
DANGER APPEARS
      ↓
PRIORITISE
      ↓
CLOSE
      ↓
INTERVENE
```

Which runner is most dangerous?

Should I step forward?

Should I drop?

Should I cover the full-back?

Should I protect the centre-back?

Casemiro's game was physical, but the physical action came after the tactical decision.

---

# 19. Casemiro Was Not Just a Destroyer

This is important.

Reducing Casemiro to:

**Tackles + Strength + Fouls**

does him a disservice.

He had strong positional awareness.

He intercepted passing lanes.

He understood how to screen centre-backs.

He could support build-up.

He could release forward players after recovering possession.

And he could contribute around the opponent's box.

His defensive aggression was built on reading danger, not simply chasing the ball. Analyses of his pivot role likewise highlight his central protection, anticipation, interceptions and transition security.

---

# 20. Busquets Was Not Just a Passer

The opposite mistake happens with Busquets.

Busquets was not simply:

**Possession + Passing.**

His positional intelligence was also defensive.

He intercepted.

Closed central routes.

Protected the centre-backs.

Prepared for counter-pressing.

His passing and defending were connected.

```text
GOOD POSITION
     ↓
GOOD POSSESSION
     ↓
GOOD REST DEFENCE
     ↓
FAST REACTION AFTER LOSS
```

That is why describing him as merely a deep playmaker also misses the point.

---

# 21. Different Weaknesses

Their strengths also created different vulnerabilities.

Busquets could become uncomfortable when the game became extremely open.

```text
BIG SPACE
+
FAST RUNNERS
+
LONG TRANSITION
       ↓
PROBLEM
```

His intelligence could compensate for speed.

But there is a limit.

If the structure around him disappeared completely, his physical range was not Casemiro's.

Casemiro had the opposite issue.

If a team required the No.6 to become the primary press-resistant controller of every build-up:

```text
HEAVY PRESS
+
CONSTANT RECEIVING
+
TEMPO CONTROL
+
TIGHT COMBINATIONS
       ↓
LESS NATURAL FIT
```

He could contribute.

But that was not the environment that maximised his greatest qualities.

---

# 22. This Is Why System Matters

A No.6 cannot be evaluated separately from the team's structure.

```text
PLAYER
  +
TEAMMATES
  +
SPACE
  +
TACTICAL MODEL
       ↓
FUNCTION
```

Ask Busquets to defend huge transition spaces alone and you reduce his advantage.

Ask Casemiro to reproduce Busquets' exact positional-play function and you reduce his advantage.

The correct question is not:

**Who can do more things?**

It is:

**Which problems does the team need this player to solve?**

---

# 23. Prevent Chaos vs Survive Chaos

Now the comparison becomes simple.

### BUSQUETS

```text
STRUCTURE
   ↓
CONTROL
   ↓
SECURITY
   ↓
PREVENT CHAOS
```

### CASEMIRO

```text
CHAOS
   ↓
READ DANGER
   ↓
INTERVENE
   ↓
RESTORE SECURITY
```

Busquets tried to keep the match inside the team's preferred structure.

Casemiro gave his team the ability to survive when the match escaped that structure.

That is the tactical heart of the comparison.

---

# 24. So Who Was the Better No.6?

This is the wrong question.

Because No.6 is a position.

Not a single function.

A better question is:

**What does your team need?**

If your system needs:

Press Resistance.

Build-up Control.

Tempo.

Positional Security.

Possession Stability.

Then the Busquets profile is extraordinary.

If your system needs:

Transition Protection.

Defensive Range.

Duels.

Box Protection.

Recovery Power.

Then the Casemiro profile is extraordinary.

```text
NEED CONTROL?
     ↓
BUSQUETS


NEED RECOVERY?
     ↓
CASEMIRO
```

Same position.

Different solution.

---

# 25. Two Extremes of the Elite No.6

Busquets and Casemiro represent two powerful directions of the position.

```text
             No.6

CONTROL ←────────────→ PROTECTION

BUSQUETS              CASEMIRO
```

### Busquets

Press Resistance
Positional Control
Technical Security
Tempo Control
Prevention

### Casemiro

Defensive Range
Duel Power
Transition Defence
Box Protection
Recovery

Neither list means the other player could not perform those actions.

It identifies where each player's greatest tactical value was concentrated.

---

# Conclusion — Two Ways to Protect a Team

Busquets and Casemiro played in the same zone.

But they protected their teams at different moments.

Busquets:

**Control the situation before it becomes dangerous.**

Casemiro:

**Control the danger after the situation breaks.**

```text
BUSQUETS

CONTROL
   ↓
PREVENT
   ↓
SECURITY


CASEMIRO

DANGER
   ↓
INTERVENE
   ↓
SECURITY
```

Busquets made his team need fewer emergency defensive actions.

Casemiro made his team better at surviving those emergencies.

One reduced the probability of chaos.

The other reduced the consequences of chaos.

**Busquets protected the team by keeping control.**

**Casemiro protected the team when control was lost.**

That is why the comparison should never be:

**Passer vs Defender.**

The smarter comparison is:

**Prevention vs Recovery.**

Two different defensive philosophies.

Two different versions of the No.6.

Both elite.
