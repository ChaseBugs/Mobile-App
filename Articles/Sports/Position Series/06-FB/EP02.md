# SERIES 06 — The Evolution of the Full-Back

# EP02 — Cafu & Roberto Carlos

## How Was the Prototype of the Attacking Full-Back Created?

Today, seeing a full-back attack is completely normal.

Overlap.

Cross.

Final-third runs.

Back-post arrivals.

But there was a time when this level of attacking responsibility was far less expected from the position.

And few players shaped the global image of the attacking full-back more powerfully than two Brazilians:

**Cafu.**

**Roberto Carlos.**

They played in the same national team.

They were both legendary attacking full-backs.

But they did not attack in the same way.

That distinction is important.

```text
CAFU
→ MOVEMENT + COMBINATION + CONTINUITY

ROBERTO CARLOS
→ POWER + WIDTH + DIRECT THREAT
```

Inside one position, they demonstrated two very different ways of creating attacking advantage.

---

# 1. An Attacking Full-Back Is More Than a Defender Who Runs Forward

The simplest definition would be:

**Defender + Forward Run**

But that misses the tactical point.

The important question is not:

**Does the full-back go forward?**

It is:

**What happens to the opposition when he goes forward?**

```text
FULL-BACK ADVANCES
        ↓
DEFENDERS REACT
        ↓
STRUCTURE SHIFTS
        ↓
NEW SPACE APPEARS
```

The movement matters because it changes the problem facing the defence.

Cafu and Roberto Carlos did this in very different ways.

---

# 2. Cafu — The Attack That Never Stopped

One of Cafu's defining qualities was:

**Continuity.**

He did not simply make one overlap and disappear from the attack.

He could advance.

Combine.

Move again.

Support again.

Then recover when necessary.

```text
PASS
 ↓
RUN
 ↓
RECEIVE
 ↓
CROSS / COMBINE
 ↓
RECOVER
```

His attacking value came not only from explosive individual moments.

It came from the ability to keep the right side active.

---

# 3. Cafu's Real Weapon — Repeatability

Many players can make one powerful overlap.

Doing it repeatedly throughout a match is different.

Cafu could attack the same channel again and again.

First overlap.

Second overlap.

Third overlap.

The defender repeatedly faced the same dilemma:

Follow Cafu?

Or protect the attacker inside?

```text
ATTACKER
    ↓
DEFENDER

CAFU ↑
```

One run might not destroy the defensive structure.

Repeated runs could gradually stretch it.

That was the power of:

**Repeatability.**

---

# 4. Cafu Did Not Attack Alone

Cafu's game was also built around combination.

He could interact with:

The winger.

The central midfielder.

The forward.

```text
WINGER
   ↘
   CAFU
   ↙
MIDFIELDER
```

His movement created additional passing angles and attacking relationships.

This is why his game should not be reduced to:

**Run + Cross.**

A better description is:

**Run + Combine + Move Again.**

---

# 5. Cafu Turned Width Into Continuous Pressure

By repeatedly occupying the outside lane, Cafu forced the opposition to respect the entire width of the pitch.

```text
CENTRE                 RIGHT

                       CAFU
                         ↑
                    HOLDS WIDTH
```

Ignore him and he could advance.

Follow him and another space might open inside.

His width therefore became more than positioning.

**Width became pressure.**

---

# 6. Cafu Created Rhythm

Cafu's attacking influence was often built through repetition.

Pass.

Move.

Overlap.

Combine.

Return.

Attack again.

His movement gave the right side rhythm.

That is an important distinction.

He did not simply:

**Join the attack.**

He helped:

**Keep the attack moving.**

This is why Cafu represents one prototype of the attacking full-back:

## The Continuous Full-Back

---

# 7. Roberto Carlos — A Completely Different Threat

Roberto Carlos represented another interpretation.

His defining characteristic was:

**Explosive Directness.**

Speed.

Power.

Ball carrying.

Crossing.

Long-range shooting.

The ability to cover enormous distance quickly.

```text
DEEP POSITION
      ↓
EXPLOSIVE ADVANCE
      ↓
FINAL THIRD
```

Where Cafu could gradually build pressure through repeated movement, Roberto Carlos could transform a situation almost instantly.

---

# 8. The Full-Back Became a Direct Attacking Threat

Traditionally, a full-back might support the dangerous players.

Roberto Carlos could become one of the dangerous players himself.

He could:

Accelerate into the final third.

Deliver with power.

Carry through open space.

Shoot from distance.

```text
ROBERTO CARLOS
      ↓
RUN / CROSS / SHOT
      ↓
DIRECT THREAT
```

The opposition could not treat him merely as a supporting defender.

They had to defend him as an attacking weapon.

---

# 9. Speed Became a Tactical Weapon

Roberto Carlos's speed was not simply an athletic quality.

It had tactical consequences.

When he accelerated forward, defenders had to react quickly.

```text
CARLOS ↑↑↑

DEFENCE ↓↓↓
```

That could push the opposition backward.

And when a defensive line retreats, other players may gain space.

The winger.

The midfielder.

The forward.

So speed does more than win a race.

**Speed can change structure.**

---

# 10. The Threat of the Shot Changed Defensive Decisions

Roberto Carlos also carried a threat from distance.

That created another defensive dilemma.

Stay too deep?

He may have space to strike.

Step forward aggressively?

Space may appear behind.

```text
DEFENDER STAYS DEEP
→ SHOOTING SPACE

DEFENDER STEPS OUT
→ SPACE BEHIND
```

The important idea is not simply that Roberto Carlos could shoot hard.

His shooting created:

**Decision Pressure.**

The defender had one more problem to consider.

---

# 11. Cafu vs Roberto Carlos

They shared the same broad positional label.

But their attacking identities were different.

| Player         | Main Weapon            | Attacking Effect                    |
| -------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Cafu           | Movement + Combination | Keeps the flank continuously active |
| Roberto Carlos | Power + Directness     | Changes the situation explosively   |
| Cafu           | Repetition             | Repeated overlap and support        |
| Roberto Carlos | Acceleration           | Rapid territorial progression       |

The simplest distinction is:

**Cafu = Continuous Pressure**

**Roberto Carlos = Explosive Pressure**

Neither model is inherently better.

They solve different attacking problems.

---

# 12. Two Different Types of Width

Even the way they created width was different.

```text
CAFU
→ MOVE TO CREATE WIDTH

ROBERTO CARLOS
→ THREATEN FROM WIDTH
```

Cafu's width supported continuity and combination.

Roberto Carlos's width could become the starting point of a direct attack.

One stretched the defence through repeated occupation.

The other threatened to attack the space with speed and power.

---

# 13. Their Movement Could Free the Attacker Inside

The attacking value of a full-back is not limited to his own touches.

If the full-back occupies the outside lane, another attacker may move inside.

```text
FULL-BACK
    ↑
   WIDE

WINGER
   → INSIDE
```

Now the defender has a decision.

Follow the inside attacker?

Or protect the outside lane?

This is one of the fundamental benefits of an attacking full-back:

**His movement can create freedom for somebody else.**

---

# 14. But Attacking Always Has a Cost

Every forward movement creates another question.

What happens behind the full-back?

```text
FULL-BACK HIGH
      ↑

BALL LOST

      ↓

SPACE BEHIND
```

The more aggressively a full-back attacks, the more important defensive balance becomes.

Centre-backs may need to cover.

Midfielders may need to protect the channel.

The opposite side may need to remain more cautious.

So the attacking full-back is never purely an individual concept.

He exists inside:

**Team Structure.**

---

# 15. Cafu — Controlled Repetition

Cafu's stamina was exceptional, but physical capacity alone does not explain his game.

Timing mattered.

When to go.

When to support.

When to recover.

When to remain high.

His repeated attacking involvement was therefore not simply endless running.

It was:

**Controlled Repetition.**

That distinction separates useful movement from movement for its own sake.

---

# 16. Roberto Carlos — Controlled Explosion

Roberto Carlos created a different balance problem.

His explosive forward movement could create enormous attacking value.

But it could also leave enormous space.

```text
CARLOS ATTACKS
      ↓
TEAM GAINS THREAT

BUT

SPACE OPENS
      ↓
TEAM NEEDS COVER
```

This relationship between attacking reward and defensive risk remains one of the central questions of full-back tactics today.

---

# 17. They Changed How Full-Backs Were Evaluated

Traditionally, the first question about a full-back was defensive.

Can he stop the winger?

Cafu and Roberto Carlos helped expand the conversation.

Now you could also ask:

How much width can he provide?

How high can he influence the game?

Can he progress the ball?

Can he repeatedly support attacks?

Can he create chances?

Can he become a direct goal threat?

The evaluation expanded from:

**Defending**

to:

**Defending + Attacking Influence.**

---

# 18. A Full-Back Could Shape the Match

When people imagined the player who would decide a match, they traditionally looked toward:

The striker.

The No.10.

The winger.

But attacking full-backs demonstrated that the flank could produce major tactical influence from deeper starting positions.

```text
FULL-BACK
   ↓
NOT ONLY SUPPORT

FULL-BACK
   ↓
CREATE ADVANTAGE
```

Cafu could change the match through movement.

Roberto Carlos could change it through explosive threat.

The full-back was becoming more than a supporting position.

---

# 19. The DNA of the Modern Attacking Full-Back

Many qualities associated with modern attacking full-backs were already visible in these two players.

### Cafu

Repeated Runs.

Combination.

Width.

Recovery.

Support.

### Roberto Carlos

Acceleration.

Carrying.

Crossing.

Shooting.

Direct Threat.

Together they give us a useful foundation:

```text
MODERN ATTACKING FULL-BACK

MOVEMENT
+
WIDTH
+
PROGRESSION
+
FINAL-THIRD THREAT
```

Modern tactics would eventually add much more complexity.

But the attacking DNA was already there.

---

# 20. But They Were Not Yet the “Midfielder Full-Back”

This distinction is essential for understanding the evolution of the position.

Cafu and Roberto Carlos expanded the idea of:

**Full-Back as Attacker.**

But another revolution was still to come.

The next question would no longer be only:

**How can the full-back attack down the flank?**

It would become:

**What if the full-back leaves the flank and enters midfield?**

```text
CAFU / ROBERTO CARLOS
→ FULL-BACK AS ATTACKER

NEXT EVOLUTION
→ FULL-BACK AS MIDFIELDER
```

And one of the most important players in that next chapter was:

**Philipp Lahm.**

---

# Conclusion — Before the Full-Back Became a Midfielder, He Became an Attacker

Cafu and Roberto Carlos created two powerful models of the attacking full-back.

Cafu represented:

**Movement.
Repetition.
Combination.
Continuity.**

Roberto Carlos represented:

**Power.
Speed.
Width.
Direct Threat.**

Their styles were different.

But their message was similar.

The full-back did not have to exist only to defend the flank.

He could create space.

Stretch the opposition.

Connect attacks.

Advance the ball.

And become a threat himself.

```text
OLD FULL-BACK
→ DEFEND THE FLANK

CAFU / ROBERTO CARLOS
→ ATTACK FROM THE FLANK
```

Their greatest legacy was not simply proving that defenders could attack.

It was proving that:

**A full-back could help define the attacking identity of a team.**

Cafu did it through continuous movement.

Roberto Carlos did it through explosive power.

One kept the attack alive.

The other could transform it in a moment.

Before the full-back moved into midfield, the position first had to escape the idea that its only home was defence.

**Before the full-back became a midfielder,
he first had to become an attacker.**

## The Lahm Revolution

### Why Did Guardiola Move a Full-Back Into Central Midfield?

Cafu and Roberto Carlos expanded what a full-back could do in attack.

The next revolution changed **where the full-back attacked from.**

Instead of always going outside and forward:

**The full-back moved inside.**

And once Philipp Lahm began appearing next to central midfielders, the position entered a completely different tactical era.

**What happens when a full-back stops behaving like a wide player and starts thinking like a midfielder?**
