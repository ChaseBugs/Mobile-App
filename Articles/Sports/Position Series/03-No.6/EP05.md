# SERIES 03 — The Evolution of the No.6

# EP05 — The Complete Modern No.6

## Why Is Rodri the Midfielder Modern Coaches Want?

The modern No.6 is asked to do a lot.

Protect the centre-backs.

Receive under pressure.

Start the build-up.

Break lines.

Control tempo.

Stop counterattacks.

Compete for aerial balls.

And sometimes influence the final third.

In the past, these responsibilities could be divided between several players.

Modern football increasingly asks one question:

**“Can one player connect all of these phases?”**

That is what makes Rodri special.

Not because he is the best in history at one isolated skill.

But because he connects several elite No.6 functions inside the same game.

```text id="zq95cr"
DEFEND
   ↓
RECEIVE
   ↓
CONTROL
   ↓
PROGRESS
   ↓
SUPPORT
   ↓
RECOVER
```

The modern No.6 is no longer a specialist in one phase.

He is a player who **connects phases**.

---

# 1. The Modern No.6 Problem — One Skill Is Not Enough

When the team has possession, you need a playmaker.

When the ball is lost, you need a defender.

When the opponent presses, you need press resistance.

When the game becomes direct, you need physical presence.

When the opponent sits deep, you need progression.

```text id="b2vr9v"
WITH BALL      → PLAYMAKER

UNDER PRESS    → PRESS RESISTANT

BALL LOST      → DEFENDER

LONG BALL      → PHYSICAL PRESENCE

LOW BLOCK      → PROGRESSOR
```

The problem is that these are not different matches.

They can all happen within a few minutes.

So the modern No.6 needs more than versatility.

The better word is:

**Phase Adaptability.**

The situation changes.

The function must change with it.

---

# 2. Rodri's Greatest Strength — Switching Functions

If you only watch Rodri's passing, you miss part of his game.

If you only watch his defending, you miss another part.

His real value is in the transition from one function to the next.

```text id="lnp98q"
INTERCEPT
   ↓
FIRST PASS
   ↓
MOVE INTO SUPPORT
   ↓
CONTROL POSSESSION
```

Or:

```text id="dr7bmk"
BUILD-UP
   ↓
PROGRESSIVE PASS
   ↓
TEAM ATTACKS
   ↓
BALL LOST
   ↓
DEFENSIVE POSITION
```

He does not need a separate reset before becoming a different type of player.

His **Transition between Functions** is fast.

---

# 3. Rodri's Position Is Not Fixed

A modern No.6 does not simply stand in front of the centre-backs.

Rodri changes his position according to the opponent's pressing structure.

```text id="9lzw43"
       OPPONENT ST

CB        RODRI        CB
```

If the centre is blocked, he may drop.

If one side is heavily pressed, he may create a passing lane on the other side.

If midfield space opens, he steps higher again.

```text id="pyz53v"
BUILD-UP NEEDS HELP
        ↓
   DROP DEEP


MIDFIELD NEEDS PLAYER
        ↓
   STEP HIGHER
```

His position is not a fixed coordinate.

It is:

**the place where the team needs the next connection.**

---

# 4. Physical Size + Technical Security

One of the most important parts of Rodri's profile is the combination.

**Physical Size + Technical Security**

A physically strong No.6 may dominate duels but struggle to turn in tight spaces.

A technical No.6 may resist pressure but struggle in open physical games.

Rodri connects both areas at a high level.

```text id="yzcqnx"
PHYSICAL PRESENCE
        +
CLOSE CONTROL
        +
BODY ORIENTATION
        ↓
SECURITY
```

If an opponent presses tightly, he can protect the ball.

If the opponent stays away, he can control the game.

That dual threat matters.

---

# 5. Press Resistance Is About Decision Quality

Rodri is not the type of player who needs to dribble past three opponents.

His pressure resistance is efficient.

First touch.

Body orientation.

Shielding.

Short turn.

One-touch pass.

```text id="rxwdd0"
PRESS
 ↓
PROTECT BALL
 ↓
TURN / RELEASE
 ↓
NEXT LINE
```

The objective is not:

**Hold the ball for a long time.**

It is:

**Choose an action the opponent cannot punish.**

That is a different form of press resistance.

---

# 6. Passing — Connection Is Not Enough

A No.6 can complete many passes and still have little impact on progression.

The important question is:

**Did the pass remove opponents?**

```text id="h8utx7"
CB
 ↓
RODRI
 ↓
──────── OPPONENT MIDFIELD ────────
 ↓
No.8 / WINGER
```

If the ball reaches a teammate behind the opponent's midfield line, the whole attack changes.

That is a **Line-Breaking Pass**.

Rodri does not only keep possession safe.

He can make safe possession become forward progression.

---

# 7. Knowing When Not to Play Forward

But progression does not mean forcing vertical passes.

An elite No.6 must also know when the forward option is wrong.

```text id="kfs6ei"
FORWARD PASS?
      ↓
TOO RISKY
      ↓
RECYCLE
      ↓
REPOSITION
      ↓
ATTACK AGAIN
```

This is where decision-making becomes more important than technique.

The question is not only:

**Can I play forward?**

It is:

**Should I play forward now?**

That difference separates control from impatience.

---

# 8. Tempo Control Is Not Just Playing Fast

Controlling tempo does not mean increasing speed all the time.

Sometimes the correct action is:

**Accelerate.**

Sometimes:

**Pause.**

Sometimes:

**Recycle.**

Sometimes:

**Switch.**

```text id="kfz0pv"
GAME STATE
    ↓

Accelerate?
Pause?
Recycle?
Switch?
    ↓
DECISION
```

Rodri can increase the speed of the attack.

He can also slow the game down when the team needs stability.

A top No.6 does not simply follow the rhythm.

He helps decide what the rhythm should be.

---

# 9. Switch of Play — Creating Width From the Centre

When the opponent presses one side heavily, the opposite side often opens.

```text id="z9rmrc"
PRESS PRESS PRESS
      ↓
     RODRI

                FREE SIDE
                   →
```

Rodri can recognise that quickly.

```text id="kt6hwl"
LEFT
 ↓
RODRI
 ↓
────────────→ RIGHT
```

A switch of play is not just a long pass.

It attacks the direction of the opponent's defensive movement.

The No.6 may not occupy the width himself.

But he can activate it.

---

# 10. Against a Low Block, Rodri Moves Higher

When the opponent sits deep, staying directly in front of the centre-backs can reduce the No.6's influence.

Rodri can step higher.

```text id="1iz5ei"
        LOW BLOCK

LW      ST      RW

    8       8

       RODRI ↑
```

Now his responsibilities change.

Recover clearances.

Win second balls.

Recycle possession.

Support around the box.

Stop the opponent from escaping.

He becomes part of the mechanism that keeps the opponent trapped.

---

# 11. The Modern No.6 Creates the Second Attack

Strong possession teams rarely attack only once.

The first cross is cleared.

The first pass is blocked.

The opponent tries to escape.

Then the second ball appears.

```text id="1lxd47"
ATTACK
  ↓
CLEARANCE
  ↓
RODRI
  ↓
RECOVER
  ↓
ATTACK AGAIN
```

If the No.6 recovers that ball, two things happen at once.

**Stop Counter**

and

**Restart Attack**

This is one of the most important modern No.6 actions.

One recovery can be both defensive and attacking.

---

# 12. Protecting Behind the Counter-Press

When attackers lose the ball, the nearest players often press immediately.

But what happens if the first press fails?

```text id="sdf8md"
ATTACKERS PRESS
      ↓
   OPPONENT
      ↓
    RODRI
      ↓
     CBs
```

Now the No.6 must make a fast decision.

Step forward?

Drop?

Block the passing lane?

Track the runner?

One wrong decision can expose the entire defensive line.

The modern No.6 does not only defend the counterattack.

He manages the moment **after the counter-press fails**.

---

# 13. Physicality Is Also an On-Ball Weapon

Physicality is often treated as a defensive trait.

For Rodri, it also helps possession.

Protect the ball.

Absorb contact.

Win aerial challenges.

Secure second balls.

```text id="vzk12k"
CONTACT
   ↓
PROTECT
   ↓
KEEP BALL
   ↓
TEAM MOVES UP
```

His body strength helps him do more than:

**Win Duel.**

It also helps him:

**Keep Possession.**

That is an important distinction.

---

```text id="k6m14s"
     ↓
```

That means a technical possession team can also survive a more physical match.

---

# 15. He Can Protect the Centre-Backs and Temporarily Become One

Modern build-up constantly changes shape.

If Rodri drops:

```text id="knbbz6"
CB     RODRI     CB
```

The structure can resemble a back three.

If he steps higher:

```text id="7z9o3a"
CB             CB

       RODRI
```

He becomes the midfield base again.

These movements change the numbers and angles in build-up.

They also make the opponent's pressing reference points less stable.

Rodri does not just receive in the structure.

He helps reshape it.

---

# 16. He Occupies the Space That Will Become Dangerous

When a full-back or midfielder moves forward, another zone becomes exposed.

Rodri must think one phase ahead.

Not only:

**Where is the ball?**

But:

**If we lose it now, where is the danger?**

```text id="pgxxk8"
ATTACK LEFT

        BALL ●

   PLAYERS ↑↑↑

        RODRI
          ↘
      DANGER SPACE
```

That is modern No.6 positioning.

Support the current attack.

Prepare for the next transition.

At the same time.

---

# 17. He Does Not Disappear in the Final Third

Traditional defensive midfielders often remained far behind the attack.

Rodri can move higher when the situation requires it.

```text id="n76g8x"
      PENALTY BOX

LW       ST       RW

     8        8

       RODRI
         ↑
```

From there he can:

Recycle.

Switch.

Recover second balls.

Support cut-backs.

Shoot.

Arrive late.

So the No.6 can influence the final phase without becoming an attacking midfielder permanently.

---

# 18. Why Goal Threat Matters

Scoring is not the first responsibility of a No.6.

But if the opponent cannot completely ignore him outside the box, the defensive structure changes.

```text id="rwkx5c"
DEFENCE DROPS
     ↓
SPACE OUTSIDE BOX
     ↓
RODRI
```

A midfielder must step toward him.

That movement creates another space.

So shooting threat is not only about goal totals.

It can become a tactical tool.

**Threat changes positioning.**

---

One moment perfectly represents Rodri's final-third influence.

The 2023 Champions League Final.

Manchester City vs Inter.

The game was 0-0.

Bernardo Silva attacked the right side and the ball arrived in the central area.

Rodri reached the space outside the defensive line.

Then:

Finish.

Goal.

```text id="cnk7m2"
CUT-BACK
   ↓
LOOSE SPACE
   ↓
RODRI
   ↓
FINISH
```

Manchester City won 1-0.

Their first Champions League title was secured.

And the Treble was completed.

A No.6 scored the decisive goal in the biggest match of the season.

It was a symbolic example of how far the position had expanded.

# 21. Modern Coaches Want Two Players in One Position

From a squad-building perspective, this matters enormously.

If your No.6 cannot build:

another midfielder must drop deeper.

If your No.6 cannot defend:

another midfielder must stay behind.

```text id="4bt6uj"
LIMITED No.6

Needs Build-up Help
        +
Needs Defensive Help
        ↓
TWO TEAMMATES RESTRICTED
```

But if the No.6 can do both:

```text id="pwn17y"
COMPLETE No.6
     ↓
BUILD + DEFEND
     ↓
OTHERS GET FREEDOM
```

One player's completeness can free multiple teammates.

That is tactical value beyond individual statistics.

---

# 22. Tactical Economy

This leads to an important concept:

**Tactical Economy.**

If one player solves several problems, the rest of the team gains freedom.

```text id="pqlms9"
1 PLAYER
   ↓
SOLVES
3–4 PROBLEMS
   ↓
MORE FREEDOM
FOR TEAMMATES
```

Without this type of No.6, a coach might need separate specialists for:

Build-up.

Defensive protection.

Physical duels.

Tempo control.

Rodri reduces that need.

Several functions can exist inside one position.

---

# 23. Complete Does Not Mean Best at Everything

The word **Complete** needs to be used carefully.

It does not mean:

```text id="2r1uvy"
COMPLETE
≠
BEST AT EVERYTHING
```

A complete No.6 is not necessarily the greatest player in history at every individual skill.

It means something more useful tactically:

**No major weakness that forces the entire team to compensate.**

The fewer problems teammates must solve for you, the more flexible the team becomes.

That is a major part of Rodri's value.

---

Different opponents create different tactical problems.

High press?

→ Build through him.

Mid-block?

→ Let him control circulation.

Low block?

→ Push him higher and recycle attacks.

Direct football?

→ Compete for aerial and second balls.

Transition game?

→ Protect the centre.

```text id="z80ntk"
HIGH PRESS → RECEIVE

MID BLOCK → CONTROL

LOW BLOCK → SUPPORT HIGHER

LONG BALL → COMPETE

TRANSITION → PROTECT
```

One player can respond to several different game states.

For a coach, that is extremely valuable.

---

# 25. The Evolution Is Not About Doing More

There is an important distinction here.

The modern No.6 has more responsibilities.

But that does not mean he should try to do everything at once.

The real evolution is not:

**More Actions.**

It is:

**Better Functional Switching.**

```text id="73l1sa"
NOT

DO EVERYTHING
    ↓

BUT

DO THE RIGHT FUNCTION
AT THE RIGHT MOMENT
```

During build-up:

Controller.

Under pressure:

Escape Point.

During attack:

Support.

After possession loss:

Protector.

On second balls:

Ball Winner.

In the final third:

Extra Threat.

That is what complete really means.

**“Can he build?”**

Then:

**“Can he survive pressure?”**

Now the questions are broader.

```text id="3cn88y"
CAN YOU DEFEND?

CAN YOU RECEIVE?

CAN YOU PROGRESS?

CAN YOU CONTROL?

CAN YOU PROTECT TRANSITIONS?
```

# Conclusion — The Complete Modern No.6

Rodri is one of the clearest examples of how the No.6 has evolved.

He does not only defend.

He does not only build.

He does not only control tempo.

He does not only stop transitions.

He connects all of them.

```text id="c3yjyp"
RECEIVE
   ↓
CONTROL
   ↓
PROGRESS
   ↓
SUPPORT
   ↓
RECOVER
   ↓
CONTROL AGAIN
```

That is the modern No.6.

Not a player who repeats one specialist function.

A player who changes function as the game changes.

Makélélé showed that the No.6 could become the team's **Balance**.

Busquets showed that the No.6 could become its **Control Centre**.

Casemiro showed that the No.6 could become its **Insurance when structure breaks**.

Rodri answers the next question:

**Why choose only one?**

The modern No.6 is not simply a player somewhere between a defender and a playmaker.

He constantly moves between those responsibilities.

**The complete No.6 does not dominate one phase.
He connects every phase.**

A complete No.6 is not a player who does everything alone.

He is the player who can take the team safely from one phase of the game into the next.

And that is why Rodri represents the modern ideal so well.
