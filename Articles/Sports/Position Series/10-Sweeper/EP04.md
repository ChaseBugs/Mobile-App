# EP04 — The Last Liberos

## Scirea, Baresi, Matthäus, Sammer — The Era of the Last Liberos

11 July 1982, Madrid, the Bernabéu. World Cup final, Italy vs West Germany.

Behind Italy's defensive line stood one Libero:

**Gaetano Scirea.**

In the second half, Italy took the lead through Rossi.

Then came Italy's second goal.

Scirea stepped up from behind the defensive line and joined the attack all the way to the edge of the opponent's box.

His pass found Tardelli, and Tardelli's shot went in.

Tardelli ran away screaming with his arms wide — one of the most famous celebrations in World Cup history.

Italy won 3-1 and lifted their third World Cup.

**The Libero from behind the defensive line had played a part in the decisive goal of a World Cup final.**

---

# 1. Gaetano Scirea — The Gentleman Libero

Scirea won Serie A seven times with Juventus.

And in his entire career, **he was never sent off.**

For a defender, that is a remarkable record.

Scirea's defending was never rough.

```text
SCIREA'S DEFENDING

READ THE OPPONENT'S PASS
          ↓
TAKE THE POSITION FIRST
          ↓
INTERCEPT WITHOUT A TACKLE
          ↓
START THE ATTACK IMMEDIATELY
```

He could step forward with the ball like Beckenbauer,

and at the same time tidy up behind the defensive line perfectly, like Picchi.

**A balance between Libero 1.0 and 2.0.**

In 1989, Scirea died in a car accident in Poland. He was 36.

Italian football still remembers him as **“the Gentleman” (Il Gentiluomo).**

# CLEAN, CALM, COMPLETE

---

# 2. Franco Baresi — From Libero to Line Leader

Baresi played 20 seasons, all for AC Milan.

### Liedholm's Libero

In 1978-79, the teenage Baresi won Serie A as Libero under coach Liedholm.

### The Serie B Years

In 1980, Milan were relegated to Serie B because of a match-fixing scandal.

In 1982, they were relegated again — this time on the pitch.

Both times, Baresi stayed.

### Sacchi's Line Leader

When Sacchi arrived in 1987, Baresi transformed from a Libero into **the leader of a flat back four.**

At his signal, the whole defensive line stepped up together and caught the opponent offside.

Milan won back-to-back European Cups in 1989 and 1990.

```text
1979     LIEDHOLM'S LIBERO
            ↓
1980s    SERIE B (TWO RELEGATIONS)
            ↓
1989–90  SACCHI'S LINE LEADER
```

**Baresi was the only defender to dominate both the age of the Libero and the age of zonal defending.**

# FROM THE FREE MAN TO THE LINE

---

# 3. 1994 — Baresi's Last Final

1994 World Cup in the United States. The group game against Norway.

Baresi tore the meniscus in his right knee.

For a 34-year-old defender, the tournament seemed to be over.

But after arthroscopic surgery, he came back about 25 days later.

The final in Pasadena. Italy vs Brazil.

For 120 minutes, Baresi directed the defence against Romário and Bebeto.

0-0.

Late in extra time, he went down and was carried off the pitch — then came back on.

In the shootout, Baresi stepped up first, and his kick flew over the bar.

Italy lost.

But the final is remembered as the match that made Baresi one of the greatest defenders in history.

# VICTORY IN DEFEAT

---

# 4. Ronald Koeman — Cruyff's Libero

In the early 1990s, Cruyff's Barcelona had a Libero too:

**Ronald Koeman.**

In Cruyff's 3-4-3, Koeman started the build-up from the centre of the defensive line.

He was a defender who created attacks with powerful free kicks and accurate long passes.

20 May 1992, Wembley. European Cup final, Barcelona vs Sampdoria.

It was 0-0 going into extra time. Then, in the 112th minute,

Koeman's free kick flew into the corner of the net.

**1-0. Barcelona's first European Cup.**

Koeman was also an early model of the **ball-playing defender** we saw in SERIES 07.

# THE LIBERO WHO SCORED

---

# 5. Lothar Matthäus — From Midfielder to Libero

Matthäus was originally one of the best midfielders in the world.

At the 1990 World Cup, he captained West Germany to the title and won the Ballon d'Or that year.

But late in his career, he dropped back to play **Libero.**

```text
1990        MATTHÄUS = MIDFIELDER (WORLD CUP, BALLON D'OR)
              ↓
LATE 1990s  MATTHÄUS = LIBERO
```

At the 1998 World Cup, the 37-year-old Matthäus played as Libero

and set what was then the record for World Cup appearances: **25 matches.**

(Messi passed the record with 26 matches in 2022.)

Then came Euro 2000.

At 39, Matthäus was still Germany's Libero.

But Germany went out in the group stage.

In their last game, they lost **3-0** to what was close to a Portugal reserve side.

In that match, Matthäus won his 150th cap, a world record at the time — but the tournament became one of the lowest points in German football history.

**Germany was the country that held on to the Libero the longest. Euro 2000 marked the end of that era.**

# THE END OF AN ERA

---

# 6. Matthias Sammer — The Last Great Libero

Sammer came from East Germany.

After reunification, he moved through Stuttgart and Inter before arriving at Dortmund.

Dortmund coach Ottmar Hitzfeld moved him from midfield into the Libero role.

### Euro 1996

Germany beat the Czech Republic 2-1 in the final with Bierhoff's golden goal.

Sammer was named **player of the tournament.**

### The 1996 Ballon d'Or

Sammer won the Ballon d'Or with 144 points — **just one point** ahead of Ronaldo on 143.

He was the first defender to win it since Beckenbauer in 1976, twenty years earlier.

### The 1997 Champions League

28 May 1997, Munich. Dortmund vs Juventus.

As captain, Sammer led Dortmund to a **3-1** win.

```text
1996  EURO WINNER + PLAYER OF THE TOURNAMENT
1996  BALLON D'OR (ONE POINT AHEAD OF RONALDO)
1997  CHAMPIONS LEAGUE WINNER (CAPTAIN)
```

But soon after, a knee injury effectively ended his career.

Sammer had to stop playing at around the age of 30.

# THE LAST GREAT LIBERO

---

# 7. What the Last Liberos Had in Common

```text
              ERA          TEAM              TRAIT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SCIREA       1970s–80s    JUVENTUS          CLEAN DEFENDING + ATTACKING RUNS
BARESI       1977–97      AC MILAN          LIBERO → LINE LEADER
KOEMAN       1989–95      BARCELONA         BUILD-UP + FREE KICKS
MATTHÄUS     LATE 1990s   BAYERN/GERMANY    MIDFIELDER → LIBERO
SAMMER       1993–98      DORTMUND          ADVANCING + LEADERSHIP
```

They were all **more than defenders.**

Scirea joined the attack.

Baresi moved the whole defensive line.

Koeman scored goals.

Matthäus and Sammer brought a midfielder's vision into defence.

**They were the heirs of Beckenbauer's Libero 2.0.**

# MORE THAN DEFENDERS

---

# Conclusion — The Last Liberos

Scirea, Baresi, Koeman, Matthäus, Sammer.

They created the Libero's final golden age.

But at the same time, football was already changing.

The moment Baresi became Sacchi's line leader,

the end of the Libero had already begun.

> **The last Liberos were not defeated. Football simply found a way to defend without them.**
