# China — 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup Opponent Analysis

## Steel Roses

> Data as of 17 October 2026. The 2027 Women's World Cup will be held in Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027. The final tournament draw has not yet taken place.
>
> **Purpose:** a scouting report for coaching staff — how China play, who decides their games, where they are vulnerable, and a concrete game plan.

---

```text

BASE FORMATION      4-4-2 / 4-2-3-1 — compact mid-block, selective high press
CAPTAIN             Wu Haiyan (Wuhan Jiangda, 150 caps)

QUALIFICATION       2026 Asian Cup QF winners (14 March 2026, 2-0 aet vs Chinese Taipei)

RECORD (12 MONTHS)  W 10  D 1  L 7 — lost the last two (DPRK, Korea) and both vs Russia
```

**One-line verdict:** A disciplined, foul-heavy, compact defensive side that "makes you earn every inch," built around 31-year-old Wang Shuang. But they **struggle to score against anyone organised**, they were **crushed 8-0 by England**, and their results against Asian rivals have slipped in the second half of 2026.

## 5. Head Coach — Ante Milicic

- Former Australia coach (2019–2020), now coaching China.
- After the Asian Cup SF: "I thought we were brave. We tried to press the opposition at times, but in the end they have that individual quality and that was probably the difference."
- Says the players have been "open to a new way of playing… learning… training," and that China are "on the right trajectory" for 2027.
- Before the semi-final he called China underdogs who would not be intimidated by the crowd.

### 6.2 Defensive Block — Their Strength
- Against Australia: China "shut down the lanes, frustrated our midfielders, and made us earn every single inch of grass" (Matildas-side commentary).
- **Selective high pressing** — Milicic: "We tried to press the opposition at times."

### 6.3 Fouls as Defence
- Three yellow cards vs the DPRK; China "relied on fouls" (SBS).
- Wang Shuang booked in consecutive Asian Cup games → **missed the semi-final.**

### 6.4 Attacking Routes

| Route | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Runs in behind from full-back | Zhang Chengxue got behind the DPRK line → cross → Wang Shuang (45+) |
| Long shots | Chen Qiaozhu from the edge of the box (34' vs DPRK) |
| Wang Shuang's movement | 49 international goals; 2 vs Hong Kong, 109' winner vs Vietnam |
| Midfield runners | Wang Yanwen (Dijon) hat-trick vs the Philippines |

### 6.5 Set Pieces
- **For:** few goals from set plays.
- **Against:** Korea's **4th-minute free-kick header** decided the bronze match.

### 6.6 Game-State Behaviour

| State | What China do |
|---|---|
| 0-0 | Compact block, foul to stop transitions |
| Trailing | React fast (equalised within 2' vs DPRK) — but rarely against strong teams |
| Against a deep block | Struggle: 0-0 after 90' vs Chinese Taipei and Vietnam |
| Against an elite press | Collapse early (England 3 goals by 16'; Scotland 3 by 15') |

## 10. Signature Tactics — Analysis of Seven Games

### Game ① 9 March 2026, Asian Cup — DPRK 1-2 China (Sydney)
| Minute | Moment |
|---|---|
| 32' | DPRK lead (Kim Kyong-yong) |
| 34' | **Chen Qiaozhu** from the edge of the box |
| 45+ | **Wang Shuang** — Zhang Chengxue behind the line, cross; VAR overturned offside |
| 80' | DPRK equaliser ruled out for offside |
**Lesson:** China **react instantly to conceding** and punish lapses — then defend with discipline.

### Game ② 14 March 2026, Asian Cup QF — China 2-0 Chinese Taipei (aet)
- 0-0 after 90'; Wang Shuang's goal ruled out; Shao 94'; **penalty saved** 112'; own goal 118'.
**Lesson:** China **cannot break deep blocks** — 118 minutes to beat a much weaker side.

### Game ③ 17 March 2026, Asian Cup SF — China 1-2 Australia (Perth)
- Without Wang Shuang: Foord 17'; Zhang Linyan pen 26' (Arnold charged out); Kerr 58'.
**Lesson:** Organised and brave — **but no match-winner without Wang Shuang.**

### Game ④ 29 November 2025 — England 8-0 China (Wembley)
- Mead 12', 14'; Hemp 16' — **three goals in four minutes.** "Under-cooked" after four months without a game.
**Lesson:** Against an elite pressing team, **China's block collapses early** — they cannot play out under pressure.

### Game ⑤ 29 September 2026, Asian Games SF — DPRK 2-0 China
- DPRK 60%+ possession; China fouled (three yellows); own goal from an aerial duel (53'); Myong 79'.
**Lesson:** **Sustained possession and aerial pressure** break China's block in the end.

### Game ⑥ 2 October 2026, Asian Games Bronze — Korea Republic 1-0 China
- **Noh Jin-young header 4'** from a free kick; no Chinese shot on target in the first half.
**Lesson:** **An early set-piece goal** leaves China without a plan to come back.

### Game ⑦ 2023 World Cup — China 1-6 England
- The same story as 2025: early goals, collapse.

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

1. **Press high from the first whistle.** England scored three by 16', Scotland three by 15'. China cannot play out under pressure.
2. **Score early from a set piece.** Korea's 4' header beat them.
3. **Keep the ball and be patient.** The DPRK's 60% possession wore China down; aerial pressure forced an own goal.
4. **Man-mark Wang Shuang, and provoke her.** She is their only proven scorer, and she picks up bookings.
5. **Win fouls near the box.** China foul a lot — set pieces are our best chance against their block.
6. **If they score, stay calm for the next five minutes.** China equalised two minutes after conceding vs the DPRK — but their reaction usually comes immediately.

```text
EARLY HIGH PRESS → FORCE ERRORS IN THE FIRST 15'
              +
EARLY SET-PIECE GOAL
              +
PATIENT POSSESSION + AERIAL PRESSURE
              +
SHUT DOWN (AND PROVOKE) WANG SHUANG
              ↓
CHINA HAVE NO PLAN B ONCE THEY ARE BEHIND
```

---

## 13. Conclusion

China are disciplined and organised, and they make opponents work for everything. But their weaknesses are clear and repeatable:

1. **Collapses against elite pressing**
2. **No goals against organised defences**
3. **Early set pieces against them**
4. **Dependence on a 31-year-old striker who picks up bookings**

> **China make you earn every inch. Earn it early, and they have no way back.**
