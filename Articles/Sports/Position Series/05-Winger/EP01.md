# SERIES 05 — The Evolution of the Winger

# EP01 — Why Did Wingers Stop Crossing?

## How Did Wingers Evolve From Crossers Into Goalscorers?

There was a time when the winger's job looked simple.

Receive wide.

Beat the full-back.

Reach the byline.

Cross to the striker.

```text
WINGER
   ↓
   ↓
BYLINE
   ↓
CROSS → ST
```

A good winger provided width, beat defenders and delivered quality crosses.

David Beckham.

Ryan Giggs.

Luís Figo.

These players help us understand that era.

Now look at many of the most dangerous wide attackers of modern football.

Mohamed Salah.

Vinícius Júnior.

Kylian Mbappé when starting from the left.

Or players like Lamine Yamal and Cole Palmer, who can create attacks from wide areas.

We no longer judge them mainly by crossing.

We expect:

**Goals. Assists. Dribbles. Final Actions.**

The winger's responsibility has changed.

So what happened?

**Why did the winger evolve from Cross Provider into Goal Threat?**

---

# 1. The Traditional Winger's First Job — Provide Width

Traditional wingers often started extremely wide.

```text
LW                         RW
│                           │
│                           │
│
        CM       CM

           ST
```

Why?

To stretch the defence.

If the winger stayed close to the touchline, the opposing full-back often had to follow.

```text
WINGER ●       ● FB
   │
Touchline
```

That could stretch the defensive line and create more room elsewhere.

Once the winger beat the full-back, the natural next action was:

**Cross.**

```text
Winger → Byline
           ↓
         CROSS
           ↓
           ST
```

The division of responsibility was relatively clear.

**Winger creates.
Striker finishes.**

---

# 2. But Crossing Is a Difficult Way to Finish an Attack

Crossing remains a dangerous weapon.

But structurally, it has one major limitation.

Even a good cross does not guarantee that the attacker will reach the ball first.

```text
          CROSS
            ↓

      ST     CB     CB
       ↑      ↑      ↑
       Who wins?
```

The striker must compete with centre-backs.

The goalkeeper may intervene.

The quality of the delivery, movement and timing all matter.

Now compare that with a wide attacker carrying the ball directly into the penalty area.

```text
WINGER + BALL
      ↓
     BOX
      ↓
PASS / SHOT
```

The attacker keeps control of the decision for longer.

Shoot?

Pass?

Dribble again?

Cut the ball back?

Modern attacking structures increasingly created situations where wide players could participate directly in the final action instead of simply delivering the ball toward someone else.

---

# 3. Full-Backs Started Doing the Winger's Old Job

One of the biggest changes came from another position.

The **full-back**.

Traditionally, the winger was usually the main provider of width.

```text
Traditional

LW                    RW

LB                    RB
```

But as full-backs became increasingly aggressive in attack, the structure could change.

```text
LB ↑                 ↑ RB

   LW              RW
      ↘          ↙
```

The full-back could occupy the outside lane.

That gave the winger somewhere else to go.

**Inside.**

The responsibilities could now be divided:

```text
Full-back → WIDTH

Winger → GOAL THREAT
```

This was a major tactical shift.

If another player could provide width, the winger no longer had to remain permanently attached to the touchline.

He could move closer to goal.

---

# 4. Moving Closer to Goal Changes the Winger's Value

What is the most valuable area in football?

Ultimately, the area around the goal.

From a deep wide position:

```text
WINGER ● ---------------- GOAL
```

the winger is still far away from scoring.

Move inside and the picture changes.

```text
           ● WINGER
             ↓
            BOX
             ↓
            GOAL
```

Now the winger can become the finisher himself.

That changes the basic attacking question.

The traditional winger often asked:

**"How can I deliver the ball to the striker?"**

The modern winger increasingly asks:

**"How can I threaten the goal myself?"**

This is more than a positional change.

It is a change in **attacking responsibility**.

---

# 5. One Goalscorer Is Easier to Defend Than Three

A simplified traditional attack might look like this:

```text
LW →          ← RW

        ST
        ↓
       GOAL
```

The wide players supply the striker.

That makes the main scoring reference relatively obvious.

**The No.9.**

But what happens when both wingers also become serious scoring threats?

```text
      LW      ST      RW
       ↓       ↓       ↓
     GOAL    GOAL    GOAL
```

Now the centre-backs cannot focus only on the striker.

The full-backs are no longer defending players whose only objective is to cross.

The defence must manage several players capable of finishing the attack.

This became one of the defining strengths of the modern **Front Three**.

Goals no longer need to come mainly from the centre-forward.

---

# 6. Salah — The Numbers We Expect From a Winger Changed

Mohamed Salah is difficult to explain as a traditional right winger.

He may start from the right.

But his destination is often the goal.

```text
RIGHT SIDE
     ↓
   SALAH
     ↙
       ↙
        BOX
         ↓
        GOAL
```

With players like Salah, the way we evaluate wingers changed.

We do not only ask:

How good is his crossing?

We also ask:

How many goals does he score?

How often does he enter the box?

How many chances does he create?

How decisive is he in the final third?

The language of evaluating wingers shifted.

**From Crossing Output to Goal Contribution.**

---

# 7. Vinícius — Width Is Still Valuable

This does not mean every modern winger simply moves into the centre.

Vinícius Júnior shows why width is still extremely important.

He can receive very wide and isolate the opposing full-back.

```text
Touchline

VINÍCIUS ●
      ↓
      ↓
     1v1
```

That wide starting position gives him room to accelerate and attack his defender.

But beating the full-back is no longer necessarily followed by an immediate cross.

```text
Beat Defender
      ↓
Enter Box
      ↓
PASS / SHOT
```

The width is the **starting point**.

The penalty area is often the **destination**.

That distinction is important.

Modern football did not eliminate wide play.

It changed what players do **after winning the wide area**.

---

# 8. The Modern Winger's Most Important Destination — The Box

A simplified image of the traditional winger might look like this:

```text
┌────────────────────────┐
│                    GOAL│
│                        │
│                        │
│ WINGER ↓               │
│ WINGER ↓ → CROSS       │
└────────────────────────┘
```

The modern winger increasingly attacks this area:

```text
┌────────────────────────┐
│        ★ BOX ★     GOAL│
│           ↑            │
│        WINGER ↗        │
│                        │
│                        │
└────────────────────────┘
```

Inside or around the box, the winger can:

Shoot.

Play a cut-back.

Attack a rebound.

Combine with the striker.

Or arrive at the far post to finish an attack from the opposite side.

So the key question changed from:

**"Can this winger deliver a good cross?"**

to:

**"How dangerous is this winger around the penalty area?"**

---

# 9. Crossing Did Not Disappear

There is an important distinction here.

Modern football did not decide that crossing was useless.

Crossing remains a major attacking weapon.

What changed was **who delivers it and from where**.

Traditionally:

```text
Winger → CROSS
```

Modern teams can create deliveries in several ways:

```text
Full-back → CROSS

Winger → CUT-BACK

Midfielder → HALF-SPACE CROSS

Opposite Winger → FAR-POST FINISH
```

Crossing became less exclusively connected to the winger.

It became a **team function**.

And once someone else could provide the delivery, the winger gained more freedom to become the player receiving it.

That is a crucial part of the evolution.

---

# 10. The Evolution Was Really an Exchange of Roles

The transformation can be simplified like this.

### Traditional Structure

```text
WINGER
   ↓
Provide Width
   ↓
Beat Full-back
   ↓
Cross
   ↓
STRIKER SCORES
```

### Modern Structure

```text
FULL-BACK
   ↓
Provide Width

WINGER
   ↓
Attack Inside
   ↓
Create / Shoot
   ↓
GOAL THREAT
```

The winger did not suddenly decide that crossing was outdated.

The distribution of **space and responsibility within the team changed**.

Different positions began performing parts of the winger's traditional job.

That freed the winger to perform new ones.

---

# Conclusion — The Winger Moved Closer to Goal

So why did the winger evolve from crosser to goalscorer?

There was no single reason.

Full-backs increasingly provided width.

That allowed wingers to attack more central spaces.

Moving closer to the penalty area created more shooting opportunities.

Teams wanted goals from several attackers rather than relying entirely on the striker.

And the winger's final actions became more diverse.

The fundamental question changed.

The old question was:

**"Can you beat the full-back and cross?"**

The modern question is:

**"Can you decide the attack?"**

Cross.

Pass.

Dribble.

Assist.

Shoot.

Score.

The winger is no longer simply the player who helps the striker finish the attack.

**He can become the player who finishes it himself.**

Wingers did not stop crossing.

**They moved closer to goal.**
