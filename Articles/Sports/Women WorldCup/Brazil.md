# Brazil — 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup Opponent Analysis

## A Seleção (Host Nation)

> Data as of 17 October 2026. The 2027 Women's World Cup will be held in Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027. The final tournament draw has not yet taken place.
>
> **Purpose:** a scouting report for coaching staff — how Brazil play, who decides their games, where they are vulnerable, and a concrete game plan to beat them.

**One-line verdict:** An aggressive, man-oriented, high-pressing team with elite pace and physical forwards, who will play every World Cup game in front of 30,000–70,000 home fans. They can beat anyone in the first 20 minutes — but **they have had a player sent off in five different games in the last 15 months**, and they leave big spaces behind their press.

## 5. Head Coach — Arthur Elias

```text
2016–2023  Corinthians — built the dominant club side in South America
Sep 2023   Appointed Brazil coach (replacing Pia Sundhage)
Aug 2024   Paris Olympic silver
Aug 2025   Copa América winners
Apr 2026   FIFA Series winners (unbeaten)
Jun 2026   Booked for his shirt colour, then sent off (77') vs the USA
```

### Profile
- **Collective football, not stars:** he wants a team that "dictates the tempo" with high synchronisation between the back line and the goalkeeper.
- **Pressing is non-negotiable.** FIFA's report on the October 2026 Argentina game: Brazil "stuck to their playing philosophy, pressing high, working tirelessly to regain possession and looking to attack at every opportunity."
- **Brave in-game decisions:** in the Copa final he made substitutions in first-half stoppage time to rebalance the team — and Brazil came back from 1-2 and 2-3.
- **Uses the whole squad:** more than 50 players called up in 12 months; a generational mix of Marta (40) and teenagers (Kaylane, 17).
- **Temperament:** his own dismissal in Fortaleza set the tone for eight red cards. **His bench is part of the emotional risk.**

## 6. Tactics and Formation

### 6.1 Base Shape — 3-4-3

```text
OUT OF POSSESSION (HIGH, MAN-ORIENTED PRESS)

     DUDINHA          ZANERATTO          KEROLIN          ← FRONT THREE JUMP ONTO CBs/GK
 ISABELA (WB)                                  YASMIM (WB) ← WING-BACKS JUMP ONTO FULL-BACKS
           ANGELINA              DUDA SAMPAIO              ← MIDFIELD MARKS MAN-FOR-MAN
      LAUREN           TARCIANE           RAFAELLE          ← BACK THREE, OFTEN 3v3 OR 2v2
                       LORENA
```

```text
IN POSSESSION (3-2-5)

 ISABELA   DUDINHA     ZANERATTO     KEROLIN    YASMIM     ← FIVE ON THE LAST LINE
              ANGELINA          DUDA SAMPAIO               ← DOUBLE PIVOT
      LAUREN           TARCIANE           RAFAELLE
                       LORENA
```

```text
SETTLED DEFENCE (LOW 5-4-1 / 5-2-3)

                ZANERATTO
   DUDINHA                       KEROLIN        ← WIDE FORWARDS NARROW, READY TO COUNTER
         ANGELINA      DUDA
 ISABELA  LAUREN  TARCIANE  RAFAELLE  YASMIM    ← VERY LOW LINE
                 LORENA
```

### 6.2 Pressing — the identity

| Element | How it works | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Back-pass to a centre-back or goalkeeper; short goal kick | Zaneratto's 9' goal vs England came **from a wayward goal kick by debutant Khiara Keating** after Brazil pressed England's makeshift back line |
| **Structure** | Man-for-man once the ball goes back; near-side winger attacks the space left by an advancing full-back | Total Football Analysis (2024); TNT: Brazil "marked man-to-man across the pitch" |
| **Goal** | Win the ball in the final third and score within three passes | Paredes own goal 5' vs Spain (2024 SF) after Cata Coll's error under pressure |
| **Cost** | Back three left in 1v1 or 2v2 | Bia Zaneratto: the aggressive press "leaves the team exposed at the back and often in 1v1 situations" |

### 6.3 Build-Up
- Goalkeeper Lorena (KIC 78) plays short to the back three; if pressed, long to Zaneratto, who holds the ball and lays it off.
- The double pivot (Angelina, Duda Sampaio) drops to receive; the wing-backs push very high.
- **Throw-ins are an attacking weapon** — Taina Maranhão scored **unmarked with a header from a throw-in** vs the USA (11').

### 6.4 Final-Third Patterns

```text
PATTERN A — "WIN IT AND GO"           PATTERN B — STRIKER LAY-OFF
  press → regain → 1–2 passes            long ball → ZANERATTO holds
  → finish (ENG 9', USA 14')             → lay-off → DUDINHA/KEROLIN run beyond
                                         (ENG 18': Zaneratto → Dudinha)

PATTERN C — LATE BOX ARRIVALS          PATTERN D — LONG SHOTS / SECOND BALLS
  wing-back cross → far-post run          MARTA 90+6' (Copa final) from outside the box
  (Adriana header vs Spain 2024)          Gutierres low shot 80' (Copa final)
```

### 6.5 Transitions
- **Defence → attack:** the front three narrow in a settled block so that the moment Brazil win the ball, three fast forwards are already in central positions. France (2024 QF) controlled the game and lost to **Brazil's first shot on target** (Gabi Portilho, 82').
- **Attack → defence:** this is where Brazil break. Colombia's 88' goal in the Copa final came from **a quick counter-attack finished by Caicedo.**

### 6.6 Set Pieces
- **For:** tall, physical targets (Ludmila PHY 81, Lauren 81, Rafaelle 80, Gutierres 77); throw-ins aimed at the near post.
- **Against:** conceded a free kick (Leicy Santos 115', Copa final) and **four penalties in 12 months** (Stanway, Norway, Argentina 55', plus the late shootout pressure).

### 6.7 Game-State Behaviour

| State | What Brazil do |
|---|---|
| 0-0, first 20' | Maximum pressing, fast starts — **most dangerous phase** |
| Leading | Drop into a low 5-4-1, counter with three forwards; **lose control of the second half** (USA, Spain) |
| Trailing | Throw bodies forward, long shots, late subs — **never give up** (Copa final 90+6', 105') |
| Down to ten | Compact, physical, man-marking — held England for 70 minutes |

## 10. Signature Tactics — Analysis of Eight Games Against Strong Opponents

### Game ① 3 August 2024, Olympic QF — France 0-1 Brazil (Nantes)

| Item | Detail |

**How they won:** Absorbed pressure, stayed compact, struck once on the counter.
**Lesson for coaches:** Brazil are **comfortable without the ball.** Do not assume possession dominance means control.

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

### Strategy ① — Survive the First 20 Minutes

- **Evidence:** ENG 9', 18'; USA 11', 14'; ARG 13'; POR 1'.
- **Instructions:**
  - First 20 minutes: **no short goal kicks** — go long to our target striker.
  - Centre-backs and goalkeeper avoid risky passes; clear wide if pressed.
  - Midfield stays compact; no turnovers in our own half.

### Strategy ② — Beat the First Line, Then Attack the Back Three

```text
BRAZIL PRESS: 3 FORWARDS + 2 WING-BACKS JUMP
                     ↓
ONE PASS OVER/THROUGH THE FIRST LINE (GK → 9 → lay-off, or CB → wide midfielder)
                     ↓
BRAZIL BACK THREE LEFT 3v3 OR 2v2 · FORWARDS DON'T TRACK BACK (DEF 38–43)
                     ↓
RUN AT RAFAELLE (PAC 51) — 1v1 IN BEHIND
```

- **Evidence:** USA 2024 final (Swanson 57'), Colombia 88' counter.
- **Personnel:** our fastest forward plays on Rafaelle's side; a strong target striker to hold up long balls.

### Strategy ③ — Keep Our Composure, Make Them Lose Theirs

- **Evidence:** red cards in five games; eight in Fortaleza; the coach sent off.
- **Instructions:**
  - Never react to hard tackles or provocation.
  - Keep the ball in the corners late; slow restarts within the laws.
  - Draw fouls near the box — Brazil concede penalties (three in 12 months).
  - Captain talks to the referee calmly; let Brazil's frustration grow.

### Strategy ④ — Take Over the Second Half

- **Evidence:** USA dominated the second half in São Paulo; Spain scored at 85' and 90+12'; Japan scored at 90+2' and 90+6'; Colombia scored four.
- **Instructions:**
  - Bring on fresh pace around **60 minutes**.
  - Push our full-backs higher as Brazil's press fades.
  - Target the last 15 minutes — Brazil defend deep and give up second balls.

### Strategy ⑤ — Mark at Throw-Ins and the Far Post

- **Evidence:** Maranhão unmarked from a throw-in (USA 11'); Adriana's far-post header (Spain 2024); Marta redirecting a cross (Copa 105').
- **Instructions:** man-mark at long throws, not just corners; our weak-side full-back tucks in to the far post.

### Strategy ⑥ — Double Up on the Ball-Side Winger

- Kerolin, Luany and Ludmila have PAC 85–87.
- Keep our full-backs a few metres deeper than usual; the near-side midfielder doubles; force them inside into traffic.

### Strategy ⑦ — Shoot Low and Early Against Lorena

- REF 86 — she wins the reflex saves. Low, early, near-post shots before she sets.

### Summary — The Formula for Beating Brazil

```text
SURVIVE THE FIRST 20 MINUTES (PLAY LONG, NO TURNOVERS)
                       +
BEAT THE FIRST LINE → ATTACK THE BACK THREE / RAFAELLE (PAC 51)
                       +
STAY CALM · DRAW FOULS · LET BRAZIL'S EMOTIONS WORK AGAINST THEM
                       +
FRESH PACE AT 60' — TAKE OVER THE SECOND HALF
                       +
MARK THROW-INS AND THE FAR POST
                       ↓
TURN THE HOME CROWD'S EXPECTATION INTO PRESSURE ON BRAZIL
```

## 13. Conclusion

Brazil are the most emotional and the most dangerous home side imaginable: fast, physical and aggressive, with elite players at Barcelona, Atlético Madrid, Lyon, PSG and the NWSL. In the last year they beat England, the USA, Italy and Canada.

But four weaknesses are proven and repeatable:

1. **Discipline** — red cards in five games, eight in one night, the coach included
2. **Exposure behind the high press**, with forwards who do not defend and a slow captain
3. **Losing control after scoring** — late goals conceded against the USA, Spain, Japan and Colombia
4. **Soft goals** — two own goals and three penalties in 12 months

> **Survive Brazil's storm, keep your head when they lose theirs, and the game — and the stadium — turns against them.**
