# Canada — 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup Opponent Analysis

## CanWNT

> Data as of 17 October 2026. The 2027 Women's World Cup will be held in Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027. The final tournament draw has not yet taken place.
>
> ⚠️ **Qualification status:** not yet qualified. Canada enter the **CONCACAF W Championship**: quarter-final vs **Panama (54th), 28 November 2026**, Mansfield, Texas. The four semi-finalists qualify directly; the tournament is also the 2028 Olympic qualifier. Qualification is highly likely.
>
> **Purpose:** a scouting report for coaching staff — how Canada play, who decides their games, where they are vulnerable, and a concrete game plan.

**One-line verdict:** An **elite, physical back line** (Gilles 84, Rose 83, Buchanan 82, Lawrence 83 — all at Bayern, City, Chelsea and Lyon) with a goalkeeper at 82, but **one of the weakest attacks of any top-ten team**. Canada **failed to score in seven of 13 games**, lost **0-1 five times**, and have **not beaten a top-15 side under Stoney**. When the opposing forwards run at them, as Denmark's did (four goals in Toronto), the defence cracks too.

## 5. Head Coach — Casey Stoney

- Appointed January 2025. Former England captain (130 caps); coached **Manchester United** (promotion to the WSL, FA Cup final) and **San Diego Wave** (2023 NWSL Shield).
- **Philosophy:** "aggression, pressing to win the ball and turning defensive play into scoring chances" (April 2025).
- **Problems she has named:**
  - Finishing: Canada crossed "without enough players in the box and without quality in the delivery" (October 2026).
  - **No win against a top-15 opponent** since she took charge.
  - Injuries and availability: "hampered by injuries and circumstance in assembling its strongest lineup".

## 6. Tactics and Formation

### 6.1 Shapes

```text
IN POSSESSION (4-2-3-1 → 2-3-5)

     O. SMITH          VIENS / HUITEMA            PRINCE / LACASSE
                       FLEMING
              GROSSO              AWUJO / REGAN
   LAWRENCE (high)   GILLES      ROSE / BUCHANAN        SONIS / CARLE (high)
                       SHERIDAN

OUT OF POSSESSION (4-4-2 HIGH PRESS → 4-4-2 MID BLOCK)

               VIENS          FLEMING
   SMITH      GROSSO       AWUJO        PRINCE
   CARLE      GILLES       ROSE / BUCHANAN    LAWRENCE
                       SHERIDAN
```

### 6.2 Build-Up
- **Lawrence** (Lyon, PAS 82, PAC 83) — the main progressive player; pushes high from full-back.
- The centre-backs **are not passers**: Gilles PAS 54, Rose PAS 54, Buchanan PAS 58.
- **Fleming** (PAS 78) drops to receive; **Grosso** (PAS 78) links.
- Result: Canada often **bypass midfield with long balls or wide play**.

### 6.3 Attacking Entry
1. **Crosses from the full-backs** — the main route, but low quality per Stoney.
2. **Olivia Smith 1v1** (Arsenal, PAC 84, DRI 83) — scored at 84' and 70' vs Denmark.
3. **Set pieces** — Gilles (three goals in 2026), Collins, Buchanan.
4. **Penalties and late goals** — Sonis (40 goals in 132 caps).

### 6.4 Pressing
- Aggressive 4-4-2 press, with Fleming jumping onto the opponents' No. 6.
- **Risk:** the back line is very slow to turn (Gilles PAC 57, Buchanan 65). Denmark scored three in nine minutes by attacking the space behind the press.

### 6.5 Game-State Behaviour

| State | What Canada do | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0-0 vs a top team | Solid, but no goal threat | Five 0-1 defeats |
| Trailing by one | **Crosses, long balls, rarely enough** | Japan, USA, Brazil, Netherlands, Switzerland |
| Trailing by two or more | Comes back partly | Denmark: 1-3 → 3-4 |
| Leading | Rare against top teams; defend well | Korea 3-1, Colombia 4-1 |

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

### Strategy 1: Score first — Canada rarely come back
- **Evidence:** five 0-1 defeats; only one comeback (Denmark 0-1 → 1-1 at 84').
- **How:** attack with intent in the first half; Canada are vulnerable around 43'–47'.

### Strategy 2: Fast combinations and runs in behind
- **Evidence:** Denmark (three goals in nine minutes), Japan (0-3).
- **How:** one-touch play around the press; diagonal runs between Gilles and Buchanan; a quick striker who turns them.

### Strategy 3: Press the centre-backs — let them have the ball
- **Evidence:** Gilles and Rose PAS 54, Buchanan 58.
- **How:** block passes into Fleming and Lawrence; force the CBs to play forward; win the second ball.

### Strategy 4: Defend crosses with your strongest headers
- **Evidence:** Gilles three goals in 2026; Canada's attack lives on crosses and set pieces.
- **How:** zonal marking on corners; block the crosser; no fouls near the box.

### Strategy 5: Counter down Canada's wings
- **Evidence:** Smith DEF 25, Lacasse 26, Viens 28; Lawrence pushes very high.
- **How:** win the ball and play quickly into the space behind Lawrence.

### Strategy 6: Win the midfield duels
- **Evidence:** Fleming PHY 56, Grosso 58.
- **How:** a physical No. 6 and No. 8 who press Fleming whenever she receives.

```text
SCORE FIRST
      +
FAST COMBINATIONS → RUN BEHIND THE SLOW CBs
      +
PRESS THE PASSING LANES, NOT THE CBs
      +
DEFEND CROSSES AND SET PIECES
      +
COUNTER BEHIND LAWRENCE
      ↓
FIVE 0-1 DEFEATS — CANADA HAVE NO PLAN B
```

## 13. Conclusion

Canada have one of the best defensive groups at the World Cup on paper — **Gilles, Rose, Buchanan, Lawrence and Sheridan all rated 82–84** and all at elite clubs. But the numbers show a team that **cannot score against good opponents** (four goals in six games against the USA, Japan, Brazil, the Netherlands and Denmark), **loses tight games 0-1**, and has a slow back line that Denmark tore apart with **three goals in nine minutes**.

> **Score first, play quickly around Canada's giants, and make them chase a game they cannot win from behind.**
