## 5. Head Coach — Kano Michihisa

```text
Dec 2024  Nils Nielsen (Denmark) appointed — the first foreign coach of the women's national team
Feb 2025  SheBelieves Cup winners
Mar 2026  Asian Cup winners
Apr 2026  Nielsen leaves (contract expiry / some reports: dismissed for a perceived "lack of passion")
Apr 2026  Kano Michihisa: assistant → interim → permanent head coach
```

### Kano's Profile
- The JFA stated its aims as **continuity** and **high-intensity football.** Kano emphasises **aggression, toughness and vigour** in both attack and defence.
- **"Soko-age" (raising the floor):** reducing dependence on first-choice players and widening the squad.
- **Heavy experimentation:**
  - Played centre-back Koga Toko as a forward.
  - Used winger Hamada Megu at right-back.
  - At the Asian Games semi-final, used striker Ueno Mami as a defensive midfielder.
  - Changes shape during games: 4-2-3-1 → 4-3-3 → 3-4-1-2.
- **Criticism:** Substitutions came too late in the second South Africa game. He skipped the official post-match interview, which drew criticism of his leadership. He also left out Ueki, one of the Asian Cup's top scorers, without a clear explanation.

**Analytical point:** Six months into the job, **the team's structure is not yet fixed.** Experimentation is likely to continue up to the World Cup.

## 6. Tactics and Formation

### Base Formation — 4-2-3-1 (Close to a 2-3-5 in Possession)

```text
OUT OF POSSESSION (4-4-2 / 4-2-3-1 BLOCK)

             HAMANO     TANIKAWA        ← FIRST PRESSING LINE (2)
   MIYAZAWA                    FUJINO
             HASEGAWA   NAGANO          ← CENTRAL SCREEN
 KITAGAWA   MINAMI    KOGA    SHIMIZU
                YAMASHITA
```

```text
IN POSSESSION (CLOSE TO A 2-3-5)

   MIYAZAWA  TANIKAWA  HAMANO  MATSUKUBO  FUJINO   ← FIVE ATTACK THE BACK LINE
        KITAGAWA    HASEGAWA     SHIMIZU            ← FULL-BACKS HIGH, HASEGAWA CENTRAL
              MINAMI          KOGA                  ← CENTRE-BACKS SPLIT WIDE
                    YAMASHITA
```

### ① Build-Up — The "Hasegawa Axis"
- The two centre-backs, **Minami** (Brighton) and **Koga** (Tottenham), split wide to either side of the box.
- **Hasegawa** drops between or just in front of them to receive the first pass.
- Hasegawa opens her body to face forward, then chooses one of two options:
  - **(a)** a direct forward pass into Tanikawa or Matsukubo between the lines;
  - **(b)** a switch to the far full-back.
- GK **Yamashita** (EA FC 27 KIC 80) joins the build-up too. Against a strong press, she goes long to the flanks.

### ② Entry — Half-Space Triangles and the "Third-Man Run"

```text
         [WINGER MOVES INSIDE] FUJINO
                    ↙ PASS
 [FULL-BACK OVERLAPS] SHIMIZU → → → RUN OUTSIDE
                    ↑
       [ATTACKING MF] TANIKAWA → ONE-TWO → RUN INTO THE BOX
```

- The winger starts on the touchline and moves inside.
- The full-back overlaps outside her.
- The attacking midfielder plays a one-two and runs into the box — **the third-man run.**
- Real example (Germany, October 2026, opening goal): Tanikawa received Fujino's pass, created space inside the box and finished.

### ③ Pressing — A High Press in a 4-4-2 Shape
- The attacking midfielder steps up alongside the centre-forward to form a **two-player first pressing line.**
- **Pressing triggers:**
  - when an opposing centre-back passes wide;
  - when an opponent receives with her back to goal;
  - when the ball is played back to the opposing goalkeeper.
- At the 2023 World Cup against Spain, AP reported that Japan "time and again swarmed the Spanish offence, stole the ball, and moved up the pitch as aggressively as possible."

### ④ When Leading — A Low, Narrow Block
- With a one-goal lead, Japan drop their two-line block to the edge of the box.
- In the 2026 Asian Cup final, they conceded 54% possession to Australia and still held on to win 1-0.

### ⑤ Transitions — Finish Within Three or Four Passes of Winning the Ball
- Many of the four goals against Spain in 2023 came from quick counter-attacks straight after winning the ball.

### ⑥ Set Pieces
- **Takers:** Tanikawa and Fujino
- **Targets:** Kumagai, Koga, Minami
- Real examples:
  - 2026 Asian Cup semi-final vs Korea Republic, 75': Kumagai headed in Tanikawa's swinging corner.
  - 2026 Asian Cup quarter-final vs Philippines: Koga scored two headers.

### ⑦ Kano-Era Variations

| Formation | Used in | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 4-2-3-1 | Most games | Default |
| 4-3-3 | Asian Games QF vs Philippines (second-half change) | More attackers |
| 3-4-1-2 | Haiti, October 2026 (after the left-back came off) | Testing a front two with wing-backs |

## 9. Top Six Players to Watch

### ① Hasegawa Yui — The Heart of Japan

**MF · Manchester City · Captain · EA FC 27 OVR 88 (PAS 86 / DRI 88 / DEF 81)**

- **Role:** The axis of the build-up. She beats the press with her first touch and plays forward passes.
- **Defending:** Interceptions 87, defensive awareness 88. She is not only a playmaker but also **the centre of Japan's ball-winning.**
- **Match examples:**
  - 2025 SheBelieves Cup vs USA (2-1 win): Japan were said to have "outworked and outthought" the USA — with Hasegawa at the heart of that midfield.
  - 2026 Asian Cup: the centre of a defensive structure that conceded only once in six games.
- **Weakness:** Strength 55. **Strong physical pressure can make her lose the ball.**

### ② Hamano Maika — The Big-Game Match-Winner

**FW/CAM · Chelsea · EA FC 27 OVR 79 (DRI 81 / PAC 76)**

- **Match examples:**
  - **18 March 2026, Asian Cup semi-final vs Korea Republic, 25':** She won the ball back after a deep cross, dribbled past two defenders into the box, and drove a right-footed shot into the roof of the net from a tight angle.
  - **21 March 2026, Asian Cup final vs Australia, 17':** She turned about five metres outside the box and struck powerfully; the ball curled past the diving Arnold into the corner. **The winning goal.**
  - **14 April 2026, away to the USA (Seattle), 27':** She beat defender Lilly Reale with a shot-fake and scored. **The winning goal** — ending the USA's 10-match winning streak, and the first time in 42 games that the USA failed to score.
- **Pattern:** **Turn → shot-fake → powerful right foot.** She shoots directly from outside the box and from wide angles.
- **How to stop her:** Stay tight behind her when she receives, so **she cannot turn.**

### ③ Tanikawa Momoko — The Next Ace

**CAM · FC Bayern · EA FC 27 OVR 79 (SHO 76 / DRI 79)**

- **Match examples:**
  - **July 2024, Paris Olympics vs Brazil, 96':** A 19-year-old substitute, she turned and hit **a first-time shot from 30 metres** for the winner. Japan won 2-1.
  - **March 2026, Asian Cup semi-final vs Korea Republic, 75':** Her swinging corner set up Kumagai's headed goal.
  - **13 October 2026 vs Germany, 15':** Received Fujino's pass, created space inside the box and finished.
  - She also scored in the 5-0 win over South Africa in June 2026.
- **Role:** An attacking midfielder who receives between the lines, and **a set-piece taker.**
- **How to stop her:** Close the space between the lines. A defensive midfielder should track her, and she must not be given space to shoot from outside the box.

### ④ Fujino Aoba — Delivery and Dribbling

**RW · Manchester City · EA FC 27 OVR 82 (DRI 82 / PAS 80 / PAC 79)**

- **Match examples:**
  - **13 October 2026 vs Germany:** Assisted Tanikawa's opener.
  - **March 2026, Asian Cup semi-final vs Korea Republic, 43':** Scored, but the goal was ruled out by VAR.
  - **June 2026 vs South Africa (5-0):** Scored.
- **Role:** Cuts in from the right to shoot with her left foot or play cut-backs. Also takes free kicks and corners.
- **Weakness:** DEF 23. **Japan's right side, where she plays, tracks back poorly.** Our left-back's overlapping runs can exploit it.

### ⑤ Matsukubo Manaka — The Hidden Threat

**CAM · Chelsea · EA FC 27 OVR 83 (SHO 82 / DRI 84)**

- Japan's highest-rated player after Hasegawa and Yamashita.
- **Match examples:**
  - Scored in the 7-0 Asian Cup quarter-final win over the Philippines (March 2026).
  - Started the second South Africa game (June 2026).
- **Profile:** Excellent at keeping the ball in a crowded midfield and getting into scoring positions. **Her SHO 82 is the highest among Japan's attacking midfielders.**
- **Weakness:** PHY 48. **Push her off the ball physically.**

### ⑥ Koga Toko — The Set-Piece Weapon

**CB · Tottenham · EA FC 27 OVR 78 (DEF 81 / PHY 72)**

## 10. Signature Tactics — Analysis of Seven Games Against Strong Opponents

### Game ① 2023 World Cup, Group Stage — Japan 4-0 Spain

**(A crushing win over the eventual world champions)**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Goals | Miyazawa 2, Ueki 1, Tanaka 1 |
| Possession | Spain well ahead |
| Tactic | Gave up possession and switched to **counter-attacking** |

**How Japan won**
- Japan abandoned the possession football of their first two games and defended in a **3-4-3 block** (under then-coach Ikeda).
- While Spain moved the ball around the flanks, Japan shut down the centre.
- As soon as they won the ball, Miyazawa ran into **the space behind Spain's full-backs.**
- AP reported that Japan "time and again swarmed the Spanish offence, stole the ball, and moved up the pitch as aggressively as possible."

**Lesson:** Japan are **a team that can win without the ball.** An opponent having more possession does not make Japan weaker.

### Game ② 2023 World Cup, Round of 16 — Japan 3-1 Norway

| Minute | Goal |
|---|---|
| 15' | Engen own goal (Japan) |
| 21' | Reiten (Norway) |
| 50' | **Shimizu** (Japan, deflected shot) |
| 81' | **Miyazawa** (Japan, her fifth goal of the tournament → Golden Boot) |

**How Japan won**
- Norway had world-class forwards such as Hegerberg and Graham Hansen. Japan **isolated them with organised defending.**
- After Norway equalised, Japan did not panic and went ahead again through **right-back Shimizu joining the attack.**

**Lesson:** Japan's **full-backs are goal threats.** They get all the way into the box.

### Game ③ 2024 Paris Olympics, Group Stage — Japan 2-1 Brazil

**(A stoppage-time comeback)**

| Minute | Moment |
|---|---|
| First half | Handball penalty → **Tanaka's weak kick saved by GK Lorena** |
| Early second half | Jhennifer opener (Brazil) |
| 90+2' | **Kumagai penalty equaliser** (after VAR) |
| 90+6' | **Tanikawa's 30-metre first-time turning shot for the winner** (a 19-year-old substitute) |

**How Japan won**
- Even trailing 0-1, they kept faith in their short-passing game and kept getting into the box.
- Substitute Tanikawa produced the decisive strike.
- Coach Ikeda said afterwards that a "strong mindset" was the key to the comeback.

**Lessons:**
- Japan are **dangerous until the final minute.**
- **The quality of their substitutes** is high.
- But the missed first-half penalty also showed **a finishing problem in big games.**

### Game ④ 2025 SheBelieves Cup — Japan 2-1 USA (San Diego)

**(The USA's first defeat under Emma Hayes)**

| Minute | Goal |
|---|---|

| 50' | **Koga Toko** (Japan, off the bench) |

**How Japan won**
- **An intense press from kick-off** disrupted the USA's build-up and brought an immediate goal.
- They pressed hard again at the start of the second half to score the winner.
- US media verdict: Japan "outworked and outthought" the USA. Japan created more chances, and US keeper Campbell made several saves.

**Lesson:** Japan press hardest **at the start of each half.** Those ten minutes must be survived.

### Game ⑦ 21 March 2026, Asian Cup Final — Japan 1-0 Australia

- It was the third time Japan had beaten Australia 1-0 in an Asian Cup final (2014, 2018, 2026).

**Lesson:** Japan **know how to win even on a day when they are not at their best.** Nielsen himself said they found a way to win even though they could not find their own rhythm.

## 12. How to Beat This Team

### Strategy ① — Shut Down Hasegawa

```text
       OUR ST           OUR ST
          ↓  BLOCK PASS  ↓
        [HASEGAWA] ← OUR MF MARKS TIGHT
```

- One of our midfielders marks Hasegawa **tightly** so she cannot turn forward.
- Our two forwards **block the passing lanes** from Japan's centre-backs to Hasegawa.
- **Evidence:** Hasegawa's strength is 55. Physical contact from behind makes her more likely to lose the ball.
- Force Japan to build through the flanks or with long balls. Japan are not strong at long-ball football.

### Strategy ② — Win It in the Air and From Set Pieces
- Win as many **corners, free kicks and long throws** as possible. Use wide dribbles and crosses to force corners.
- Keep tall forwards in the box, and prepare **routines that attack the near and far posts at the same time.**
- **Evidence:**
  - Sweden scored their opener from a set piece in the 2023 World Cup quarter-final.
  - South Africa beat Japan in June 2026 with a single headed goal from a corner.
  - The PHY of Japan's first-choice attackers ranges from 48 to 56.
- **Defending Japan's set pieces:** Put dedicated markers on Koga and Kumagai, and prepare for the swinging deliveries of Tanikawa and Fujino.

### Strategy ③ — Make the Second Half Decisive
- In the first half, keep it at **0-0 or within one goal** with a compact block. Japan are at their technical sharpest in the first half.
- Be especially alert **in the first ten minutes of each half.** Japan scored in the 2nd and 50th minutes against the USA in 2025.
- **Bring on fresh, high-energy substitutes around the 60th minute** and raise the intensity of the press.
- **Change our midfield shape during the game** to slow Japan's response — the approach Germany's Wück used.
- **Evidence:**
  - Germany came from 1-0 down with goals in the 28th and 73rd minutes.
  - Australia took control in the second half.
  - The DPRK scored twice, in the 47th and 69th minutes, in the Asian Games final.

### Strategy ④ — Attack the Space Behind High Full-Backs and the Slow Centre-Backs

```text
   JAPAN RB (SHIMIZU) PUSHES HIGH   +   FUJINO (DEF 23) DOES NOT TRACK BACK
                ↓
   SPACE OPENS ON JAPAN'S RIGHT ←←← OUR LEFT WINGER + LEFT-BACK COUNTER
                ↓
        1v1 WITH A SLOW CENTRE-BACK (PAC 60)
```

- Japan's full-backs push high to form the half-space triangles.
- As soon as we win the ball, send **a fast winger into the space behind the full-back.**
- Target **Japan's right side** in particular: Fujino does not track back, leaving Shimizu alone.
- Koga and Minami both have PAC 60. Create **1v1 situations against fast forwards.**

### Strategy ⑤ — Go Over Japan's First Press With Long Balls
- Japan press high.
- Trying to play short against it walks into their trap.
- The goalkeeper or a centre-back should play **long passes over the first pressing line**, with our midfielders winning the second balls from our tall forward.

### Strategy ⑥ — Stay Narrow and Force Them Wide
- Out of possession, **compress the centre** to block the half-space triangles and Tanikawa and Matsukubo between the lines.
- **Give them no space to shoot from outside the box.** Many of Hamano's and Tanikawa's key goals came from outside the box or from its edges.
- Force Japan to cross from wide. Their attackers are small, so they are not competitive in the air.

### Strategy ⑦ — The Psychological Battle: The Quarter-Final Wall
- Japan have repeatedly struggled in tight big games: three straight quarter-final exits, and missed penalties in big games in 2023 and 2024.
- **Keeping the game tight until the very end** puts pressure on Japan in itself.
- Prepare for a penalty shootout too — Japan converted all five of their kicks in the Asian Games final.

### Summary — The Formula for Beating Japan

```text
SHUT DOWN HASEGAWA (PHYSICAL PRESSURE)
              +
SET PIECES · AERIAL DUELS
              +
SURVIVE THE FIRST 10 MINUTES → RAISE THE INTENSITY AFTER 60'
              +
ATTACK JAPAN'S RIGHT SIDE · THE SLOW CENTRE-BACKS
              ↓
BREAK JAPAN'S RHYTHM, AND FIGHT THEM IN THE WAYS THEY ARE WEAKEST
```

## 13. Conclusion

Japan are the world's fifth-ranked team, combining **technique, organisation and experience at top overseas clubs.**

They are Asia's strongest side, winners of both the Asian Cup and the Asian Games — but three weaknesses are clear:

1. **Defending aerial balls and set pieces**
2. **Coping with second-half physical pressure and in-game tactical changes**
3. **Finishing and mental strength in big games against the world's top teams**

> **Fight Japan with technique, and you lose. Fight them in the ways they dislike — height, intensity and time — and you can win.**
