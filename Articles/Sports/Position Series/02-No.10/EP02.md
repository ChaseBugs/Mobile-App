# EP02 — The Masters of No.10

## Zidane, Totti, Del Piero, Kaká — Same Role, Different Genius

The golden age of the No.10 was special not simply because there were many technically gifted players.

What made that era fascinating was this:

**They were all playmakers, but they controlled matches in completely different ways.**

Zidane attracted opponents.

Rui Costa waited for movement.

Totti created and scored.

Del Piero blurred the line between playmaker and forward.

Aimar constantly moved into new passing lanes.

Kaká attacked the defensive line with the ball.

A great No.10 was not just a good passer.

**He forced defenders to make difficult decisions.**

---

## 1. Zidane — Attracting Pressure to Create Space

To understand Zidane, do not look only at his passes.

Watch the opponents around him.

He often did not release the ball immediately.

He waited.

One midfielder approached.

Sometimes a second player came closer.

Then Zidane escaped with a turn, first touch or body movement.

```text
CM →     ZIDANE     ← CM
             ↓
        SPACE OPENS
```

The important point was not simply that he beat a player.

When two opponents moved toward Zidane, someone else became free.

His technique changed the opponent's defensive shape.

### Zidane's pattern

**Attract → Escape → Create**

He invited pressure, escaped it, then used the space created behind the press.

That is why Zidane could control a match without constantly sprinting.

---

## 2. Rui Costa — Seeing Two Seconds Into the Future

Rui Costa's great weapon was not just passing accuracy.

It was **timing**.

A striker starts a run.

The defender reacts.

A small gap appears.

Then the pass comes.

```text
Forward runs
      ↓
Defender reacts
      ↓
Space appears
      ↓
PASS
```

The best playmakers do not only pass to where a teammate is.

They pass to **where the teammate will be**.

That is the difference between seeing the current picture and seeing the next one.

### Rui Costa's key quality

**Vision + Timing**

He made dangerous passes not only because he saw space, but because he understood when that space would become available.

---

## 3. Totti — Creator or Goalscorer?

Defending Francesco Totti created a different problem.

What exactly should you stop?

```text
             TOTTI
          ↙    ↓    ↘
       PASS   SHOT   RUN
```

Block the pass?

He could shoot.

Step forward?

He could release a teammate behind you.

Drop deeper?

He could advance himself.

Totti was both creator and finisher.

### Totti's pattern

**Create + Finish**

He did not simply prepare the final action.

He could become the final action.

That is why Totti is a useful historical reference when looking at modern attacking playmakers such as Cole Palmer.

---

## 4. Del Piero — Living Between Responsibilities

Alessandro Del Piero was not a traditional central midfielder.

He often operated somewhere between a **No.10 and a second striker**.

```text
        ST

   DEL PIERO
        ↘
          ↘
           GOAL
```

That made him difficult to mark.

Should the midfielder follow him?

Should the centre-back step forward?

Should the full-back move inside?

His danger came from occupying areas where defensive responsibility became unclear.

### Del Piero's key weapon

**Positional ambiguity**

The defender's problem was simple:

**"Who is supposed to mark him?"**

Great attacking players often live in that uncertainty.

---

## 5. Aimar — Creating New Passing Angles

Pablo Aimar was different again.

He rarely stayed in one place waiting for the ball.

He moved constantly.

```text
Receive
   ↓
Pass
   ↓
Move
   ↓
Receive again
```

After passing, he immediately searched for another useful position.

This created new triangles.

```text
        Aimar
       /     \
     CM       FW
```

A few seconds later, the triangle could appear somewhere else.

Aimar's game was not about dominating through many touches.

It was about becoming a new passing option again and again.

### Aimar's pattern

**Connect → Move → Connect Again**

This type of mobility can still be seen in modern creative players such as Florian Wirtz.

---

## 6. Kaká — Carrying the Attack Himself

Kaká brought a much more direct form of playmaking.

Many classic creators moved the ball.

Kaká often moved **with the ball**.

```text
KAKÁ + BALL
      ↓
      ↓
      ↓
  Defensive Line
```

When he accelerated through midfield, defenders had to make an immediate choice.

Step forward?

Then space opens behind them.

Stay back?

Kaká keeps advancing.

```text
        CB
        ↓
       KAKÁ
      ↙    ↘
   PASS    SHOT
```

### Kaká's pattern

**Carry → Force → Exploit**

His ball carrying forced defenders to react.

Then he exploited their decision.

This idea remains important in modern players such as Jude Bellingham and Jamal Musiala.

---

## 7. Same No.10, Different Problems

The easiest way to understand these players is to compare the question each one asked the defence.

| Player        | Main Weapon          | Question for the Defender                   |
| ------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Zidane**    | Attracting pressure  | How many players should we send toward him? |
| **Rui Costa** | Passing timing       | When should we follow the runner?           |
| **Totti**     | Creation + finishing | Should we block the pass or the shot?       |
| **Del Piero** | Positional ambiguity | Which defender is responsible for him?      |
| **Aimar**     | Constant movement    | Who keeps following him?                    |
| **Kaká**      | Ball carrying        | Who steps forward to stop him?              |

This is what made the golden age of the No.10 so interesting.

**There was never just one type of No.10.**

---

## 8. Their DNA Still Exists in 2026

Modern players are not copies of the old masters.

It is better to compare specific qualities.

```text
Zidane
Attract + Escape
      ↓
Musiala

Rui Costa / Aimar
Vision + Connection
      ↓
Wirtz / Ødegaard

Totti
Create + Score
      ↓
Palmer / Bruno Fernandes

Kaká
Carry + Attack Space
      ↓
Bellingham / Musiala

Del Piero
No.10 + Forward
      ↓
Olmo / Palmer
```

Bruno Fernandes represents the aggressive final-pass mentality of the traditional No.10 combined with the workload of a modern midfielder.

Wirtz combines creativity with constant repositioning.

Musiala attacks tight spaces through dribbling.

Palmer combines chance creation with finishing.

Bellingham adds powerful forward runs and box arrival.

Olmo connects lines and moves intelligently between midfield and attack.

Szoboszlai adds running power, pressing and direct vertical play.

Fermín López is especially strong at **arriving into dangerous spaces without the ball**.

They all carry part of the No.10 tradition.

But none of them is the same type of player.

---

## Conclusion — Great No.10s Controlled Decisions

The golden age of the No.10 was not simply an era of beautiful passing.

The best No.10s controlled the opponent's choices.

Zidane pulled defenders toward him.

Rui Costa waited for them to move.

Totti made defenders choose between stopping the pass and stopping the shot.

Del Piero attacked the border between defensive responsibilities.

Aimar constantly created new connections.

Kaká ran directly at the defence and forced someone to react.

Different styles.

Same principle.

**A great No.10 does not just control the ball.**

**He controls the opponent's decisions.**
