# France — 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup Opponent Analysis

## Les Bleues

> Data as of 17 October 2026. The 2027 Women's World Cup will be held in Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027. The final tournament draw has not yet taken place.
>
> **Purpose:** a scouting report for coaching staff — how France play, who decides their games, where they are vulnerable, and a concrete game plan.

```text
BEST WORLD CUP      Fourth (2011) — never reached a final
QUALIFICATION       UEFA League A Group 2 winners (9 June 2026, 1-0 vs Ireland)
RECENT RESULTS      Euro 2025 QF (lost on pens to ten-player Germany); Nations League 2025 bronze
RECORD (12 MONTHS)  W 7  D 3  L 2
```

**One-line verdict:** The fastest wide attack in women's football — six players with pace 87+ — built on Lyon, London City, Chelsea and Real Madrid players. They destroy high lines in transition. But France have gone out of **three straight major tournaments in the quarter-finals (two on penalties)**, and their press can be bypassed with long diagonal passes.

```text
Assistant to Hervé Renard with France women
Aug 2024  Appointed head coach after Paris 2024
Jul 2025  Euro QF exit on pens; FFF president Diallo backs him until 2027
```

### Profile
- **Aggression is the half-time message:** trailing the Netherlands 1-2 at Euro 2025, he told his players "to be more aggressive" — France scored four second-half goals.
- **Risk-taker:** experimented with a **back three and a high press**, which "left spaces" (French press); reviewed the system afterwards.
- **Tough preparation:** scheduled Brazil before Euro 2025 to rehearse big games.

## 6. Tactics and Formation

### 6.1 Shapes

```text
IN POSSESSION (4-3-3 → 2-3-5)

   CASCARINO / DIANI        KATOTO / MATEO        BALTIMORE / MALARD
          GEYORO                               TOLETTI
                            DALI
  DE ALMEIDA / KARCHAOUI   MBOCK BATHY   LAKRAR    BACHA          ← FULL-BACKS PUSH HIGH
                        PEYRAUD-MAGNIN / CHAVAS
```

```text
OUT OF POSSESSION — TWO VERSIONS

  (A) PASSIVE: KATOTO PRESSES ALONE             (B) AGGRESSIVE (BONADEI'S FIX)
        KATOTO                                       KATOTO   GEYORO (steps up)
  W   GEYORO   DALI   TOLETTI   W                W    DALI      TOLETTI    W
  FB    CB      CB     FB                         FB    CB      CB     FB
  → opponent CBs free to play long diagonals      → long balls cut off, second balls won
```

### 6.2 Pressing — Strong in Duels, Weak With One Forward

| Situation | Evidence (UEFA technical observers) |
|---|---|
| Pressing **from behind** opponents facing away, midfielders joining from the front | vs England: won 1v1 duels high, set up quick attacks |
| **Interception → vertical pass over the line** | De Almeida intercepted Stanway, played Cascarino in behind → Katoto 36' |
| **Lone-striker press fails** | vs Netherlands (1st half): Katoto "pressed alone and could not pressure Spitse and Janssen," who hit long diagonals into the half-spaces |
| **Fix: a midfielder steps up beside Katoto** | 2nd half vs Netherlands: France won second balls and scored four |

### 6.3 Transitions — The Main Weapon
- "Increased the tempo and played more vertically" (Fuhrmann, UEFA).
- Exploit **the space behind high full-backs** — Cascarino attacked the space behind England's left-back Jess Carter.

### 6.4 1v1 Wingers
- **Cascarino** — player of the match vs England and the Netherlands; beat two defenders for her first goal vs the Netherlands.
- **Diani** (PAC 91), **Baltimore** (DRI 86), **Malard** (PAC 87).

### 6.5 Full-Backs
- **Selma Bacha** (OL Lyonnes, PAS 85) — elite crosser; but scored an own goal vs the Netherlands.
- **De Almeida** — interceptions and progressive carries.
- **Karchaoui** — penalty taker.

### 6.6 Late Goals and Bench
- Mbock 90+6' (Sweden), Mateo 89' (Germany), Malard 71' & 79' (Ireland), Gago 106'.

### 6.7 Game-State Behaviour

| State | What France do |
|---|---|
| 0-0 | High press, quick vertical attacks |
| Trailing | Become **more aggressive** — midfielder joins the first line (Netherlands 2nd half) |
| Leading | Defend with blocks in the box (England 87' goal conceded) |
| Against ten / deep block | **Struggle** — no winner vs ten-player Germany for 107 minutes |

## 10. Signature Tactics — Analysis of Seven Games Against Strong Opponents

### Game ① 5 July 2025, Euro — France 2-1 England (Zurich)

| Minute | Moment |
|---|---|
| 36' | **De Almeida intercepts Stanway** → carries → ball in behind for **Cascarino** → first-time cross → **Katoto** |
| 39' | **Baltimore** right-foot strike after England hesitation |
| 87' | Walsh (England) after England changed shape |

UEFA: "effective pressing and incisive attacking transitions… solid defence and strong 1v1 play."
**Lesson:** France's **press from behind + vertical pass** destroys high lines. Two goals in three minutes.

### Game ② 13 July 2025, Euro — Netherlands 2-5 France (Basel)

| Phase | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1st half | Netherlands led 2-1: **Spitse and Janssen's long diagonals** bypassed Katoto's lone press |
| Half-time | Bonadei: "be more aggressive" |
| 2nd half | **A midfielder joined Katoto** → long balls cut off, second balls won → Toletti, Katoto 61', **Cascarino 2** |

**Lesson:** France's press has a structural flaw (one forward) — **and a fix (midfielder steps up).** Expect France to start with the fix in big games.

### Game ③ 19 July 2025, Euro QF — France 1-1 Germany (lost 5-6 on pens)

- Germany had ten from the 13th minute; Geyoro's penalty was cancelled out by Nüsken; France could not find a winner in 107 minutes.
**Lesson:** France **cannot break down a deep, compact block** — even with an extra player.

### Game ④ 24 & 28 October 2025, NL SF — Germany 1-0 France, France 2-2 Germany

- First leg: France "had no trouble exploiting" Germany's set-up but were denied by Johannes; Bühl won it 79'.
- Second leg: Malard 3', Mateo 89'.
**Lesson:** France create plenty — **finishing in big games** is the issue.

### Game ⑤ Nov–Dec 2025, NL 3rd Place — France vs Sweden (2-1, 2-2 aet)

- Won the first leg with **Mbock 90+6'**; in the second, **Sweden scored at 84' and 90+2'** to force extra time.
**Lesson:** France score late — and **concede late.**

### Game ⑥ 3 March 2026, WCQ — Ireland 1-2 France (Dublin)

- Ireland led from 12'; **Malard 71', 79'** — France's bench pace broke a tired block.
**Lesson:** France get stronger as opponents tire.

### Game ⑦ 14 & 18 April 2026, WCQ — Netherlands 2-1 France, France 1-1 Netherlands

| Game | Goals |
|---|---|
| Breda | van Asten 11', Baltimore 54', Brugts ~68' |
| Auxerre | Katoto 45+1', Kaptein 76' |

**Lesson:** The Netherlands — technical, with good long passers at the back — **out-played France over 180 minutes.** This is the template.

## 12. How to Beat This Team — Game Plan

### Strategy ① — Bypass the Press With Diagonals From the Centre-Backs
- **Evidence:** Netherlands (Euro 2025 1st half; April 2026).
- **Instructions:** our two best passing centre-backs hit diagonals into the half-spaces behind France's full-backs; runners from midfield attack the space.

### Strategy ② — Defend Deep and Narrow — No Space in Behind
- **Evidence:** Germany with ten held France for 107 minutes.
- **Instructions:** low line, compact; take away the ball in behind for Cascarino/Diani; let France cross.

### Strategy ③ — Never Isolate Our Full-Back Against Cascarino or Diani
- Double up every time; winger tracks back.

### Strategy ④ — Counter Before the Attackers Recover
- France's forwards (DEF 39–49) don't track back. First pass forward, quickly.

### Strategy ⑤ — Keep It Close — Take It to Penalties
- **Evidence:** two of France's last three exits on penalties.

### Strategy ⑥ — Stay Focused Late
- France score at 89', 90+6'; their bench (Malard, Mateo) is dangerous after 70'.

### Summary

```text
CB DIAGONALS OVER THE LONE-STRIKER PRESS
                    +
DEEP, NARROW BLOCK — NO SPACE IN BEHIND
                    +
DOUBLE UP ON CASCARINO / DIANI
                    +
FAST COUNTERS BEFORE THEIR ATTACKERS RECOVER
                    +
DRAG IT TO PENALTIES
                    ↓
TURN FRANCE'S SPEED INTO NOTHING — AND THEIR HISTORY INTO PRESSURE
```

## 13. Conclusion

France have the fastest wide attack in the world and a squad from the very top clubs. In group stages they are brilliant.

Their repeatable weaknesses:

1. **Knockout football and penalties** — three straight QF exits
2. **A press that long diagonals can bypass**
3. **No answer to deep, compact blocks**
4. **Late concessions**

> **Take away France's space, and their speed becomes useless. Keep it close, and their history does the rest.**
